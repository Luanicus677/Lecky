Madden, Phantasmata

Richard Robert Madden, Phantasmata; or, Illusions and Fanaticisms of Protean Forms

Author
Richard Robert Madden (1798–1886), Irish physician and historian (details not verified here).
Work
Phantasmata, 2 vols., London: T. C. Newby, 1857. The relevant chapter is headed “Witchcraft monomania in an epidemic form”.
Edition used
London, 1857, vol. I, p. 305 (scan); the printed page number was confirmed on the neighbouring leaf (p. 307 is at the next-but-one image).
Cited in
Rationalism in Europe, ch. I, note 5; Lecky also cites Madden at notes 6, 106 and others (not yet transcribed)
Status
One passage summarised and compared. Short quotations only.

Madden's book is a nineteenth-century medical and historical survey of religious and witch manias. On p. 305 he gives a rapid chronology of papal bulls and trials in Italy. Lecky's note 5 cites “Madden, vol. i. 305”, and it is the source of his claim that “the severity of the inquisitors at last created an absolute rebellion”.

P. 305: Venice 1518, and Como

English: London, 1857, vol. I, p. 305 (scan). The text was read from the scan’s text layer; short quotations only. Highlighted: the words Lecky’s sentence rests on.

In 1518, in the Venetian territories, a great number of persons, accused of sorcery and heresy, were condemned by the Inquisition, and handed over to the secular arm […]. But the people of Venice rose up against the Inquisitors, and were hardly to be appeased by the Council.

[…] The Dominicans were charged with its execution in Lombardy. In the district of Como alone, the number who perished are estimated by Barth. de Spina (in Malleo Maleficorum) at a thousand a year; and in the space of seven years, in Piedmont, Lombardy, and Mirandola, the number who perished was enormous.

The source is in English, so there is no translation. The passage after the first paragraph, omitted here, is a sentence about Pope Adrian VI’s bull of 1523.

Lecky’s sentence (p. 5, note 5) is: “In Italy a thousand persons were executed in a single year in the province of Como; and in other parts of the country, the severity of the inquisitors at last created an absolute rebellion.” Set against Madden:

Lecky, 1865Madden, 1857
Como“a thousand persons were executed in a single year in the province of Como”“the number who perished are estimated by Barth. de Spina … at a thousand a year” in “the district of Como alone”. Lecky’s wording follows Madden’s, and not Spina’s, whose text says more than a thousand were examined and about a hundred burned.
The rebellion“in other parts of the country, the severity of the inquisitors at last created an absolute rebellion”“the people of Venice rose up against the Inquisitors, and were hardly to be appeased by the Council” (1518). This is the only rising Madden mentions on p. 305. “Absolute rebellion” and “the severity of the inquisitors” are Lecky’s words: Madden says only that the people “rose up” and says nothing here of why.
Madden’s source—None given on this page for either statement. The Venice rising has not been verified from another source here.
Reference“Madden, vol. i. 305”Exact: both statements are on p. 305.

So Lecky’s sentence is a compression of Madden’s two sentences, with the “thousand” turned from a figure for those who “perished” into “executed”, and the Venetian rising turned into a general “absolute rebellion”.
