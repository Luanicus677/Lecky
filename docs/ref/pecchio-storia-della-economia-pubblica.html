Pecchio, Storia della economia pubblica in Italia

Amalfi and the maritime code (Introduction, p. 11)

Author
Giuseppe Pecchio (1785–1835), Italian exile in England. The book was first published in 1829 (Lugano), the same title as Lecky’s citation. Lecky cites it in Chapter VI, note 90.
Edition used
A Google scan catalogued 1849: storiadellaecon01peccgoog. Printed p. 11 is image n20 (image = page + 9). Lecky’s “(Lugano, 1849)” and p. 11 match. The translation is this edition’s own draft.
Cited in
Rationalism, ch. VI, note 90.
Status
One page transcribed from the image and compared with Lecky’s sentence. A keyword search of the scan’s OCR for “Amalfi” and “Pandette” found Amalfi on pp. 10–11 (commerce, the Tavola, the compass) and on a later page (Gioia, the compass); the only “Pandette” hit is a note on Roman law and the value of money (Carli), unrelated. Only p. 11 was read on the image

Lecky writes: “The accidental discovery at Amalphi of a manuscript of Roman laws is said to have produced the navigation laws”, and cites Pecchio, p. 11. What Pecchio says on that page is different: that Pisa, Barcelona or (more probably, from the name) Amalfi was the first to introduce the maritime code known as the Tavola Amalfitana, which became the code of seafarers, and that some ascribe the invention of the compass to Amalfi. The page says nothing about a manuscript of Roman laws or about an accidental discovery. The story of the Pandects found at Amalfi is a different, older tradition; I have not found it in a keyword search of the OCR, and so Lecky’s note does not support the sentence it is attached to, at least in this printing.

The Tavola Amalfitana (Introduction, p. 11)

Italian: p. 11, scan. Highlighted: the Amalfi sentences.

Non solo queste repubbliche avevano prosperato prima che la scienza nascesse, ma avevano altresì messi in pratica quasi tutti gli espedienti che poscia la scienza raccolse, spiegò, perfezionò per condurre i popoli alla ricchezza. O Pisa, o Barcellona, o più verosimilmente dal nome, Amalfi introdusse la prima il codice marittimo conosciuto sotto il nome di Tavola Amalfitana, che divenne il codice de’ naviganti. Ad Amalfi pure si attribuisce da alcuni la scoperta della Bussola. La repubblica di Milano fin dal 1260 praticò il censimento delle terre. Venezia fin dal 1171 trovò ne’ prestiti una sorgente nuova e straordinaria di finanze. …

Not only had these republics prospered before the science was born, but they had also put into practice almost all the expedients which the science afterwards collected, explained and perfected in order to lead peoples to wealth. Either Pisa, or Barcelona, or, more probably from the name, Amalfi was the first to introduce the maritime code known under the name of the Tavola Amalfitana, which became the code of seafarers. To Amalfi also some ascribe the discovery of the compass. The republic of Milan practised the census of lands as early as 1260. Venice as early as 1171 found in loans a new and extraordinary source of finance. …

Lecky’s sentence has no “accidental discovery” and no “manuscript of Roman laws” in this passage. It is possible that Lecky draws on another page of Pecchio or on another work and has cited the wrong page; I did not find it in a search of Pecchio’s text for “Pandette”. The footnote is left as printed.
