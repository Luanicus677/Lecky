    Witch confessions taken before presbyteries and special commissioners (vol. 3, p. 598), quoted in ch. I, note 149. Accurate; Lecky’s second citation of p. 598 (note 162, the Earl of Mar) is not on the page.
  • Glanvill, Saducismus Triumphatus1 passage
    “Atheism is begun in Sadducism” (Preface, 1700 ed.), quoted in ch. I, note 122. Accurate.
  • Bossuet, Histoire des variations2 passages
    Zwingli on the children of the pagans (liv. II, p. 73; ch. IV note 17) and book X, § 56: Catholics and Protestants agree on punishing heretics (p. 475), quoted in ch. IV (cont.), note 75. Accurate, with Jurieu’s references abridged.
  • Taylor, Liberty of Prophesying2 passages
    “The water and the Spirit … signify the same thing” (pp. 328–329, ch. IV note 13) and Christ’s refusal of fire (p. 368, ch. IV cont. note 7). Accurate.
  • Laing, Notes of a Traveller1 passage
    The Swiss as “eminently moral in conduct, yet eminently irreligious” (pp. 323–324), quoted in ch. IV, note 35. Accurate with small changes; Lecky gives no title.
  • Pascal, Pensées (original sin)1 passage
    The infant damned for a sin committed six thousand years before, quoted in ch. IV, note 12. Compared with a Wikisource text, not a scan; accurate.
  • Voltaire, Dictionnaire philosophique, “Patrie”1 passage
    “Souhaiter la grandeur de son pays c’est souhaiter du mal à ses voisins”, quoted in ch. VI, note 177. Compared with a Wikisource text; Lecky reverses Voltaire’s order.
  • Voltaire, Lettres philosophiques, XIV1 passage
    Descartes, driven from Utrecht, accused of atheism, “the last resort of slanderers” (Lanson’s edition). Cited in vol. II, ch. IV, note 56.
  • Gioja, Filosofia della statistica1 passage
    Melchiorre Gioja, Milan 1826, tome II, p. 389 (Lecky’s page): Queen Ulrika Eleonora nearly had Linnaeus’s sexual system of plants banned as corrupting the young. Cited in vol. II, ch. IV, note 56.
  • Vindiciae contra tyrannos1 passage
    The Huguenot case for resisting tyrants (1579). Elijah’s people put the priests of Baal to death “by public authority, a council having been held” (pp. 38–39 of Lecky’s 1610 edition). Cited in vol. II, ch. V, notes 112–116.
  • De Thou, Histoire universelle, book 962 passages
    1589: Sixtus V compares the murder of Henri III to the Incarnation and sets the murderer “far above Judith”; Leaguers drown with a boatload of the regicide’s bloodstained earth (French translation, 1734, tome X, pp. 679–680). Cited in vol. II, ch. V, note 70.
  • Carena, De officio Sanctissimae Inquisitionis1 passage
    The Inquisition’s handbook (Lyon 1649) on confessors who solicit their penitents: Title VI, pp. 135–161, Lecky’s exact pages, with the bulls of Paul IV, Pius IV and Gregory XV. Cited in vol. II, ch. V, note 12.
  • Camden, Annals of Elizabeth1 passage
    1571: the Scots’ discourse “That the Scottish People are above their Kings”, which Elizabeth read “not without Indignation” (English version, 1688, pp. 154–155). Cited in vol. II, ch. V, note 84.
  • Elrington, Life of Ussher2 passages
    The Irish bishops’ judgment of 1626: toleration of Catholics “is a grievous sin” (p. 73); and Ussher in the Civil War: “war made by subjects, though really intending such an end, is unjust” (p. 239). Dublin 1847, vol. I, Lecky’s pages. Cited in vol. II, ch. IV, note 47, and ch. V, note 89.
  • Montesquieu, De l’esprit des lois, XXI, 201 passage
    “Nous devons aux spéculations des scholastiques tous les malheurs …”, quoted in ch. VI, note 39. Compared with a Wikisource text; accurate.
  • De Maistre, Soirées de Saint-Pétersbourg3 passages
    Locke in the sixth conversation (pp. 437, 452) and Bacon in the Examen (p. 100), quoted in ch. IV, note 45. Exact; “un charlatan” not found.
  • Mackay, The Progress of the Intellect1 passage
    Robert William Mackay on Greek and Hebrew religion, 1850. The pagan mysteries as “a sacred drama” (vol. II, p. 115), the root of Lecky’s history of the theatre; Lecky’s page numbers do not match this edition.
  • Concina, Adversus usuram and De spectaculis13 passages
    Daniele Concina’s books against usury (Rome 1746) and the theatre (Rome 1752), in Lecky’s editions. Calvin and Le Coreur, the 28 councils and 17 popes Lecky counted, Benedict XIV on carnival, actors barred from communion, the friar who pulled down Córdoba’s theatres, the bull of Cuenca, monks acting comedies.
  • Lamet and Fromageau, Dictionnaire des cas de conscience3 passages
    The Sorbonne’s casebook (Paris 1733), read in its Latin translation (Venice 1753). Three per cent is usury (1677); actors are in mortal sin (1694); the three baptisms. Lecky’s French edition is not online.
  • Naudé, Apologie pour les grands hommes soupçonnez de magie4 passages
    Gabriel Naudé’s defence of great men accused of magic, Paris 1625, Lecky’s edition. Toledo’s magic as mathematics, sorcery after heresy, Bodin’s human failing, Pietro d’Abano’s spirits in a crystal and his straw effigy.
  • Binsfeld, De confessionibus maleficorum3 passages
    Peter Binsfeld’s treatise on witches’ confessions, Trier 1591, Lecky’s edition. Sadness as the devil’s opening with women, the cold-water test (a judge who uses it “sins mortally”), and St Cyprian’s devils defeated by Justina.
  • Sarpi, Historia dell’origine … dell’Inquisizione1 passage
    Paolo Sarpi on the Inquisition in Venice, 1675 printing (Lecky used 1639). The bishops who once urged princes to care for religion and now tell them it is none of their business; where Lecky’s other references fall.
  • Storch, Cours d’économie politique3 passages
    Heinrich Storch’s lectures for the Russian grand dukes, St Petersburg 1815. The Old Believers against interest; Louis XV’s and Catherine II’s usury laws that raised the rate they meant to lower; Christianity and the end of serfdom.
  • Milman, The History of Christianity2 passages
    H. H. Milman’s history to the end of paganism, London 1840, Lecky’s edition. The Donatists and Circumcellions; Eusebius’s claims that Constantine banned sacrifice, weighed against Constantine’s own letters of toleration.
  • Renan, Averroès et l’averroïsme1 passage
    Ernest Renan’s thesis on Averroes, Paris 1852, Lecky’s edition. Pietro d’Abano, his “horoscope of religions”, and the Inquisition’s revenge on his bones, compared with Naudé’s straw dummy.
  • Strutt, Manners and Customs of the Inhabitants of England2 passages
    Joseph Strutt’s illustrated antiquities, vol. III, 1776. The rack under Mary, after Foxe; the Coventry Corpus Christi play, with God on the upper stage and Lucifer claiming his seat.
  • Eusebius, Life of Constantine3 passages
    The emperor’s biographer, in the English translation of 1845. Governors forbidden to sacrifice, divination and sacrifice banned (with the translator’s “that is, private sacrifices”), temples stripped and statues paraded in Constantinople.
  • Grotius, De jure belli ac pacis2 passages
    Book I, ch. IV, § 7, in Whewell’s 1853 edition: the early Christians who could have fought but did not, after Tertullian; the Theban legion, and a footnote calling it “mere fable”.
  • M’Culloch, Principles of Political Economy and the Wealth of Nations4 passages
    J. R. M’Culloch’s Principles (1849) and his edition of Adam Smith (1838). The Dublin Foundling Hospital’s 12,561 deaths, Calvin on interest (via Dugald Stewart), the Scottish colliers bound to their pits until 1775–1799.
  • Vitet, Études sur l’histoire de l’art3 passages
    Ludovic Vitet’s essays, vol. I (1864). The French consuls at Athens; why the saints in Rome’s mosaics grew ugly, where Vitet blames the barbarians and Lecky, citing him, blames asceticism.
  • Riccoboni, Histoire du théâtre italien5 passages
    Luigi Riccoboni (“Lelio”), Paris 1728, Lecky’s tome I. Harlequin’s costume from the Roman mimes, the Calandra, an emperor ennobling a Harlequin, and the Italian play Riccoboni says Molière copied for Tartuffe.
  • Troplong, Du prêt (the Mémoire on interest)2 passages
    A French judge’s history of usury, read at the Institute in 1844 and printed as the preface to Du prêt (1845). Lecky’s source for Nicaea’s canon against clerical usury, Constantine’s 12 per cent, and St Louis telling the Jews to give up usury or leave.
  • Maury, Croyances et légendes de l’antiquité2 passages
    Alfred Maury’s essays of 1863. Eusebius doctoring Josephus, the Fathers’ “little frauds” as fair play against heretics (p. 268); Simon Magus’s Helena as the incarnate Thought of God, and Prounikos as “lascivious” (pp. 338–340).
  • Champagny, La Charité chrétienne2 passages
    Franz de Champagny, Paris 1854. The section “Défense du travail” behind Lecky’s Fathers who “employed all their eloquence in favour of labour”, with Augustine scolding idle monks: “senators labouring in the cells, artisans at rest!”
  • Wier, De praestigiis daemonum1 passage
    Johann Wier, Basel 1568: the old canon (Trullo, 692) against bear-leaders, fortune-tellers and cloud-chasers that Lecky quotes, and the dedication to Duke William of Cleves. Lecky’s page fits none of the three editions found.
