Sources

The books, pamphlets, and documents Lecky cites, each on its own page with the passage he used, a translation where it isn’t in English, and a link to a scan of the edition the text comes from. Every source page lists the places in Lecky that cite it, so you can move in either direction.

  • Grillandus, De Sortilegiis2 passages
    Paolo Grillandi’s handbook on sorcery, 1530s. Quaestio III, §12, compared against the 1545 and 1592 editions, and the passage on sorcerers and physicians from the Lyon 1620 Malleus collection, which also shows which edition of the Malleus Lecky used. Cited in Rationalism, ch. I, notes 1 and 71.
  • Garinet, Histoire de la Magie en France3 passages
    Jules Garinet’s history of sorcery in France, 1818. The preface’s survey of ancient laws against sorcerers (pp. xiii–xiv), including the sorceress “Lemia”, and the famine of 795 and the eclipse under Louis the Debonair (pp. 38, 42), in French and English, each compared with Lecky. Cited in Rationalism, ch. I, notes 3, 4, 9, 14, 39, 40, and others.
  • Bodin, Démonomanie des Sorciers6 passages
    Jean Bodin’s treatise on sorcerers, 1580. Baal-zebub and the flies (p. 15), God “au grand nez” (p. 40), the pardon of Trois-Échelles (pp. 151–152), stoning against burning and the plague stories (pp. 217–218, 228), and the appeal to the laws of all peoples against Wier (near p. 252), in French and English, each compared with Lecky. Cited in Rationalism, ch. I, notes 68, 74, 88, 89 and 137.
  • Codex Theodosianus, IX.166 passages
    Constantine’s laws against haruspices (319, laws 1–2) and the exception for remedies and hail (321, law 3), Constantius II’s laws of 357 and 358 against divination and magic (laws 4 and 6), and Valentinian’s ban on night sacrifices (364, law 7), in Latin and English from the 1738 Leipzig edition, compared with Lecky. Cited in Rationalism, ch. I, notes 26, 27, 28 and 32.
  • Sprenger, Malleus Maleficarum9 passages
    The inquisitors’ handbook, Lyon 1620 edition. “Because of the angels” and incubi (Pars I), incubi and women’s hair, exorcism before baptism (Pars II), the etymologies of femina and diabolus (Pars I), the story of St. Silvanus (Pars II), the angels and Serenus and Equitius (Nider’s text in the same volume), and the Cologne preface on sceptical preachers, in Latin and English, each compared with Lecky. Cited in Rationalism, ch. I, notes 21, 22, 68, 78, 79 and 83.
  • Gerson, De probatione spirituum2 passages
    Jean Gerson’s tracts on testing visions and on errors about magic, from the Lyon 1620 Malleus collection. The holy man who shut his eyes against a devil disguised as Christ (p. 77), and those who ridicule theologians who speak of demons (p. 253, part 2), in Latin and English. Cited in Rationalism, ch. I, notes 37 and 82.
  • Spina, Quaestio de Strigibus and Apologia4 passages
    Bartolomeo Spina on the numbers burned at Como (p. 193), the inquisitors’ opponents and Peter Martyr (p. 278), and the doubters of the witch trials (pp. 191, 299–300), Lyon 1620 reprint, in Latin and English, compared with Lecky. Cited in Rationalism, ch. I, notes 5, 67 and 84.
  • Madden, Phantasmata1 passage
    R. R. Madden’s 1857 survey of religious and witch manias, vol. I p. 305: the Venetian rising of 1518 and the Como figures that Lecky’s note 5 draws on. Cited in Rationalism, ch. I, note 5.
  • Thiers, Traité des Superstitions1 passage
    Jean-Baptiste Thiers on papal bulls against sorcerers in Germany and Lombardy (pp. 139–140 of the 1697 printing; Lecky’s 1741 edition was not found). Cited in Rationalism, ch. I, note 5.
  • Middleton, Free Inquiry into the Miraculous Powers4 passages
    Conyers Middleton’s 1749 attack on the Patristic miracles. The exorcists (pp. 85–87), the “continual succession” of miracles in the Church historians (Preface, p. xv), the Fathers’ “surprizing confidence” (Introductory Discourse, p. lxxxiv), and the “about ninety Heresies” footnote (p. lxxxvi), in English from the 1749 Dublin printing, compared with Lecky. Cited in Rationalism, ch. II, notes 3, 10 and 11, and ch. IV, note 25.
  • Simancas, De Catholicis Institutionibus4 passages
    Diego de Simancas’s manual for judges of heresy, Rome 1575 edition. Faith with heretics (pp. 365–366), the inquisitors’ use of torture (p. 494), his attack on Vives (p. 495), and the Spanish rule of private torture (p. 506), in Latin and English, compared with Lecky. Lecky’s page numbers for torture do not match this edition. Cited in Rationalism, ch. III, notes 155 and 158 and ch. IV, note 37.
  • Chrysostom, On the Priesthood1 passage
    John Chrysostom’s defence of well-meant deceit (Book I, ch. VIII), in Cowper’s 1866 English translation checked against the scan, compared with Lecky’s “panegyric on the habit of telling lies”. Cited in Rationalism, ch. IV, note 37.
  • Augustine, Contra mendacium3 passages
    Augustine’s refusal to catch heretics by lying, and his charge that the Priscillianists teach lying as a doctrine (PL 40, cols. 519–520), in Latin and English, compared with Lecky and set beside Chrysostom. Cited in Rationalism, ch. IV, note 37.
  • Tertullian, De spectaculis1 passage
    Tertullian’s picture of the Christians watching the damned in the fire (ch. 30), in Latin and English from Migne, with Lecky’s Latin quotation collated word by word. Cited in Rationalism, ch. III, note 152.
  • Aquinas, Summa Supplement q. 941 passage
    Whether the blessed will see the punishments of the damned (art. 1), in Latin and English from an 18th-century edition. Lecky’s quotation is a paraphrase, not a sentence of the text. Cited in Rationalism, ch. III, note 150.
  • Augustine, De civitate Dei2 passages
    The miracles of St. Stephen at Hippo and Calama, with the five cases of restoration to life (XXII.8), and the judge who tortures the innocent (XIX.6), in Latin and English from Migne, compared with Lecky. Cited in Rationalism, ch. II, note 15, and ch. III, note 161.
  • Agobard, Liber de imaginibus6 passages
    The ninth-century archbishop of Lyons’s treatise against image-worship: images called “holy”, the excuse of honouring the saint, the crosses of Alexandria, Hezekiah and the bronze serpent, the Devil’s trick, in Latin and English from Baluze’s 1666 edition, compared with Lecky’s five quotations. Cited in Rationalism, ch. III, notes 42–46.
  • Tertullian, De baptismo2 passages
    “We little fishes … are born in water” (ch. I) and the making of man completed by water (ch. II), in Latin and English from Migne, compared with Lecky. Cited in Rationalism, ch. III, notes 3 and 15.
  • Tertullian, Apologeticum1 passage
    Epicurus on pain, and the Christians’ fear of an everlasting punishment (ch. 45), in Latin and English from Migne, compared with Lecky. Cited in Rationalism, ch. III, note 136.
  • Tertullian, De anima1 passage
    The corporeality of the soul in the story of the rich man and Lazarus (ch. 7), in Latin and English from Migne, compared with Lecky. Cited in Rationalism, ch. III, note 166.
  • Minucius Felix, Octavius1 passage
    Jupiter swearing by the Styx and the fire that feeds on the bodies it burns (ch. 35), in Latin and English, compared with Lecky’s “somewhat ferocious passage”. Cited in Rationalism, ch. III, note 139.
  • Luther, De servo arbitrio2 passages
    The will as a beast ridden by God or Satan, and “the highest degree of faith”, in Latin and English from an annotated 1664 edition, compared with Lecky’s quotations. Cited in Rationalism, ch. IV, notes 30 and 31.
  • Hecker, The Epidemics of the Middle Ages3 points
    The Black Death’s toll (a quarter of Europe, 25 millions), the dancers who felt immersed in a stream of blood, and the pointed shoes, from Babington’s English translation, compared with Lecky. Cited in Rationalism, ch. I, notes 57, 58 and 60.
  • Dalyell, Darker Superstitions of Scotland2 passages
    The box in the church, the Aberdeen inquisition of 1603 (pp. 623–624), and “waking” up to nine nights (pp. 644–645), compared with Lecky’s account of the Scottish witch-hunt. Cited in Rationalism, ch. I, notes 146–148, 151, 153.
  • Scot, Discovery of Witchcraft1 passage
    Book II, chapters 1–2 (pp. 11–12 of the 1665 printing): the inquisitors’ “order of examination”, with the Scottish box in the church, compared with Lecky’s “frightful catalogue of the tortures”. Cited in Rationalism, ch. I, note 66.
  • Burt, Letters from the North of Scotland1 passage
    The burning of a Sutherland woman at Dornoch in June 1727 (Letter XII), from the 1754 edition, compared with Lecky’s note on the last Scottish witch-execution. Cited in Rationalism, ch. I, note 166.
  • Hutchinson, Historical Essay concerning Witchcraft1 page
    The 1640s entries of Hutchinson’s “Table of Facts” (1718, p. 37), compared with Lecky’s claims about English executions under the Commonwealth. The total of 140 is not checked. Cited in Rationalism, ch. I, notes 101 and 111.
  • Mariana, De rege et regis institutione6 passages
    Mariana’s case for tyrannicide (Book I, ch. 6), in the 1605 Mainz printing, compared with Lecky’s Latin in Rationalism, ch. V, notes 52–60. One quotation not found.
  • Suárez, Defensio fidei catholicae, Book VI3 passages
    Suárez on deposing tyrants and heretic kings (lib. vi, cap. 4), compared with Lecky’s Latin in Rationalism, ch. V, notes 34 and 41–45. The quotations are accurate.
  • Buchanan, De jure regni apud Scotos1 passage
    “The king a speaking law, the law a silent king”, from the 1581 printing, compared with Lecky’s Latin (Rationalism, ch. V, note 83).
  • Bossuet, Defensio declarationis cleri gallicani1 passage
    Bossuet on the royal name Abimelech (1745 ed., p. 128), cited in Rationalism, ch. V, note 96. Lecky’s book and chapter reference does not match; the ‘wolves and sheep’ passage behind note 77 is not found.
  • Ayala, De jure et officiis bellicis1 passage
    Ayala on killing a usurping tyrant (1582), compared with Lecky’s Latin in Rationalism, ch. V, note 68. Verbatim; Ayala’s restriction to usurpers confirms Lecky’s gloss.
  • Molina, De justitia et jure1 passage
    Molina on the two kinds of tyrant (vol. 4, tract. 3, disp. 6), compared with Lecky’s Latin in Rationalism, ch. V, note 67. Lecky’s version is a compressed paraphrase that drops Molina’s qualifications.
  • Sá, Aphorismi confessariorum1 passage
    The entry “Tyrannus” (1599), quoted verbatim by Lecky in Rationalism, ch. V, note 66.
  • Toletus, Instructio sacerdotum1 passage
    Toletus on the two kinds of tyrant (Book V, 1625 printing), compared with Lecky’s Latin in ch. V, note 65. Small differences; Lecky’s gloss goes beyond the passage.
  • Lebrun, Discours sur la comédie1 passage
    Molière’s death and Rosimond’s burial (1731 ed., p. 259), compared with Lecky’s French in Rationalism, ch. VI, note 143.
  • Montaigne, Essais II.121 passage
    “Voyez l’horrible impudence…” (Courbet–Royer text), compared with Lecky’s French in ch. V, note 117. Lecky reads “imprudence”.
  • Rousseau, Du contrat social IV.81 passage
    The inseparability of civil and theological intolerance, compared with Lecky’s French in Rationalism, ch. IV (continued), note 81.
  • Eymericus, Directorium inquisitorum3 passages
    The Barcelona priest in the flames, the rule on continuing torture, and the sentence on the relapsed (ch. IV (continued), notes 40–42). Lecky cuts the end of the anecdote.
