Sources

The books, pamphlets, and documents Lecky cites, each on its own page with the passage he used, a translation where it isn’t in English, and a link to a scan of the edition the text comes from. Every source page lists the places in Lecky that cite it, so you can move in either direction.

  • Grillandus, De Sortilegiis2 passages
    Paolo Grillandi’s handbook on sorcery, 1530s. Quaestio III, §12, compared against the 1545 and 1592 editions, and the passage on sorcerers and physicians from the Lyon 1620 Malleus collection, which also shows which edition of the Malleus Lecky used. Cited in Rationalism, ch. I, notes 1 and 71.
  • Garinet, Histoire de la Magie en France3 passages
    Jules Garinet’s history of sorcery in France, 1818. The preface’s survey of ancient laws against sorcerers (pp. xiii–xiv), including the sorceress “Lemia”, and the famine of 795 and the eclipse under Louis the Debonair (pp. 38, 42), in French and English, each compared with Lecky. Cited in Rationalism, ch. I, notes 3, 4, 9, 14, 39, 40, and others.
  • Bodin, Démonomanie des Sorciers3 passages
    Jean Bodin’s treatise on sorcerers, 1580. Baal-zebub and the flies (p. 15), the pardon of Trois-Échelles (pp. 151–152), and the appeal to the laws of all peoples against Wier (near p. 252), in French and English, each compared with Lecky. Cited in Rationalism, ch. I, notes 68, 74, 88, and 89.
  • Codex Theodosianus, IX.162 passages
    Constantius II’s laws of 357 and 358 against divination and magic (laws 4 and 6), in Latin and English from the 1738 Leipzig edition, compared with Lecky’s quotation. Cited in Rationalism, ch. I, note 28.
