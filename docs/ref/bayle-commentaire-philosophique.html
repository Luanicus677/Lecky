Bayle, Commentaire philosophique

The title page (1686) and Part II, chapter IV (p. 295)

Author
Pierre Bayle (1647–1706). Lecky calls the book “Contrains-les d’entrer” and describes it as “that treatise on the text ‘Compel them to enter in’” (ch. IV Part II, note 76; also note 7).
Edition used
The 1686 first edition: vol. 1 commentairephilo01bayl (title page = image n2) and vol. 2 commentairephilo02bayl (p. 295 = image n110). The page number is that of this edition, which Lecky’s “pt. ii. ch. iv.” does not use. The translation is this edition’s own draft.
Cited in
Rationalism, ch. IV (continued), note 7 and note 76.
Status
The title page and the first page of Part II chapter IV read on images. I did not read the rest of the chapter, so I cannot say what it says about the effect of the Mosaic law on Christian persecution beyond what its heading announces.

Lecky’s note 76 gives the false imprint accurately in substance: “Traduit de l’Anglois du Sieur Jean Fox de Bruggs, par M. J. F.”, “À Cantorbery, chez Thomas Litwel”. On the title page of the first edition it reads “Traduit de l’Anglois du Sieur Jean Fox de Bruggs par M. J. F.” and “A Cantorbery. Chez Thomas Litwel. 1686”, with the full title “Commentaire philosophique sur ces paroles de Jésus-Christ Contrain-les d’entrer”. Lecky’s “k Cantorberry” has a misprinted “à” and an extra “r” in “Cantorbery”.

Note 7 refers to “pt. ii. ch. iv.”; in this edition, Part II chapter IV (p. 295) is headed “Quatrième objection; on ne peut condamner le sens littéral de ces paroles Contrain-les d’entrer, sans condamner en même temps les loix que Dieu a établies parmi les Juifs, et la conduite que les Prophètes ont quelquefois tenue. Disparité et raisons particulières pour l’ancienne loi qui n’ont point lieu sous l’Évangile”, which is Bayle’s answer to the argument from the law of Moses.

The false title page (1686)

French: vol. 1, title page, scan.

Commentaire philosophique sur ces paroles de Jésus-Chrit Contrain-les d’entrer ; où l’on prouve par plusieurs raisons démonstratives qu’il n’y a rien de plus abominable que de faire des conversions par la contrainte, et l’on réfute tous les sophismes des convertisseurs à contrainte, et l’Apologie que S. Augustin a faite des persécutions. Traduit de l’Anglois du Sieur Jean Fox de Bruggs par M. J. F. À Cantorbery, chez Thomas Litwel. 1686.

Philosophical Commentary on these words of Jesus Christ, Compel them to come in; in which it is proved by several demonstrative reasons that nothing is more abominable than to make conversions by constraint, and all the sophisms of the forcing converters are refuted, together with St Augustine’s apology for persecutions. Translated from the English of Mr John Fox of Bruggs by M. J. F. At Canterbury, printed for Thomas Litwel. 1686.

The title page spells “Jésus-Chrit” (sic) and “Contrain-les d’entrer”. Lecky’s “par M. J. F.” and “Jean Fox de Bruggs” agree with the print; “Fox de Bruggs” is Bayle’s joke on “Fox of Bruges”. The imprint is a fiction; Canterbury is not where the book was printed (I give this from memory, not from the scan).

Part II, chapter IV: the objection from the Mosaic law (p. 295)

French: vol. 2, p. 295, scan. Heading and opening only.

Chapitre IV. Quatriéme objection ; on ne peut condanner le sens literal de ces paroles Contrain-les d’entrer, sans condanner en même-tems les loix que Dieu a établies parmi les Juifs, et la conduite que les Prophetes ont quelquefois tenüe. Disparité et raisons particulieres pour l’ancienne loi qui n’ont point lieu sous l’Evangile.

Avant que de proposer cette objection je me crois obligé de dire deux mots sur un scrupule qui se pourroit élever dans l’ame de quelques personnes. Il semble dira-t-on, que je veuille soutenir qu’il n’y a que 2. chemins à prendre envers les Hérétiques, celui de les faire mourir, ou celui de les abandonner à leurs erreurs …

Chapter IV. Fourth objection: one cannot condemn the literal sense of these words “Compel them to come in” without condemning at the same time the laws that God established among the Jews, and the conduct the Prophets have sometimes held. Difference, and particular reasons for the old law, which have no place under the Gospel.

Before putting this objection I think myself bound to say two words on a scruple that might arise in the minds of some persons. It will seem, they will say, that I wish to maintain that there are only two paths to take towards heretics, that of putting them to death, or that of abandoning them to their errors …

This is the chapter Lecky cites in note 7 for “the influence of this command [the Levitical penalty for idolatry] on Christian persecution”. The heading shows that Bayle treats the Mosaic laws as an objection to be answered, not as the cause of persecution; whether the chapter also says that Christians were led by them to persecute I have not read.
