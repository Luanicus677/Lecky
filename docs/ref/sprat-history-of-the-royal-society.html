Sprat, History of the Royal Society of London

Miracles and the dark ages (p. 350)

Author
Thomas Sprat (1635–1713), later bishop of Rochester. Lecky calls him “Bishop Spratt” and cites “Hist. of Royal Society, p. 350” in Chapter II, note 6.
Edition used
The 1667 first edition: Internet Archive bub_gb_g30OAAAAQAAJ, p. 350 = image n373 (image = page + 23). Lecky’s page number matches. The later printings (1702, 1722) are also online; the 1702 text has “left himself without witness” and modernised spelling.
Cited in
Rationalism, ch. II, note 6.
Status
One page read on the image; the quotation collated. The context (the argument for natural philosophy and the “proverbial speech” that the most learned ages are most atheistical) was read on the same page.

Lecky’s quotation is accurate to the word except for one inserted “a” (“without a witness”, where Sprat has “without witness”) and modern spelling and punctuation. Lecky calls the observation “extremely ingenious”; in Sprat’s page it comes in the course of a defence of natural philosophy against devout men who would reject it: he adds that it is “almost a proverbial speech” that the most learned ages are the most atheistical and the ignorant the most devout, and says the true piety is “little beholden” to whoever devised that distinction.

“The dark and ignorant Ages, wherein to work Miracles”

English: p. 350, scan. The long s is printed as s. Highlighted: Lecky’s quotation.

… if any devout man shall reject all Natural Philosophy, he may blot Genesis, and Job, and the Psalms, and some other Books, out of the Canon of the Bible. God never yet left himself without witness in the World: And it is observable, that he has commonly chosen the dark and ignorant Ages, wherein to work Miracles; but seldom or never the times when Natural Knowledge prevail’d: For he knew there was not so much need to make use of extraordinary signs, when men were diligent in the works of his hands, and attentive on the impressions of his footsteps in his Creatures.

It is almost a proverbial speech, That the most Learned Ages are still the most Atheistical, and the ignorant most devout. …
