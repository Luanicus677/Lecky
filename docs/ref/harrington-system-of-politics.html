Harrington, A System of Politics

A seventeenth-century republican predicts the fall of the French Church and Crown

Author
James Harrington (1611–1677), English republican theorist, author of The Commonwealth of Oceana (1656). A System of Politics, a series of numbered aphorisms, was printed after his death.
Edition used

Cited in
Rationalism, ch. IV (continued), note 88 (“System of Politics, ch. vi”).

Harrington argues that liberty of conscience and civil liberty stand or fall together, so that absolute monarchy cannot live with religious freedom. Applying this to France, where the Protestants then enjoyed a limited toleration, he predicts that the Catholic hierarchy will destroy that liberty unless the liberty destroys the hierarchy, and the monarchy with it. Both halves came true: in 1685, eight years after Harrington’s death, Louis XIV revoked the Edict of Nantes; a century later the Revolution pulled down Church and Crown together. Lecky quotes the aphorism almost exactly, but prints “the monarchy and hierarchy will be beforehand with it”, where Harrington wrote “monarchy or hierarchy”.

“It pulls down that monarchy also” (ch. VI §§ 9–10)

English: Works, 1771, pp. 474–475, scan; § 9 from the OCR of the previous page, § 10 read on the image. Long s modernised; Harrington’s own spelling (“coms”, “wherfore”) kept. Highlighted: Lecky’s quotation.

9. Democracy being nothing but intire liberty, and liberty of conscience without civil liberty, or civil liberty without liberty of conscience being but liberty by halves, must admit of liberty of conscience both as to the perfection of its present being, and as to its future security … because this excludes absolute monarchy, which cannot stand with liberty of conscience in the whole, and regulated monarchy, which cannot stand safely with it in any part.

10. If it be said that in France there is liberty of conscience in part, it is also plain that while the hierarchy is standing this liberty is falling, and that if ever it coms to pull down the hierarchy, it pulls down that monarchy also: wherfore the monarchy or hierarchy will be beforehand with it, if they see their true interest.
