Spina, Quaestio de Strigibus and Apologia

Bartolomeo Spina, Quaestio de strigibus, and his Apologia against Ponzinibio

Author
Bartolomeo Spina (Spina is Lecky’s “Spina”), a Dominican theologian and Master of the Sacred Palace (Wikipedia; details not verified here). The running heads give “Fr. Barthol. de Spina in Ponz. de Lam.” for the Apologia.
Work
The Quaestio de strigibus (1523) and the Apologia replying to Giovanni Francesco Ponzinibio’s De lamiis, both printed in the Malleus maleficarum collection.
Edition used
Lyon: Claude Landry, 1620, vol. II, part 1: p. 191 (scan) and pp. 299–300 (scan). See the edition note.
Cited in
Rationalism in Europe, ch. I, note 84
Status
Two passages transcribed and translated (p. 191, and pp. 299–300). Translation is this edition’s own draft.

Lecky says that about thirty years after the Cologne doctors of 1487, Spina recorded “a spirit of most acrimonious opposition” to the trials, including “a positive rebellion” in north Italy, and he quotes a lament in English. Note 84 cites “Vol. ii. pp. 191, 299, 300”.

The lament is on p. 299 and the dangers to the doubters on p. 300; p. 191 supports a different point. The statement about a “rebellion” in north Italy was not found on any of the three pages.

Apologia, pp. 299–300: The Doubters, and the Danger to Them

Latin: Lyon, 1620, vol. II, part 1, p. 299 (header “Apologia secunda”) and p. 300 (header “F. Barthol. Spin. in Ponz. de Lam.”) (scan, p. 299). Spelling regularised: u/v and æ normalised and abbreviations expanded. The words are in Spina’s Apologia, not the Quaestio itself. The margin note on p. 300 reads “Persecutio Inquisitorum” (“Persecution of the Inquisitors”). Highlighted: the sentences Lecky translates.

P. 299. […] Et contra imprudentissimi, indevotissimi vel etiam infideles sunt, quia ea quae credenda sunt non credunt. Et quod magis dolendum est, persequentibus inimicos Christi, viribus corporeis vel etiam literalibus se opponunt, exponentes se (licet contra intentionem suam) maximo periculo, quod censeantur manifesti fautores haereticorum

P. 300. et consequenter quod ab Inquisitoribus merito molestentur. Et quod non moveantur nonnulli ex devotione quam fingunt, hoc est evidentissimum indicium, quod pecuniis acceptis vel promissis, vel amore carnali, aut aemulatione et inani gloria plurimi persecutionem Inquisitoribus excitant. Sed patres Inquisitores, si solum Deum timuerint, fideique zelo acti officium, quod sibi ex praecepto sanctae matris Ecclesiae sub censuris diligenter perficiendum incumbit, invicte prosequentur, etiam hos persecutores, qui potissima causa sunt quod pestis haec invalescat, et innumera multitudo animarum pereat a Christi fidelium consortio, quasi lupos arcebunt ab ovium grege.

P. 299. […] And, on the other side, they are most imprudent, most undevout, and even unfaithful, because they do not believe what is to be believed. And what is more to be lamented, they set themselves, with bodily force or even with writings, against those who are pursuing the enemies of Christ, exposing themselves (though against their own intention) to the greatest danger of being held to be open supporters of heretics

P. 300. and, as a consequence, of being deservedly troubled by the Inquisitors. And that some are moved not by the devotion they pretend, this is the plainest proof: that many stir up persecution against the Inquisitors for money taken or promised, or out of carnal love, or rivalry and empty vainglory. But the Fathers Inquisitors, if they fear God alone and, moved by zeal for the faith, unconquerably pursue the duty which lies on them by command of Holy Mother Church to be diligently carried out under censures, will keep even these persecutors, who are the chief cause why this plague gains strength and a countless multitude of souls perishes from the fellowship of Christ’s faithful, away from the flock of sheep like wolves.

Reading notes

“Literalibus” (literally “by letters”) is translated “with writings”; Spina may mean legal or written opposition. The first sentence continues a longer one begun earlier on p. 299 about devout and truly faithful people, and the “contra” (“on the other side”) is read as the turn to the sceptics. The lines before “et contra” were read only in outline.

Lecky’s account (note 84, p. 92): “About thirty years later, Spina mentions that, in some places, the innumerable executions had aroused a spirit of most acrimonious opposition. Indeed, in the north of Italy, a positive rebellion had broken out, accompanied by a tone of incredulity which that theologian piteously laments. ‘Most imprudent, most undevout, and most unfaithful men will not believe the things they ought to believe; and what is still more lamentable, they exert all their influence to obstruct those who are destroying the enemies of Christ.’ Such a conduct, Spina justly observes, was full of danger for those who were guilty of it, as they might themselves be justly punished for conniving at the crime; and it was a distinct reflection upon the Church which was represented by the inquisitors; and upon the Pope, by whom the inquisitors were commissioned.” Set against the print:

The lament“Most imprudent, most undevout, and most unfaithful men will not believe the things they ought to believe”“imprudentissimi, indevotissimi vel etiam infideles sunt, quia ea quae credenda sunt non credunt”. Close. Lecky drops “vel etiam” (“or even”) and renders infideles as “unfaithful”.
The obstruction“they exert all their influence to obstruct those who are destroying the enemies of Christ”“persequentibus inimicos Christi, viribus corporeis vel etiam literalibus se opponunt”: they oppose, “with bodily force or even with writings”, those pursuing Christ’s enemies. Lecky’s “exert all their influence” is looser than the Latin.
The danger“full of danger … might themselves be justly punished for conniving at the crime”“manifesti fautores haereticorum … ab Inquisitoribus merito molestentur” (open supporters of heretics, deservedly troubled by the Inquisitors). Close in sense; “conniving at the crime” is Lecky’s paraphrase.
“A positive rebellion in the north of Italy”“in the north of Italy, a positive rebellion had broken out”Not found on pp. 299–300 or p. 191. Spina names no region on these pages, and “rebellion” is Lecky’s word. It may come from another page of the Apologia or the Quaestio (which names Lombard towns in other places), which has not been read.

Quaestio, p. 191: Criticising the Inquisitors Is Criticising the Pope

Latin: Lyon, 1620, vol. II, part 1, p. 191 (header “Quaest. de Strigibus”) (scan), conventions as above. Highlighted: the sentences that support Lecky’s remark about the Church and the Pope.

[…] Nam id esset de summa negligentia Romanam Ecclesiam sugillare, imo de summa crudelitate et iniustitia reprehensionem reddere et calumniari. Quandoquidem inquisitores non nisi ut delegati summi Pontificis haec efficiant, a quo principaliter pendet quicquid per eos juste, vel inique, ipso praesertim sciente, fit. Propter quod si in hoc iudicio inveniretur iniquitas, ipsi principaliter imputandum esset, si taceret aut non prohiberet. Quod quia non nisi temerarium est, ideo nullus potest nisi ignarus, vel valde praesumptuosus, ista negare sic contingere, prout ab Inquisitoribus comprobantur.

[…] For that would be a mark of the greatest negligence, to taunt the Roman Church; indeed, of the greatest cruelty and injustice, to bring a reproach against it and to slander it, since the inquisitors act in this only as delegates of the supreme Pontiff, on whom principally depends whatever is done by them, justly or unjustly, he especially knowing of it. Wherefore if iniquity were found in this judgement, it would be chiefly to be charged to him, if he were silent or did not forbid it. And because this is nothing but rash, no one can deny that these things happen as the Inquisitors attest, unless he is ignorant or very presumptuous.

This is the passage behind Lecky’s sentence that opposition “was a distinct reflection upon the Church which was represented by the inquisitors; and upon the Pope, by whom the inquisitors were commissioned.” The sense is right, though in the Latin it is an argument that criticism of the trials is an attack on the Church and Pope, not a description of a rebellion. The words before it on p. 191 (the sentence about the doctors being unanimous that the facts are real and bodily) are summarised, not quoted, by Lecky. The page numbers in note 84 (191, 299, 300) are therefore real and relevant, but taken from two different works of Spina’s, about a hundred pages apart.
