Clement V at the Council of Vienne, Ex gravi

Clementinae V.5 (De usuris), § 2

Author
Pope Clement V, “in concilio Viennensi” (the Council of Vienne, 1311–12). The Clementines were published by John XXII in 1317. Lecky quotes the decree in Chapter VI, note 46 (“The Council of Vienna, presided over by Clement V., pronounced it to be heretical to justify usury”).
Edition used
Emil Friedberg’s Corpus iuris canonici, vol. 2 (Leipzig, 1881), Clementinarum lib. V tit. V, cols. 1183–1184: Internet Archive BD1141952, columns 1183–1184 on image n627. The title page of this scan was not read, so the year of the printing is from memory. The translation is this edition’s own draft.
Cited in
Rationalism, ch. VI, note 46.
Status
Latin transcribed from the page image, translated and collated. Lecky’s own citation (Conférences sur l’Usure, tom. i. p. 93) was not seen; the Conférences are not on the site.

Lecky’s Latin is the second paragraph of the single chapter of the Clementine title De usuris. He quotes it nearly word for word and stops before the clause that tells bishops and inquisitors to proceed against anyone defamed or suspected of the error. His “istum” is the edition’s “illum”; the edition’s “cum velut haereticum” (read on the image) looks like a misprint for “eum”, which Lecky has.

The first paragraph of the chapter, which Lecky does not quote, is the decree against the statutes of Italian communes that allow or enforce the payment of usury: officials who make or enforce them are excommunicated.

Whoever affirms that usury is no sin is a heretic (§ 2)

Latin: Friedberg vol. 2, col. 1184, scan, right column, last lines. Highlighted: Lecky’s words.

§. 2. Sane, si quis in illum errorem inciderit, ut pertinaciter affirmare praesumat, exercere usuras non esse peccatum: decernimus, cum velut haereticum puniendum, locorum nihilominus ordinariis et haereticae pravitatis inquisitoribus districtius iniungentes, ut contra eos, quos de errore huiusmodi diffamatos invenerint aut suspectos, tanquam contra diffamatos vel suspectos de haeresi procedere non omittant.

§ 2. Indeed, if anyone shall have fallen into that error, so as to presume to affirm obstinately that to practise usury is not a sin, we decree that he is to be punished as a heretic, enjoining, moreover, more strictly on the ordinaries of places and the inquisitors of heretical pravity that against those whom they find defamed of, or suspected of, an error of this kind they shall not fail to proceed as against persons defamed of, or suspected of, heresy.

Lecky’s Latin has the ligature “hæreticum” and “præsumat” where this edition prints “haereticum” and “praesumat”. The differences in the table are the only ones in the words he quotes. Lecky ends at “puniendum” and so leaves out the instruction to the inquisitors.
