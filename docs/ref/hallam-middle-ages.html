Hallam, View of the State of Europe during the Middle Ages, vol. 1

The Church and manumission (p. 221)

Author
Henry Hallam (1777–1859). Lecky cites it as “Hallam, Middle Ages, vol. i. p. 221” in Chapter VI and also cites pp. 217–218 for the causes of serfdom (note 18).
Edition used
The 1822 edition, vol. 1: archive.org/details/stateofeuropedur01hall. Printed p. 221 is image n244 (p + 23). The page number matches Lecky’s; the chapter is Chap. II, Part II (the feudal system, “Gradual abolition of villenage”).
Cited in
Rationalism, ch. VI, note 19. Note 18 (pp. 217–218) is not transcribed.
Status
The sentence Lecky quotes transcribed from the page image and compared. The footnotes of the page (Hallam’s sources, including Schmidt, Du Cange and the Marca Hispanica) are not transcribed.

In note 19 Lecky puts a sentence of Hallam’s in quotation marks. It is Hallam’s, with three small changes: “enforced it” becomes “enforced manumission”, “equally ready” becomes “as ready”, and “church lands” becomes “the Church lands”. Hallam’s “it is said” marks the claim as hearsay, and Lecky keeps it.

“The clergy, and especially several popes” (p. 221)

English, from the page image. Highlighted: the words Lecky quotes.

… This, as is well known, was an exceedingly common practice with the Romans; and is mentioned, with certain ceremonies prescribed, in the Frankish and other early laws. The clergy, and especially several popes, enforced it as a duty upon laymen; and inveighed against the scandal of keeping Christians in bondage.* But they were not, it is said, equally ready in performing their own parts: the villeins upon church lands were among the last who were emancipated.† As society advanced in Europe, the manumission of slaves grew more frequent.‡ …

Lecky, 1865Hallam, 1822
Objectenforced manumission as a dutyenforced it as a duty (“it” = manumission, from the sentence before)
Readinessas ready in performingequally ready in performing
Punctuation… bondage; but they were not … parts. The villeins upon the Church lands were …… bondage. But they were not … parts: the villeins upon church lands were …

The changes do not alter the sense. “It” has no antecedent without the sentence before, so Lecky’s “manumission” is a sensible gloss, but he should have used brackets. The Church’s own record on emancipating villeins comes from the second sentence, which Hallam qualifies with “it is said”; Lecky’s “it is probable that this last fact in some degree lessened her zeal” (in his text, before note 19) goes a step beyond Hallam on this page.
