Turgot, Mémoire sur les prêts d’argent

How canon law shaped the lawyers’ view of interest

Author
Anne-Robert-Jacques Turgot (1727–1781), economist and minister. Lecky cites “Mém. sur les Prêts d’Argent, § 29” in Chapter VI.
Edition used
Œuvres de Turgot et documents le concernant, ed. Gustave Schelle, vol. 3, a modern reprint (I did not check the date of the original edition): archive.org/details/turgot-oc-3. The passage is on printed p. 168, image n168. I did not find the paragraph number (§ 29) on the page, so Lecky’s section reference is unconfirmed. I do not know which edition Lecky used and have not seen it. The translation is this edition’s own draft.
Cited in
Rationalism, ch. VI, note 30.
Status
The sentence transcribed from the page image, translated and compared. The Alexander VII proposition in the same note (Conférences sur l’usure) is not transcribed.

Lecky quotes Turgot for the view that the theologians’ “subtlety” of alienating the capital (so that a loan became a sale of an income) was adopted by lawyers under the influence of canon law. The sentence is Turgot’s. Lecky’s version has two wording differences (“des peuples” for “du peuple”, and “a été adopté aussi par les jurisconsultes, à raison de” for “est devenu aussi celui des jurisconsultes, par”), plus punctuation and accents. They may come from an older edition of the text.

The “subtilité de l’aliénation du capital” (p. 168)

French: p. 168, scan. Highlighted: Lecky’s words.

Cependant, l’opinion la plus rigide et la plus populaire prit peu à peu le dessus, et le plus grand nombre des théologiens s’y rangea, surtout dans les siècles d’ignorance qui suivirent ; mais, tandis que le cri du peuple contre le prêt à intérêt le faisait proscrire, l’impossibilité de l’abolir entièrement fit imaginer la subtilité de l’aliénation du capital, et c’est ce système qui, étant devenu presque général parmi les théologiens, est devenu aussi celui des jurisconsultes, par l’influence beaucoup trop grande qu’ont eue, sur notre jurisprudence et notre législation, les principes du droit canon.

However, the most rigid and most popular opinion gradually prevailed, and the greater number of theologians joined it, especially in the ages of ignorance that followed; but, while the cry of the people against lending at interest got it proscribed, the impossibility of abolishing it entirely led to the invention of the subtlety of the alienation of capital, and it is this system which, having become almost general among theologians, has become that of the jurists too, through the far too great influence that the principles of canon law have had on our jurisprudence and our legislation.
