Voltaire, Dictionnaire philosophique, “Patrie”

“Telle est donc la condition humaine …”

Author
Voltaire (1694–1778). Lecky: “(Dict. Phil., art. Patrie.)” (Chapter VI, note 177).
Edition used
A text, not a scan: the Garnier (1878, the Moland Œuvres complètes) text of the article on French Wikisource. The sentences below come from the section the editors identify as the last of three. The translation is this edition’s own draft.
Cited in
Rationalism, ch. VI, note 177.
Status
Text compared; no page image seen.

Lecky’s two sentences are Voltaire’s, in reverse order and joined by an ellipsis: Voltaire writes first “Il est clair qu’un pays ne peut gagner sans qu’un autre perde, et qu’il ne peut vaincre sans faire des malheureux”, then “Telle est donc la condition humaine, que souhaiter la grandeur de son pays c’est souhaiter du mal à ses voisins.” Lecky gives the conclusion first (dropping “donc” from “Telle est donc la condition humaine”) and the premise second, and ends the premise at “un autre perd”, which is a slip for Voltaire’s subjunctive “perde” (as printed in Lecky’s note, whose OCR also had “hrnnaine” and “pent”, corrected here).

The good patriot and his neighbours

French: “Patrie”, Garnier ed. (French Wikisource). Highlighted: Lecky’s sentences.

… Être bon patriote, c’est souhaiter que sa ville s’enrichisse par le commerce, et soit puissante par les armes. Il est clair qu’un pays ne peut gagner sans qu’un autre perde, et qu’il ne peut vaincre sans faire des malheureux. Telle est donc la condition humaine, que souhaiter la grandeur de son pays c’est souhaiter du mal à ses voisins. Celui qui voudrait que sa patrie ne fût jamais ni plus grande, ni plus petite, ni plus riche, ni plus pauvre, serait le citoyen de l’univers.

… To be a good patriot is to wish that one’s city may be enriched by trade and powerful by arms. It is clear that a country cannot gain without another losing, and that it cannot conquer without making people wretched. Such, then, is the human condition, that to wish for the greatness of one’s country is to wish harm to one’s neighbours. He who would wish that his country should never be either greater or smaller, richer or poorer, would be the citizen of the universe.
