Wright, Narratives of Sorcery and Magic

The Bamberg burnings (p. 326)

Author
Thomas Wright (1810–1877), antiquary. Lecky cites “Wright’s Sorcery, vol. i. p. 186” (Chapter I, note 2), “vol. i. pp. 286, 296” (note 108) and “vol. i. pp. 165, 166” (note 161).
Edition used
The 1852 reprint, two volumes in one scan with continuous page numbers: Internet Archive narrativesofsorc00wrig_0; p. 326 = image n331 and p. 186 = image n191 (image = page + 5). The Bamberg passage is in the second volume, which Lecky may be citing as “vol. i” by mistake or from a different arrangement of volumes.
Cited in
Rationalism, ch. I, note 2.
Status
pp. 186 and 326 read on the images, and p. 187 (Remigius’s nine hundred) seen. Not found in Wright pp. 325–327: seven thousand burnt at Treves, eight hundred in a year at Würzburg. Notes 108 and 161 are not checked.

Lecky says: “Seven thousand victims are said to have been burned at Treves, six hundred by a single bishop of Bamberg, and eight hundred in a single year in the bishopric of Wurtzburg.” Note 2 cites Wright, vol. i. p. 186. On p. 186 of this edition there is nothing of the kind: it describes French werewolf trials of 1573–88. Wright’s Bamberg figure is on p. 326, in the chapter on the burnings at Würzburg: a pamphlet published at Bamberg in 1659 “states the number of persons which Bishop John George had caused to be burnt for sorcery, to have been six hundred”, and Wright adds that between 1625 and 1639 “not less than nine hundred trials took place in the two courts of Bamberg and Zeil”. So “six hundred by a single bishop of Bamberg” is Wright’s; the Treves and Würzburg figures are not on these pages.

“Six hundred” burnt by Bishop John George of Bamberg (p. 326)

English: p. 326, scan. Highlighted: the figure Lecky uses.

… the statement of the most authentic historians of this city, that between 1625 and 1639, not less than nine hundred trials took place in the two courts of Bamberg and Zeil; and a pamphlet published at Bamberg by authority, in 1659, states the number of persons which Bishop John George had caused to be burnt for sorcery, to have been six hundred.

Among the persons thus sacrificed were the chancellor, his son, Doctor Horn, with his wife and two daughters, and many of the lords and councillors of the bishop’s court … It must be stated that these confessions were made under tortures of the most fearful kind, far more so than anything that was practised in France or other countries. …

Lecky (ch. I, text above note 2): “six hundred by a single bishop of Bamberg”. Matches Wright’s “six hundred” for Bishop John George (Johann Georg II Fuchs von Dornheim). Lecky’s “vol. i. p. 186” is not this page; the page 186 of this edition (“In 1573, a man who was burnt at Drôle, on the charge of having changed himself into a wolf …”) is on werewolves. Wright’s p. 187 gives Remigius’s nine hundred burnt in Lorraine, 1580–95, which is not Lecky’s figure either.
