About this project

What this is

A reading edition of William Edward Hartpole Lecky’s major works, built page by page rather than published all at once. The design borrows its reading philosophy — semantic zoom, footnotes you can hover to preview, and links that pop up what they point to — from Gwern Branwen’s gwern.net. The site’s code is our own; the footnote and popup styling is adapted from gwern.net’s stylesheet, which Gwern publishes under CC0/MIT licenses. The Wikipedia link icon is from Font Awesome Free (CC BY 4.0).

Current status

The typography, layout, footnote system, and hover-popup mechanism are built and working. Real text has started landing: the Introduction to Rise and Influence of Rationalism in Europe is sourced, OCR-cleaned, and proofread against the scan. Volume II is complete as far as Lecky’s text goes (Chapter IV Part II, Chapter V and Chapter VI, pages 1–409), so the whole of the Rationalism text is up; the index is not transcribed. Volume I (pages 1–447: Chapters I–III and Part I of Chapter IV) is complete. Sixty-nine source pages are up: Grillandus, Garinet, Bodin, the Codex Theodosianus, Sprenger’s Malleus, Gerson, Spina, Hecker, Dalyell, Scot, Burt, Hutchinson, Madden and Thiers, all for works cited in Chapter I; and Middleton, Simancas Chrysostom, Augustine on lying, Tertullian (also on baptism and the Apology and De anima), Minucius Felix and Luther, Aquinas, Agobard and Augustine’s City of God, for a few notes in Chapters II–IV. Most notes of Chapters II–IV are not yet linked to source pages. Hover a citation in the notes to preview its source, or a foreign quotation’s “English” button for a translation.

There is no clean transcription of this title anywhere (it isn’t on Project Gutenberg or Wikisource), so each chapter comes from a 160-year-old library scan’s raw OCR, run through an automated cleanup pass and then read through by hand before publishing. That means it goes up one chapter at a time rather than all at once.

Next steps, in order:

  1. Continue with Volume II (Volume I, printed pages 1–447, is done).
  2. Identify every work, document, and figure Lecky cites, and build reference pages for them — starting with the source texts (the witchcraft manuals, pamphlets, and letters), each transcribed in full where we can find them.
  3. Wire citations in the main text to hover-previews and click-throughs into those reference pages.
  4. A cross-reference map of how it all connects.

On the text

Lecky’s writing and the primary sources he cites are in the public domain. Where we rely on a particular library scan or edition, we credit it on the relevant page.
