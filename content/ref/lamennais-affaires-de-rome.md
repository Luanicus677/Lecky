abstract: |
  <p>For “a clear view of the successive stages of the secularising movement in France”, Lecky sends the reader to “the memorial on the subject drawn up by the Abbé Lacordaire, and reproduced by Lammenais”. Lamennais introduces it in one sentence: since the Pope kept them waiting indefinitely, “we thought we should address to the Pope the following Memorial, drawn up almost entirely by M. Lacordaire, which Cardinal Pacca very obligingly undertook to present”. The memorial, signed in Rome in February 1832, argues among other things that the Catholic religion is incompatible neither with liberty of worship, nor of teaching, nor of the press, “nor with any form of government”, and that in France these liberties were the only force that could save the Church from a catastrophe like the one that had ruined Catholicism in England.</p>
  <p>Gregory XVI’s answer was the encyclical <i>Mirari vos</i> (August 1832), which condemned those liberties.</p>
