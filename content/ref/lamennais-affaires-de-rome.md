abstract: |
  <p>For “a clear view of the successive stages of the secularising movement in France”, Lecky sends the reader to “the memorial on the subject drawn up by the Abbé Lacordaire, and reproduced by Lammenais”. Lamennais introduces it in one sentence: since the Pope kept them waiting indefinitely, “we thought we should address to the Pope the following Memorial, drawn up almost entirely by M. Lacordaire, which Cardinal Pacca very obligingly undertook to present”. The memorial, signed in Rome in February 1832, argues among other things that the Catholic religion is incompatible neither with liberty of worship, nor of teaching, nor of the press, “nor with any form of government”, and that in France these liberties were the only force that could save the Church from a catastrophe like the one that had ruined Catholicism in England.</p>
  <p>Gregory XVI’s answer was the encyclical <i>Mirari vos</i> (August 1832), which condemned those liberties.</p>

<section id="mirari-vos" class="passage">
<h2>Gregory XVI, <i>Mirari vos</i> (1832), as printed by Lamennais (pp. 376–379)</h2>
<p class="passage-source">Latin with facing French: Brussels 1837, “Pièces justificatives”, pp. 376–379, <a href="https://archive.org/details/a589606400lameuoft/page/n379/mode/2up">scan n379</a>–n382. Lamennais prints the whole encyclical, Latin and French on facing pages. <span class="cited-passage">Highlighted</span>: the sentences Lecky translates in the text at note 82.</p>
<figure class="facsimile"><a href="../assets/img/ref/lamennais-mirari-vos-1837-pp376-378.jpg"><img src="../assets/img/ref/lamennais-mirari-vos-1837-pp376-378.jpg" alt="Two printed pages headed ‘Pièces justificatives’, 376 and 378, in Latin: “Atque ex hoc putidissimo indifferentismi fonte absurda illa fluit ac erronea sententia, seu potiùs deliramentum, asserendam esse ac vindicandam cuilibet libertatem conscientiæ …” and “Huc spectat deterrima illa, ac nunquam satis exsecranda et detestabilis libertas artis librariæ …”" loading="lazy"></a><figcaption><i>Mirari vos</i> in Lamennais, <em>Affaires de Rome</em>, Brussels 1837, pp. 376 and 378. <span class="credit">Scan: Internet Archive (University of Toronto) · public domain</span></figcaption></figure>
<div class="parallel">
<div class="original" lang="la">
<p>[p. 376] <span class="cited-passage">Atque ex hoc putidissimo <i>indifferentismi</i> fonte absurda illa fluit ac erronea sententia, seu potiùs deliramentum, asserendam esse ac vindicandam cuilibet <i>libertatem conscientiæ</i>.</span> Cui quidem pestilentissimo errori viam sternit plena illa, atque immoderata libertas opinionum, quæ in sacræ et civilis rei labem latè grassatur, dictantibus per summam impudentiam nonnullis, aliquid ex ea commodi in religionem promanare. <span class="cited-passage">At <i>quæ pejor mors animæ, quam libertas erroris?</i> inquiebat Augustinus</span> (1). …</p>
<p>[p. 378] <span class="cited-passage">Huc spectat deterrima illa, ac nunquam satis exsecranda et detestabilis libertas artis librariæ ad scripta quælibet edenda in vulgus</span>, quam tanto convicio audent nonnulli efflagitare ac promovere. … Sunt tamen, proh dolor! qui eo impudentiæ abripiantur, ut asserant pugnaciter hanc errorem colluviem inde prorumpentem satis cumulatè compensari ex libro aliquo, qui in hac tanta pravitatum tempestate ad religionem ac veritatem propugnandam edatur. Nefas profectò est, omnique jure improbatum, patrari datâ operâ malum certum ac majus, quia spes sit, inde boni aliquid habitum iri. <span class="cited-passage">Numquid venena liberè spargi, ac publicè vendi, comportarique, imò et obbibi debere, sanus quis dixerit, quod remedii quidpiam habeatur, quo qui utuntur, eripi eos ex interitu identidem contingat?</span></p>
<p>(1) S. Aug. Ep. CLXVI.</p>
</div>
<div class="rendering">
<p><span class="cited-passage">And from this most foul fountain of <i>indifferentism</i> flows that absurd and erroneous opinion, or rather madness, that <i>liberty of conscience</i> must be asserted and secured for everyone.</span> The way to this most pestilent error is paved by that full and immoderate liberty of opinions which spreads far and wide to the ruin of Church and State, some men saying, with the utmost impudence, that some benefit flows from it to religion. <span class="cited-passage">But <i>what worse death of the soul is there than the liberty of error?</i> said Augustine.</span> …</p>
<p><span class="cited-passage">To this belongs that worst, never sufficiently execrated and detestable liberty of the booksellers’ art to publish any writings whatever to the public</span>, which some dare to demand and promote with such clamour. … Yet there are some, alas! so carried away by impudence as to assert stubbornly that the flood of errors bursting out from it is amply made up for by some book published, in this great storm of wickedness, to defend religion and truth. It is certainly wrong, and condemned by every law, to do a certain and greater evil on purpose because there is hope that some good will come of it. <span class="cited-passage">Would any sane man say that poisons should be freely scattered, publicly sold and carried about, and even drunk, because there is some remedy by which those who use it are now and then snatched from death?</span></p>
<p class="editorial-note">Translation: this edition’s draft from the Latin. Lamennais’s facing French is close to it.</p>
</div>
</div>
<p>Lecky translates the encyclical in his text (vol. II, ch. IV, at note 82) and gives the date and place correctly (“delivered at St. Maria Maggiore on the Feast of the Assumption, 1832”). His English is faithful except at one point: “that … liberty of publication of all books which the populace relish” adds a relish that is not in the Latin, which says only books published “in vulgus”, to the public. The Augustine quotation that Gregory borrowed is referred by Lamennais’s footnote to Augustine’s Letter 166.</p>
