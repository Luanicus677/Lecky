abstract: |
  <p>In the middle of his chapter on persecution Lecky turns to his own country: Ireland, he says, “is now the only civilised country where public opinion is governed, not occasionally but habitually, by theological considerations”. He declines to investigate the causes there, and adds in a note: “I have endeavoured to trace them in a book called The Leaders of Public Opinion in Ireland.”</p>
  <p>The book is his own, published four years earlier, in his early twenties, and it appeared without his name: the title page gives only the title, a motto from Grattan (“The breath of liberty, like the word of the holy man, will not die with the prophet, but will survive him”) and the publisher. The 1865 note keeps the anonymity, calling it simply “a book”. The contents list an introduction and essays on Jonathan Swift (p. 5), Henry Flood (p. 61), Henry Grattan (p. 94) and Daniel O’Connell, followed by a chapter headed “Clerical Influences”, where the causes Lecky means are presumably discussed (not checked).</p>
