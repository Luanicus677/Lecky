abstract: |
  <p>Lecky quotes Sá for the view that a nation may depose a lawful tyrant, even one to whom it swore perpetual obedience, and that anyone of the people may kill a usurping tyrant if there is no other remedy. The quotation is word for word what the 1599 printing has. In this printing Sá’s entry is short and unqualified, unlike Molina’s <a href="molina-de-iustitia-et-iure.html">longer discussion</a>.</p>

<section id="tyrannus" class="passage">
<h2>“Tyrannus” (p. 308)</h2>
<p class="passage-source">Latin: <a href="https://archive.org/details/bub_gb_5AK7-pKY-O8C/page/n309/mode/2up">scan</a>. <span class="cited-passage">Highlighted</span>: Lecky’s quotation (the second aphorism).</p>
<div class="parallel">
<div class="original" lang="la">
<p><em>1.</em> Ad tyrannum gubernantem licet recurrere pro justitia, si aliunde non possit haberi.</p>
<p><em>2.</em> <span class="cited-passage">Tyrannicè gubernans justè acquisitum dominium non potest spoliari sine publico judicio; lata verò sententia, potest quisque fieri executor: potest autem deponi à populo, etiam qui juravit ei obedientiam perpetuam, si monitus non vult corrigi. At occupantem tyrannicè potestatem, quisque de populo potest occidere, si aliud non sit remedium; est enim publicus hostis.</span></p>
</div>
<div class="rendering" lang="en">
<p><em>1.</em> One may have recourse, for justice, to a governing tyrant, if it cannot be had elsewhere.</p>
<p><em>2.</em> <span class="cited-passage">One who governs tyrannically a lawfully acquired lordship cannot be dispossessed without public judgment; but once sentence is passed, anyone may become its executor; and he can be deposed by the people, even one to whom they swore perpetual obedience, if when warned he will not amend. But one who seizes power tyrannically, anyone of the people may kill, if there is no other remedy; for he is a public enemy.</span></p>
</div>

<p>Lecky’s remark that Sá and Molina “expressed the same opinion with still greater emphasis” than Toletus (see the <a href="toletus-instructio-sacerdotum.html">Toletus page</a>) is not borne out on these pages for Molina, whose treatment is hedged with exceptions. Sá’s own entry, taken alone, is blunt: “quisque de populo potest occidere”.</p>
