abstract: |
  <p>Lecky’s list of the “types and images” of the cross, from “a writer in the beginning of the second century”, is Justin’s chapter 55: the sea cannot be crossed unless the sail (Lecky says mast) stays whole on the ship; the earth is not ploughed, and diggers and craftsmen do not work, without tools of this shape; and man differs from the beasts only by standing upright, stretching out his arms, and having a nose that runs down from the forehead, which together show “nothing other than the figure of the cross”. Then Justin quotes “the prophet”: “The breath before our face is Christ the Lord” (Lamentations 4:20).</p>
  <p>Lecky’s summary is faithful in substance, with two small liberties. Justin’s cross in the face is the nose descending from the brow; Lecky has “the eyes and nose together”. And the remark that Jeremiah “probably alluded” to the nose is Lecky’s irony: Justin simply quotes the verse after the observation.</p>

<h2>The figure of the cross in the world (c. 55)</h2>

<div class="parallel">
<div class="original" lang="grc">
<p>[θά]<span class="cited-passage">λασσα μὲν γὰρ οὐ τέμνεται, ἢν μὴ τοῦτο τὸ τρόπαιον, ὃ καλεῖται ἱστίον, ἐν τῇ νηῒ σῶον μείνῃ· γῆ δὲ οὐκ ἀροῦται ἄνευ αὐτοῦ· σκαπανεῖς δὲ τὴν ἐργασίαν οὐ ποιοῦνται οὐδὲ βαναυσουργοὶ ὁμοίως, εἰ μὴ διὰ τῶν τὸ σχῆμα τοῦτο ἐχόντων ἐργαλείων. Τὸ δὲ ἀνθρώπειον σχῆμα οὐδενὶ ἄλλῳ τῶν ἀλόγων ζώων διαφέρει, ἢ τῷ ὀρθόν τε εἶναι καὶ ἔκτασιν χειρῶν ἔχειν καὶ ἐν τῷ προσώπῳ ἀπὸ τοῦ μετωπίου τεταμένον τὸν λεγόμενον μυξωτῆρα φέρειν, δι’ οὗ ἥ τε ἀναπνοή ἐστι τῷ ζώῳ, καὶ οὐδὲν ἄλλο δείκνυσιν ἢ τὸ σχῆμα τοῦ σταυροῦ. Καὶ διὰ τοῦ προφήτου δὲ ἐλέχθη οὕτως· Πνεῦμα πρὸ προσώπου ἡμῶν Χριστὸς κύριος.</span></p>
</div>
<div class="rendering">
<p>[Consider everything in the world, whether without this figure it could be governed or hold together.] <span class="cited-passage">For the sea is not cut unless this trophy, which is called the sail, stays whole on the ship; the earth is not ploughed without it; diggers do not do their work, nor craftsmen either, except with tools that have this shape. And the human form differs from the irrational animals in nothing else than in standing upright, having the arms stretched out, and bearing on the face, extending from the forehead, what is called the nose, through which the creature breathes; and this shows nothing other than the figure of the cross. And it was said through the prophet thus: The breath before our face is Christ the Lord.</span></p>
<p class="editorial-note">Translation: this edition’s draft. The bracketed opening sentence is translated from the OCR of p. 246, not checked on the image.</p>
</div>
</div>
<p>Otto’s Latin on the facing page (p. 249) renders ἱστίον as “velum”, the sail; the “trophy” is the cross formed by mast and yard. The verse is Lamentations 4:20, which tradition ascribed to Jeremiah.</p>
