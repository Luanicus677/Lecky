abstract: |
  <p>Lecky, a Liberal writing in 1865, quotes “one of the ablest modern books in defence of the Tory theory” without naming its author in his text, and only the citation reveals it: the young Gladstone. Gladstone argues that the established Church is bound up with belief in a fixed religious truth, so that wherever Protestantism slides into latitude and toleration it turns against the union of Church and State. Lecky quotes the sentence accurately except for one word: Gladstone wrote “an <em>immutable</em> objective truth”. By the time Lecky quoted him, Gladstone was moving towards the Liberal leadership; four years later he disestablished the Church of Ireland.</p>

<h2>“Where Protestantism becomes vicious” (ch. VI § 19, p. 188)</h2>
<p class="passage-source">English: London 1838, p. 188, <a href="https://archive.org/details/stateinitsrelati00glad/page/n203/mode/2up">scan</a>. <span class="cited-passage">Highlighted</span>: Lecky’s quotation.</p>
<figure class="facsimile"><a href="../assets/img/ref/gladstone-1838-p188.jpg"><img src="../assets/img/ref/gladstone-1838-p188.jpg" alt="A printed paragraph numbered 19: “To speak of the influence of Protestantism as such upon the principle of union between the church and the state …”" loading="lazy"></a><figcaption>Gladstone, <i>The State in its Relations with the Church</i> (London, 1838), p. 188. <span class="credit">Scan: Internet Archive · public domain</span></figcaption></figure>
<blockquote>
<p>19. To speak of the influence of Protestantism as such upon the principle of union between the church and the state, to some may appear visionary or unintelligible—under many circumstances it may have been latent; but upon examination we shall find it to have been both direct and very substantial. Its character has indeed, at different stages, been very different: at first it would seem to have operated in England very favourably to this principle; and we may find that more strict regard has been paid to it, in some instances at least, by Protestant than by Romish governments. But <span class="cited-passage">at the point where Protestantism becomes vicious, where it receives the first tinge of latitudinarianism, and begins to join hands with infidelity, by superseding the belief of an</span> immutable <span class="cited-passage">objective truth in religion necessary for salvation, at that very spot it likewise assumes an aspect of hostility to the union of church and state.</span></p>
</blockquote>
