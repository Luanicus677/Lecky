abstract: |
  <p>“In former times the holy bishops preached and commended nothing to princes more than the care of religion … and now nothing is preached and urged upon the prince more than that the care of divine things does not belong to him.” Lecky quotes this sentence of Sarpi’s to make a sharp point: the doctrine that rulers have no business with religion, later the argument for toleration, was first pressed by churchmen, to keep heresy trials in the Inquisition’s hands.</p>
  <p>Lecky’s 1639 Italian edition is not online, but the passage is here in a printing of 1675, word for word apart from spelling, with one exception: where Sarpi has religion commended to the prince’s protection “by the Divine Majesty” (<i lang="it">dalla</i>), Lecky prints “della”. Lecky’s own translation, “by the Divine Majesty”, shows what he meant.</p>

<p class="passage-source">Italian: 1675, p. 79, <a href="https://archive.org/details/bub_gb_Gmvz2lsPs20C/page/n92/mode/2up">scan n92</a>. Sarpi has just described the new doctrine “that the care of religion does not belong to the prince”, coloured with two pretexts: that it is spiritual and divine, and that the prince is busy with greater things.</p>
<figure class="facsimile"><a href="../assets/img/ref/sarpi-1675-p79.jpg"><img src="../assets/img/ref/sarpi-1675-p79.jpg" alt="Printed Italian: “E certo è degna di gran maraviglia la mutazione, ch’il mondo ha fatto: Altre volte li santi Vescovi niuna cosa più predicavano, e raccommandavano à Prencipi, che la cura della Religione …”" loading="lazy"></a><figcaption>Sarpi, 1675, p. 79. <span class="credit">Scan: Internet Archive (Google Books) · public domain</span></figcaption></figure>
<div class="parallel">
<div class="original" lang="it">
<p>E certo è degna di gran maraviglia la mutazione, ch’il mondo ha fatto: <span class="cited-passage">Altre volte li santi Vescovi niuna cosa più predicavano, e raccommandavano à Prencipi, che la cura della Religione. Di niuna cosa più li ammonivano, e modestamente riprendevano, che del trascurarla. Ed adesso niuna cosa più si predica, e persuade al Prencipe, senon ch’à lui non s’aspetta la cura delle cose divine, con tutto che pel contrario la Scrittura sacra sia piena di luoghi dove la Religione è raccommandata alla Protezione del Prencipe dalla Maestà Divina</span>, laqual anco promette tranquillità, e prosperità à quei stati, dove la Pietà è favorita, si come minaccia desolazione, e destruzione, à quei Governi, dove le cose divine sono tenute come aliene.</p>
</div>
<div class="rendering" lang="en">
<p>And truly the change the world has undergone is a great wonder. <span class="cited-passage">In former times the holy bishops preached and commended nothing to princes more than the care of religion; of nothing did they admonish them more, and modestly reprove them, than of neglecting it. And now nothing is preached and urged upon the prince more than that the care of divine things does not belong to him, although, on the contrary, Holy Scripture is full of passages where religion is commended to the prince’s protection by the Divine Majesty</span>, which also promises peace and prosperity to states where piety is favoured, as it threatens desolation and destruction to governments where divine things are treated as foreign to them.</p>
