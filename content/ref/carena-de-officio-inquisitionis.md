abstract: |
  <p>Lecky notes that one ecclesiastical offence occupied “a very prominent place” in the Inquisition’s writings: “the employment by priests of the confessional for the purpose of seducing the penitents”. He sends the reader to Carena’s manual, and his pages are exactly the right ones: Title VI of Part II, “On confessors who solicit”, fills pp. 135–161 of the Lyon edition of 1649. Its opening summary lists what Lecky says: the bulls of Paul IV and Pius IV, after which the inquisitors took cognisance of the crime “even outside the territories of Spain”, and the “constitution of Gregory XV”, which the summary calls <i>odiosa</i>, a law to be construed narrowly. The summary also mentions earlier bulls “of Paul and of Sixtus”, so Lecky’s “three popes” is, if anything, short of the count.</p>

<p><b>Titulus VI. De Confessariis Sollicitantibus. Summarium.</b></p>
<p>1 De enormitate huius criminis, remissive.<br>
2 De Bullis Apostolicis, quarum vigore Inquisitores cognoscunt de hoc crimine &amp; sequentibus.<br>
5 An etiam de iure, hoc delictum spectaret ad Inquisitores, &amp; seq.<br>
7 <span class="cited-passage">Post Bullas Pauli IV. &amp; Pii IV.</span> Inquisitores cognoscebant de hoc crimine, etiam extra loca Hispaniæ. &amp; seq.<br>
10 Legum, alia favorabilis alia odiosa.<br>
11 <span class="cited-passage">Constitutio Gregorii XV.</span> est odiosa, &amp; correctoria Iuris communis, &amp; seq.<br>
15 Ante Bullam Gregor. disputabatur, an in Bullis Pauli, &amp; Sixti comprehenderetur Confessarius solicitans pueros, vel per interpositam personam, vel post Confessionem, hæc dubia hodie sunt decisa, &amp; sequentibus.</p>

<div class="rendering" lang="en">
<p><b>Title VI. On Confessors who Solicit. Summary.</b></p>
<p>1 On the enormity of this crime (by reference).<br>
2 On the apostolic bulls by virtue of which inquisitors take cognisance of this crime, and following.<br>
5 Whether, even by law, this offence belonged to the inquisitors, and following.<br>
7 <span class="cited-passage">After the bulls of Paul IV and Pius IV</span> the inquisitors took cognisance of this crime even outside the territories of Spain, and following.<br>
10 Of laws, some are favourable, some odious [to be construed narrowly].<br>
11 <span class="cited-passage">The constitution of Gregory XV</span> is odious, and corrective of the common law, and following.<br>
15 Before the bull of Gregory it was disputed whether the bulls of Paul and of Sixtus covered a confessor who solicited boys, or through a go-between, or after confession; these doubts are now decided, and following.</p>
</div>
</div>
<p>Lecky (vol. II, ch. V, note 12): “See, for example, the full discussion of the matter in Carena, <i>De Officio S. Inquisitionis</i> (Lugduni, 1649), pp. 135–161. Three popes — Paul IV., Pius IV., and Gregory XV. — found it necessary to issue bulls on the subject”. The page range is exact. Item 15 also refers to bulls “of Paul and of Sixtus”; which Sixtus is not stated on this page.</p>
