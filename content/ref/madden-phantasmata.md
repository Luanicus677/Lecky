  <p>Madden's book is a nineteenth-century medical and historical survey of religious and witch manias. On p. 305 he gives a rapid chronology of papal bulls and trials in Italy. Lecky's note 5 cites “Madden, vol. i. 305”, and it is the source of his claim that “the severity of the inquisitors at last created an absolute rebellion”.</p>

<section id="p305" class="passage">
<h2>P. 305: Venice 1518, and Como</h2>

<p class="passage-source">English: London, 1857, vol. I, p. 305 (<a href="https://archive.org/details/phantasmataorill01madd/page/n354/mode/2up">scan</a>). The text was read from the scan’s text layer; short quotations only. <span class="cited-passage">Highlighted</span>: the words Lecky’s sentence rests on.</p>

<div class="parallel">
<div class="original" lang="en">
<p>In 1518, in the Venetian territories, a great number of persons, accused of sorcery and heresy, were condemned by the Inquisition, and handed over to the secular arm […]. <span class="cited-passage">But the people of Venice rose up against the Inquisitors, and were hardly to be appeased by the Council.</span></p>
<p>[…] The Dominicans were charged with its execution in Lombardy. <span class="cited-passage">In the district of Como alone, the number who perished are estimated by Barth. de Spina (in Malleo Maleficorum) at a thousand a year</span>; and in the space of seven years, in Piedmont, Lombardy, and Mirandola, the number who perished was enormous.</p>
</div>
<div class="rendering" lang="en">
<p class="editorial-note">The source is in English, so there is no translation. The passage after the first paragraph, omitted here, is a sentence about Pope Adrian VI’s bull of 1523.</p>
</div>
</div>

<p>Lecky’s sentence (p. 5, <a href="../rationalism/vol1-ch1.html#fn5">note 5</a>) is: “In Italy a thousand persons were executed in a single year in the province of Como; and in other parts of the country, the severity of the inquisitors at last created an absolute rebellion.” Set against Madden:</p>

<tr><th>The rebellion</th><td>“in other parts of the country, the severity of the inquisitors at last created an absolute rebellion”</td><td>“the people of Venice rose up against the Inquisitors, and were hardly to be appeased by the Council” (1518). This is the only rising Madden mentions on p. 305. “Absolute rebellion” and “the severity of the inquisitors” are Lecky’s words: Madden says only that the people “rose up” and says nothing here of why.</td></tr>

<p>So Lecky’s sentence is a compression of Madden’s two sentences, with the “thousand” turned from a figure for those who “perished” into “executed”, and the Venetian rising turned into a general “absolute rebellion”.</p>
