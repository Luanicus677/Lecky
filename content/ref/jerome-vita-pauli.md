abstract: |
  <p>Lecky’s first example of a miracle that no one in the Middle Ages thought worth doubting is the death of Paul, the first hermit. In Jerome’s telling, a raven had brought Paul half a loaf every day for sixty years; when Antony came to visit, it brought a whole one, and Paul said, “Christ has doubled the ration of his soldiers.” When Paul died, two lions came running out of the desert, lay down at his feet, roared “so that you would understand that they were mourning, in the only way they could”, dug his grave with their paws, and then came to Antony with bowed heads, licking his hands and feet, “as if asking a reward for their work”: he understood that they were begging his blessing.</p>
  <p>Lecky’s summary is close, with small liberties: his “birds” are one raven, and in Jerome the lions mourn before they dig.</p>

<section id="lions" class="passage">
<h2>The two lions (cols. 27–28)</h2>
<p class="passage-source">Latin: Migne PL 23, cols. 27–28, <a href="https://archive.org/details/bim_early-english-books-1641-1700_patrologiae-cursus-completus-_1845_23/page/n15/mode/2up">scan n15</a>. <span class="cited-passage">Highlighted</span>: what Lecky summarises.</p>
<figure class="facsimile"><a href="../assets/img/ref/jerome-vita-pauli-lions.jpg"><img src="../assets/img/ref/jerome-vita-pauli-lions.jpg" alt="Printed Latin in Migne’s two-column page: “ecce duo leones ex interioris eremi parte currentes, volantibus per colla jubis, ferebantur … At ille animadvertit benedictionem eos a se precari.”" loading="lazy"></a><figcaption>Jerome, <em>Vita Pauli</em>, Migne PL 23, cols. 27–28 (the end of one column, the top of the next). <span class="credit">Scan: Internet Archive · public domain</span></figcaption></figure>
<div class="parallel">
<div class="original" lang="la">
<p>… <span class="cited-passage">ecce duo leones ex interioris eremi parte currentes</span>, volantibus per colla jubis, ferebantur: quibus aspectis primo exhorruit. Rursusque ad Deum referens mentem, quasi columbas videret, mansit intrepidus. Et illi quidem directo cursu, ad cadaver beati senis substiterunt: adulantibusque caudis circa ejus pedes accubuere: <span class="cited-passage">fremitu ingenti rugientes, prorsus ut intelligeres [<i>Al.</i> intelligeret] eos plangere</span>, quo modo poterant. <span class="cited-passage">Deinde haud procul cœperunt humum pedibus scalpere; arenamque certatim egerentes, unius hominis capacem locum foderunt</span> [<i>Ver.</i> effoderunt. Et]. Ac statim quasi mercedem pro opere postulantes, cum motu aurium cervice dejecta, ad Antonium perrexerunt, manus ejus pedesque lingentes. <span class="cited-passage">At ille animadvertit benedictionem eos a se precari.</span></p>
</div>
<div class="rendering" lang="en">
<p>… <span class="cited-passage">behold, two lions came running from the inner part of the desert</span>, their manes flying over their necks. At the sight of them he was at first afraid; but turning his mind again to God, he stood undaunted, as though he were seeing doves. They came straight to the body of the blessed old man and stopped there; fawning with their tails, they lay down at his feet, <span class="cited-passage">roaring with a great roar, so that you would understand that they were mourning</span> in the only way they could. <span class="cited-passage">Then, not far off, they began to scratch the ground with their paws, and, vying with each other to throw out the sand, they dug a place big enough to hold one man.</span> And at once, as if asking a reward for their work, moving their ears and with heads bowed, they went to Antony, licking his hands and feet. <span class="cited-passage">He perceived that they were begging a blessing from him.</span></p>
</div>
</div>
<p>Lecky (ch. II, text at note 1): “when he died, two lions issued from the desert to dig his grave, uttered a long howl of mourning over his body, and knelt down to beg a blessing from the survivor.” Jerome’s lions roar first and dig afterwards, and “knelt down” is Lecky’s version of <i lang="la">cervice dejecta</i>, “with heads bowed”.</p>
</section>

<section id="raven" class="passage">
<h2>The raven’s loaf (cols. 25–26)</h2>
<p class="passage-source">Latin: Migne PL 23, cols. 25–26, <a href="https://archive.org/details/bim_early-english-books-1641-1700_patrologiae-cursus-completus-_1845_23/page/n14/mode/2up">scan n14</a>.</p>
<figure class="facsimile"><a href="../assets/img/ref/jerome-vita-pauli-raven.jpg"><img src="../assets/img/ref/jerome-vita-pauli-raven.jpg" alt="Printed Latin: “Inter has sermocinationes suspiciunt alitem corvum in ramo arboris consedisse … Sexaginta jam anni sunt quod dimidii semper panis fragmentum accipio: verum ad adventum tuum, militibus suis Christus duplicavit annonam.”" loading="lazy"></a><figcaption>Jerome, <em>Vita Pauli</em>, Migne PL 23, cols. 25–26. <span class="credit">Scan: Internet Archive · public domain</span></figcaption></figure>
<div class="parallel">
<div class="original" lang="la">
<p>Inter has sermocinationes suspiciunt alitem corvum in ramo arboris consedisse, qui inde leniter subvolabat, et integrum panem ante ora mirantium deposuit; post cujus abscessum: Eia, inquit Paulus, Dominus nobis prandium misit, vere pius, vere misericors. <span class="cited-passage">Sexaginta jam anni sunt quod</span> [<i>Al.</i> ex quo] <span class="cited-passage">dimidii semper panis fragmentum</span> [<i>Ver.</i> fragmen] <span class="cited-passage">accipio: verum ad adventum tuum, militibus suis Christus duplicavit annonam.</span></p>
</div>
<div class="rendering" lang="en">
<p>While they were talking, they saw that a raven had settled on the branch of a tree; it flew gently down from there and laid a whole loaf before their wondering eyes. When it had gone, Paul said: “See, the Lord has sent us our dinner, truly kind, truly merciful. <span class="cited-passage">For sixty years now I have always received the piece of half a loaf; but at your coming Christ has doubled the ration of his soldiers.</span>”</p>
</div>
</div>
<p>Lecky: “the birds daily brought him a supply of food, which was just sufficient for his wants; and when a kindred spirit visited him in his retirement, they doubled the supply.” In Jerome it is one raven, and the half-loaf is Paul’s daily portion for sixty years.</p>
</section>
