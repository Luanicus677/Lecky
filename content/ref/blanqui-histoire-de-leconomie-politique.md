  <p>Note 83 is accurate: pp. 225–226 print the ordinance of 1294 on robes and tables and the ordinance on the wives of burghers, and Blanqui goes on, as Lecky says, to argue that the French king was trying to keep gold in the kingdom. Note 85 is accurate as to the officer “charged with repressing the luxury of women”, but Blanqui’s table is the Florentine budget of <em>1336–1338</em>; Lecky puts the office “in the beginning of the fourteenth century”.</p>

<section id="ordonnance-1294" class="passage">
<h2>The sumptuary ordinances of 1294 (pp. 225–226)</h2>
<p class="passage-source">French: tom. i pp. 225–226, <a href="https://archive.org/details/histoiredelco01blan/page/n256/mode/2up">scan</a>. <span class="cited-passage">Highlighted</span>: what Lecky summarises.</p>
<div class="parallel">
<div class="original" lang="fr">
<p>« Nous voulons, dit une ordonnance de 1294, que toute manière de gens qui n’ont six mille livres de rente tournois n’usent et ne puissent user de vaissellement d’or et d’argent, ni pour boire, ni pour manger, ni pour autre usage, et que nul, sous peine de corps et de biens, n’y fasse fraude ; et de l’argent susdit nous voulons faire nos monnaies pour <em>le commun profit de notre royaume</em>. »</p>
<p>Une autre ordonnance de la même année disposait ce qui suit :</p>
<p>« Nulle bourgeoise n’aura char.</p>
<p>« <span class="cited-passage">Nul bourgeois ni bourgeoise ne portera vert, ni gris, ni hermines</span>, et se délivreront de ceux qu’ils ont, de Pâques prochaines en un an. <span class="cited-passage">Ils ne porteront, ni pourront porter or, ni pierres précieuses, ni couronnes d’or, ni d’argent.</span></p>
<p>« <span class="cited-passage">Les ducs, les comtes, les barons de six mille livres de terres, ou plus, pourront faire quatre robes par an et non plus, et les femmes autant.</span></p>
<p>« <span class="cited-passage">Chevalier qui aura trois mille livres de terre pourra avoir trois paires de robes par an, et non plus</span> ; et sera l’une de ces trois robes pour l’été.</p>
<p>« Nul ne donnera au grand manger que deux mets et un potage au lard, sans fraude ; et s’il est jeûne, il pourra donner deux potages aux harengs et deux mets.</p>
<p>« Il est ordonné que nul prélat ou baron ne puisse avoir robe pour son corps de plus de 25 sous tournois l’aune de Paris. »</p>
</div>
<div class="rendering" lang="en">
<p>“We will,” says an ordinance of 1294, “that every sort of people who have not six thousand livres tournois of income shall not use, and shall not be able to use, plate of gold and silver, whether for drinking, or for eating, or for any other use, and that no one, on pain of body and goods, shall practise fraud in this; and of the said silver we will make our coin for <em>the common profit of our kingdom</em>.”</p>
<p>Another ordinance of the same year provided as follows:</p>
<p>“No burgher’s wife shall have a carriage.</p>
<p>“<span class="cited-passage">No burgher, man or woman, shall wear green, or grey, or ermine</span>, and they shall rid themselves of those they have within a year from next Easter. <span class="cited-passage">They shall not wear, nor be able to wear, gold, nor precious stones, nor crowns of gold or silver.</span></p>
<p>“<span class="cited-passage">Dukes, counts and barons with six thousand livres of land or more may have four robes a year and no more, and their wives as many.</span></p>
<p>“<span class="cited-passage">A knight who has three thousand livres of land may have three pairs of robes a year, and no more</span>; and one of these three robes shall be for summer.</p>
<p>“No one shall give at a great meal more than two dishes and a soup with bacon, without fraud; and if it is a fast-day he may give two herring soups and two dishes.</p>
<p>“It is ordained that no prelate or baron may have a robe for his body costing more than 25 sous tournois the Paris ell.”</p>
</div>
</div>
<p>Lecky’s summary (“dukes, counts, and barons, who have 6,000 livres rent, may have four robes a year, and their wives as many. Knights with 3,000 livres rent may have three. No member of the middle class may wear any ornament of gold or precious stone, or any dress that was green or grey”) follows Blanqui closely, and “the ordinance (which was issued in 1294)” is Blanqui’s dating; Lecky adds nothing about the table (two dishes and a soup) or the price of cloth. The sentences about the loss of French gold are Blanqui’s comment on p. 226 (“il fallait absolument que le numéraire sortît”, with a little less force than Lecky’s “the greatest evil that could befall the country”, which is Lecky’s gloss on “current notions”). Page 226 of this scan is blurred in places; the passage on gold is only partly legible on the image.</p>
</section>

<section id="florence-luxe" class="passage">
<h2>The Florentine officer “charged with repressing the luxury of women” (p. 250)</h2>
<p class="passage-source">French: tom. i p. 250, <a href="https://archive.org/details/histoiredelco01blan/page/n281/mode/2up">scan</a>. The page is a table of expenditure; the line is read from a blurred image.</p>
<div class="parallel">
<div class="original" lang="fr">
<p><em>Dépenses de la république de Florence, de 1336 à 1338, en livres florentines, le florin d’or à 3 livres 2 sols.</em> … Juge des appellations sur les droits de la communauté … 1,100 · <span class="cited-passage">Officier chargé de réprimer le luxe des femmes</span> … 1,000 · Officier du marché d’Orto San-Michele … 1,300 · Office de la solde des troupes … 1,000 …</p>
</div>
<div class="rendering" lang="en">
<p><em>Expenses of the republic of Florence, from 1336 to 1338, in Florentine livres, the gold florin at 3 livres 2 sols.</em> … Judge of appeals on the rights of the community … 1,100 · <span class="cited-passage">Officer charged with repressing the luxury of women</span> … 1,000 · Officer of the market of Orsanmichele … 1,300 · Office of the soldiers’ pay … 1,000 …</p>
</div>
</div>
<p>Lecky: “Even in Florence, among the officers of the republic, in the beginning of the fourteenth century, was one especially appointed ‘to repress the luxury of women.’” The phrase matches; the date in Blanqui’s table is 1336–38, which is the first half, not the beginning, of the century. Lecky’s quotation marks mark a translation, not a quotation from the French.</p>
</section>
