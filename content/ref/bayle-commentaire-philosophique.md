<section id="title-page" class="passage">
<h2>The false title page (1686)</h2>
<p class="passage-source">French: vol. 1, title page, <a href="https://archive.org/details/commentairephilo01bayl/page/n2/mode/2up">scan</a>.</p>
<div class="parallel">
<div class="original" lang="fr">
<p><strong>Commentaire philosophique</strong> sur ces paroles de Jésus-Chrit <em>Contrain-les d’entrer</em> ; où l’on prouve par plusieurs raisons démonstratives qu’il n’y a rien de plus abominable que de faire des conversions par la contrainte, et l’on réfute tous les sophismes des convertisseurs à contrainte, et l’Apologie que S. Augustin a faite des persécutions. <span class="cited-passage">Traduit de l’Anglois du Sieur Jean Fox de Bruggs par M. J. F. À Cantorbery, chez Thomas Litwel. 1686.</span></p>
</div>
<div class="rendering" lang="en">
<p><strong>Philosophical Commentary</strong> on these words of Jesus Christ, <em>Compel them to come in</em>; in which it is proved by several demonstrative reasons that nothing is more abominable than to make conversions by constraint, and all the sophisms of the forcing converters are refuted, together with St Augustine’s apology for persecutions. <span class="cited-passage">Translated from the English of Mr John Fox of Bruggs by M. J. F. At Canterbury, printed for Thomas Litwel. 1686.</span></p>
</div>
</div>
<p>The title page spells “Jésus-Chrit” (sic) and “Contrain-les d’entrer”. Lecky’s “par M. J. F.” and “Jean Fox de Bruggs” agree with the print; “Fox de Bruggs” is Bayle’s joke on “Fox of Bruges”. The imprint is a fiction; Canterbury is not where the book was printed (I give this from memory, not from the scan).</p>
</section>

<section id="part-ii-ch-iv" class="passage">
<h2>Part II, chapter IV: the objection from the Mosaic law (p. 295)</h2>
<p class="passage-source">French: vol. 2, p. 295, <a href="https://archive.org/details/commentairephilo02bayl/page/n110/mode/2up">scan</a>. Heading and opening only.</p>
<div class="parallel">
<div class="original" lang="fr">
<p><strong>Chapitre IV.</strong> <em>Quatriéme objection ; on ne peut condanner le sens literal de ces paroles Contrain-les d’entrer, sans condanner en même-tems les loix que Dieu a établies parmi les Juifs, et la conduite que les Prophetes ont quelquefois tenüe. Disparité et raisons particulieres pour l’ancienne loi qui n’ont point lieu sous l’Evangile.</em></p>
<p>Avant que de proposer cette objection je me crois obligé de dire deux mots sur un scrupule qui se pourroit élever dans l’ame de quelques personnes. Il semble dira-t-on, que je veuille soutenir qu’il n’y a que 2. chemins à prendre envers les Hérétiques, celui de les faire mourir, ou celui de les abandonner à leurs erreurs …</p>
</div>
<div class="rendering" lang="en">
<p><strong>Chapter IV.</strong> <em>Fourth objection: one cannot condemn the literal sense of these words “Compel them to come in” without condemning at the same time the laws that God established among the Jews, and the conduct the Prophets have sometimes held. Difference, and particular reasons for the old law, which have no place under the Gospel.</em></p>
<p>Before putting this objection I think myself bound to say two words on a scruple that might arise in the minds of some persons. It will seem, they will say, that I wish to maintain that there are only two paths to take towards heretics, that of putting them to death, or that of abandoning them to their errors …</p>
</div>
</div>
<p>This is the chapter Lecky cites in note 7 for “the influence of this command [the Levitical penalty for idolatry] on Christian persecution”. The heading shows that Bayle treats the Mosaic laws as an objection to be answered, not as the cause of persecution; whether the chapter also says that Christians were led by them to persecute I have not read.</p>
</section>
