abstract: |
  <p>Lecky’s note on the physician Pietro d’Abano tells the reader to “compare Naudé … Renan, Averroes”, and the comparison is worth making, because the two disagree. Naudé, defending d’Abano in 1625, says that he died in 1305 in his eightieth year and that Padua burned a dummy of straw in his place (see the <a href="naude-apologie.html#pietro-dabano">Naudé page</a>). Renan says the Inquisition “took its revenge by having his bones burned”. Then he adds a footnote that undercuts his own sentence: d’Abano’s tombstone could still be seen in the church of the Eremitani at Padua, inscribed “the ashes of Pietro d’Abano, died 1315, aged 66”.</p>
  <p>Renan also credits d’Abano with “the impious thought of the horoscope of religions”: that every great conjunction of Saturn and Jupiter brings new kingdoms, laws and prophets, “as appeared in the coming of Nebuchadnezzar, Moses, Alexander the Great, the Nazarene, Mahomet”.</p>

<section id="pietro-dabano" class="passage">
<h2>“Le fondateur de l’averroïsme padouan” (pp. 258–259)</h2>
<p class="passage-source">French: Paris 1852, § II of the chapter on Padua, pp. 258–259, <a href="https://archive.org/details/in.ernet.dli.2015.292581/page/n278/mode/2up">scans n278</a>–<a href="https://archive.org/details/in.ernet.dli.2015.292581/page/n279/mode/2up">n279</a>, with Renan’s footnote 2.</p>
<figure class="facsimile"><a href="../assets/img/ref/renan-1852-p259.jpg"><img src="../assets/img/ref/renan-1852-p259.jpg" alt="Printed French page 259: “mérita bien mieux le nom d’averroïste. La pensée impie de l’horoscope des religions … Pierre d’Abano mourut pendant qu’on instruisait son procès; l’inquisition prit sa revanche en faisant brûler ses os”" loading="lazy"></a><figcaption>Renan, 1852, p. 259. <span class="credit">Scan: Internet Archive (Digital Library of India) · public domain</span></figcaption></figure>
<div class="parallel">
<div class="original" lang="fr">
<p>C’est surtout l’étude de la médecine qui contribua à fonder à Padoue le règne des Arabes. <span class="cited-passage">Pierre d’Abano mérite, sous ce rapport, d’être considéré comme le fondateur de l’averroïsme padouan.</span> Le <i lang="la">Conciliator differentiarum philosophorum et medicorum</i>, prélude déjà aux essais de Zimara, de Tomitanus, pour mettre d’accord Aristote et Averroès. Chose singulière pourtant! Pierre d’Abano ne connaît ni le <i>Colliget</i> ni les œuvres médicales d’Averroès: toutes les citations qu’il fait de cet auteur sont empruntées à ses œuvres philosophiques. Mais à un autre titre, je veux dire par sa réputation suspecte et son orthodoxie équivoque, Pierre d’Abano mérita bien mieux le nom d’averroïste. La pensée impie de l’horoscope des religions, ensuite reprise par Pomponat, Pic de la Mirandole, Cardan, Vanini, est énoncée pour la première fois, ce me semble, dans ses écrits avec une surprenante hardiesse. <span lang="la">« Ex conjunctione Saturni et Jovis in principio Arietis, quod quidem circa finem 960 contingit annorum…. totus mundus inferior commutatur, ita quod non solum regna, sed et leges et prophetæ consurgunt in mundo…. sicut apparuit in adventu Nabuchodonosor, Moysi, Alexandri Magni, <i>Nazarei</i>, Machometi. »</span> Ceci s’écrivait en 1303. <span class="cited-passage">Pierre d’Abano mourut pendant qu’on instruisait son procès; l’inquisition prit sa revanche en faisant brûler ses os</span>, et son nom resta dans la mémoire populaire chargé de machinations infernales, et entouré de mystérieuses terreurs.</p>
<p class="note">(2) Sa pierre sépulcrale se voit pourtant aux Ermites, avec cette inscription: <i lang="la">Petri Aponi cineres. Ob. ann. 1315, æt. 66.</i></p>
</div>
<div class="rendering" lang="en">
<p>It was above all the study of medicine that helped to found the reign of the Arabs at Padua. <span class="cited-passage">Pietro d’Abano deserves, in this respect, to be considered the founder of Paduan Averroism.</span> His <i>Conciliator of the Differences of Philosophers and Physicians</i> already foreshadows the attempts of Zimara and Tomitano to reconcile Aristotle and Averroes. A strange thing, though! Pietro d’Abano knows neither the <i>Colliget</i> nor Averroes’s medical works: all his quotations from that author come from the philosophical works. But on other grounds, I mean his suspect reputation and doubtful orthodoxy, Pietro d’Abano far better deserved the name of Averroist. The impious idea of the horoscope of religions, later taken up by Pomponazzi, Pico della Mirandola, Cardano and Vanini, is stated for the first time, I think, in his writings, with astonishing boldness: “From the conjunction of Saturn and Jupiter at the beginning of Aries, which happens about every 960 years …, the whole lower world is changed, so that not only kingdoms but laws and prophets arise in the world …, as appeared in the coming of Nebuchadnezzar, Moses, Alexander the Great, the <i>Nazarene</i>, Mahomet.” This was written in 1303. <span class="cited-passage">Pietro d’Abano died while his trial was being prepared; the Inquisition took its revenge by having his bones burned</span>, and his name remained in popular memory loaded with infernal machinations and surrounded with mysterious terrors.</p>
<p class="note">(2) His tombstone can, however, be seen at the Eremitani, with this inscription: <i>The ashes of Pietro d’Abano. Died in the year 1315, aged 66.</i></p>
