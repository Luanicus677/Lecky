abstract: |
  <p>Lecky calls it “perhaps the most preposterous accusation ever brought against a philosopher”: Descartes, author of “the most sublime of all modern proofs of the existence of the Deity”, was denounced in Holland as an atheist. Voltaire, his authority, puts it more sharply: Descartes was driven from Utrecht and “suffered the accusation of atheism, the last resort of slanderers; and he who had spent all the sagacity of his mind in looking for new proofs of the existence of a God was suspected of acknowledging none.” One difference: Lecky says the accusers were the Reformed clergy; Voltaire calls them “the so-called philosophers of Holland, who understood him no better” than his French critics. Lecky’s other source, the <em>Biographie universelle</em>, may name the clergy; it has not been checked.</p>

<div class="original" lang="fr">
<p>… Universités de la Hollande, où il se retira: car dans le tems qu’on condamnoit en France les seules propositions de sa Philosophie qui fussent vraies, il fut aussi persécuté par les prétendus Philosophes [de Hollande] qui ne l’entendoient pas mieux, &amp; qui voïant de plus près sa gloire, haïssoient davantage sa personne. Il fut obligé de sortir d’Utrecht, <span class="cited-passage">il essuïa l’accusation d’Atheïsme, derniere ressource des calomniateurs, &amp; lui qui avoit emploïé toute la sagacité de son esprit à chercher de nouvelles preuves de l’existance d’un Dieu, fut soupçonné de n’en point reconnoître.</span></p>
</div>
<div class="rendering" lang="en">
<p>… the universities of Holland, where he withdrew: for at the time when the only propositions of his philosophy that were true were being condemned in France, he was persecuted as well by the so-called philosophers [of Holland], who understood him no better, and who, seeing his fame more closely, hated his person the more. He was obliged to leave Utrecht; <span class="cited-passage">he suffered the accusation of atheism, the last resort of slanderers; and he who had spent all the sagacity of his mind in looking for new proofs of the existence of a God was suspected of acknowledging none.</span></p>
</div>
</div>
<p>Lecky (vol. II, ch. IV, text at note 56): “the reformed clergy directed against him all the force of their animosity, and the accusation by which they endeavoured to stir up the civil power against the author of the most sublime of all modern proofs of the existence of the Deity, was atheism.” Voltaire supplies the charge and the irony; the clergy and the appeal to the civil power are not on this page.</p>
