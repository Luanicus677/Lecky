abstract: |
  <p>In note 19 Lecky puts a sentence of Hallam’s in quotation marks. It is Hallam’s, with three small changes: “enforced it” becomes “enforced manumission”, “equally ready” becomes “as ready”, and “church lands” becomes “the Church lands”. Hallam’s “it is said” marks the claim as hearsay, and Lecky keeps it.</p>

<section id="manumission" class="passage">
<h2>“The clergy, and especially several popes” (p. 221)</h2>
<p class="passage-source">English, from the page image. <span class="cited-passage">Highlighted</span>: the words Lecky quotes.</p>
<blockquote>
<p>… This, as is well known, was an exceedingly common practice with the Romans; and is mentioned, with certain ceremonies prescribed, in the Frankish and other early laws. <span class="cited-passage">The clergy, and especially several popes, enforced it as a duty upon laymen; and inveighed against the scandal of keeping Christians in bondage.</span><sup>*</sup> <span class="cited-passage">But they were not, it is said, equally ready in performing their own parts: the villeins upon church lands were among the last who were emancipated.</span><sup>†</sup> As society advanced in Europe, the manumission of slaves grew more frequent.<sup>‡</sup> …</p>
</blockquote>

<p>The changes do not alter the sense. “It” has no antecedent without the sentence before, so Lecky’s “manumission” is a sensible gloss, but he should have used brackets. The Church’s own record on emancipating villeins comes from the second sentence, which Hallam qualifies with “it is said”; Lecky’s “it is probable that this last fact in some degree lessened her zeal” (in his text, before note 19) goes a step beyond Hallam on this page.</p>
