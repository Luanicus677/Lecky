abstract: |
  <p>Lecky quotes Bédarride for the turning point in the history of the Jews: “Christianity took on a real consistency only under the reign of Constantine, and it is from that time that there begins, properly speaking, for the Jews, the era of religious persecutions.” The next sentence, which Lecky leaves out, is sharper: “The first Christians had professed principles of gentleness and humanity; intolerance and pride took their place when the <i>labarum</i> was unfurled at the head of the Roman legions.”</p>
  <p>For the other side Lecky sends the reader to Bédarride on the Popes who protected the Jews. The first is <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Alexander_II">Alexander II</a>, who in the eleventh century wrote to the viscount of Narbonne, who had saved the Jews of his city from crusaders on their way to fight the Saracens in Spain: “We have seen with pleasure that you have rescued the Jews from the death that threatened them: God does not rejoice in the shedding of blood.”</p>

<section id="alexander-ii" class="passage">
<h2>“Dieu ne se réjouit pas du sang répandu”: Alexander II (p. 114)</h2>
<p class="passage-source">French: Paris 1859, ch. VIII (“XI<sup>e</sup> siècle”), p. 114, <a href="https://archive.org/details/lesjuifsenfranc00beda/page/n129/mode/2up">scan</a>.</p>
<figure class="facsimile"><a href="../assets/img/ref/bedarride-1859-p114.jpg"><img src="../assets/img/ref/bedarride-1859-p114.jpg" alt="Printed French page: “Nous avons vu avec plaisir (lui dit ce pontife) que vous avez soustrait les Juifs à la mort dont on les menaçait”" loading="lazy"></a><figcaption>Bédarride, p. 114. <span class="credit">Scan: Internet Archive · public domain</span></figcaption></figure>
<div class="parallel">
<div class="original" lang="fr">
<p>Quelques années auparavant, lorsque les Français allaient combattre les Sarrasins en Espagne, la même ferveur s’était emparée des esprits, et le glaive s’était également levé sur la tête des Juifs. Dans plusieurs provinces ils avaient succombé. A Narbonne, la protection du vicomte Béranger les sauva. Ce que fit le seigneur de Narbonne était-il impossible aux chefs des autres provinces? En parlant de la conduite de Béranger, nous ne devons pas oublier la lettre que le pape Alexandre II lui écrivit à ce sujet: «&#8239;Nous avons vu avec plaisir (lui dit ce pontife) que vous avez soustrait les Juifs à la mort dont on les menaçait: Dieu ne se réjouit pas du sang répandu.&#8239;»</p>
<p>Le pape Alexandre II rendait à la Divinité le caractère dont on s’efforçait de la dépouiller; mais tout le clergé était loin de partager ces principes.</p>
</div>
<div class="rendering" lang="en">
<p>Some years before, when the French were going to fight the Saracens in Spain, the same fervour had seized men’s minds, and the sword had likewise been raised over the heads of the Jews. In several provinces they had perished. At Narbonne, the protection of the viscount Bérenger saved them. Was what the lord of Narbonne did impossible for the rulers of the other provinces? In speaking of Bérenger’s conduct we must not forget the letter that Pope Alexander II wrote him about it: “We have seen with pleasure (the pontiff tells him) that you have rescued the Jews from the death that threatened them: God does not rejoice in the shedding of blood.”</p>
<p>Pope Alexander II gave back to the Deity the character that men were striving to strip from it; but the clergy as a whole were far from sharing these principles.</p>
</div>
</div>
<p>Bédarride gives the Pope’s words in French; his note 11 to this page (not read) gives the source. On p. 260 (from the OCR) he sums up the papacy’s record: “The Holy See had almost always been a refuge for the Jews against the storm. The Popes had sometimes sold them their protection, but they had rarely refused it.” The welcome that <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Alexander_VI">Alexander VI</a> gave to the Jews expelled from Spain, which Lecky praises, is on pp. 307–310 of this edition (OCR only).</p>
</section>
