abstract: |
  <p>Lamennais broke with Rome in the 1830s. Montalembert, who had been one of his young disciples, wrote that Lamennais “drew no one after him into the abyss, absolutely no one”, and that this was, “if I am not mistaken, the only example in the history of Christianity of a man who, having in him all the stuff of the most formidable heresiarch, did not even succeed in detaching the least of the acolytes from the centre of unity.” Lecky paraphrases it fairly, and then disagrees with the moral: Lamennais, he says, never meant to found a sect.</p>

<div class="original" lang="fr">
<p>Du moins, M. de la Mennais, en s’enfonçant de plus en plus dans l’abîme, n’y entraîna personne avec lui, absolument personne. <span class="cited-passage">C’est, si je ne me trompe, le seul exemple dans l’histoire du christianisme, d’un homme qui, ayant en lui toute l’étoffe du plus redoutable hérésiarque, n’a pas même réussi à détacher du centre de l’unité le moindre des acolytes.</span></p>
</div>
<div class="rendering" lang="en">
<p>At least M. de la Mennais, sinking deeper and deeper into the abyss, drew no one after him, absolutely no one. <span class="cited-passage">It is, if I am not mistaken, the only example in the history of Christianity of a man who, having in him all the stuff of the most formidable heresiarch, did not even succeed in detaching the least of the acolytes from the centre of unity.</span></p>
</div>
</div>
<p>Lecky (ch. II, note 20): “M. de Montalembert, in his <i>Life of Lacordaire</i>, has observed of Lammenais, that there is probably no instance in history of a man possessing so eminently the gifts of a great heresiarch making so little impression by his defection from the Church, and failing so completely to become the nucleus of a sect.” A fair paraphrase; Lecky’s “probably” is Montalembert’s “si je ne me trompe”, and his “in history” is Montalembert’s “in the history of Christianity”. “Lammenais” is Lecky’s spelling, left as printed.</p>
