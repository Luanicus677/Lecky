abstract: |
  <p>Lecky says the French Church on the eve of the Revolution “on every occasion showed herself the implacable enemy of toleration”, and for “striking evidence of these sentiments” cites this pamphlet, written against the edict of 1787 that gave Protestants a legal existence. It is framed as a speech to Louis XVI in Council, and it rewrites the Wars of Religion as a warning. The toleration of the chancellor <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michel_de_l%27H%C3%B4pital">Michel de l’Hôpital</a>, it says, must be judged not “by the philosophical declamations of our century” but by the German horsemen the Calvinists hired, “by the blood shed on the plains of Dreux”, and by the murder of the Duke of Guise; and it ends the paragraph with “cette fatale tolérance”.</p>

<section id="fatale-tolerance" class="passage">
<h2>“Cette fatale tolérance” (p. 17)</h2>
<p class="passage-source">French: copy dated 1788, part I, p. 17, <a href="https://archive.org/details/discourslireauco00bonn/page/n14/mode/2up">scan n14</a>. Long s modernised. <span class="cited-passage">Highlighted</span>: the sentiment Lecky describes.</p>
<figure class="facsimile"><a href="../assets/img/ref/ministre-patriote-1788-p17.jpg"><img src="../assets/img/ref/ministre-patriote-1788-p17.jpg" alt="Printed page 17: “C’est dans ces sanglantes circonstances qu’il faut se replacer pour juger des principes qui dominoient alors le Gouvernement. Non, Sire, non, ce n’est pas par les déclamations philosophiques de notre siecle … qu’il faut apprécier le systême du tolérant l’Hôpital, & de tous ceux qui, après lui, ont été ou seroient les échos de cette fatale tolérance.”" loading="lazy"></a><figcaption><em>Discours … par un ministre patriote</em>, 1788, p. 17. <span class="credit">Scan: Internet Archive (Newberry Library) · public domain</span></figcaption></figure>
<div class="parallel">
<div class="original" lang="fr">
<p>C’est dans ces sanglantes circonstances qu’il faut se replacer pour juger des principes qui dominoient alors le Gouvernement. Non, S<span class="sc">ire</span>, non, ce n’est pas par les déclamations philosophiques de notre siecle, mais par les sept mille Réitres que les Calvinistes armerent, par le sang répandu dans les plaines de Dreux, par celui du Duc de Guise égorgé par Poltrot, <span class="cited-passage">qu’il faut apprécier le systême du <i>tolérant l’Hôpital</i>, &amp; de tous ceux qui, après lui, ont été ou seroient les échos de cette fatale tolérance.</span></p>
</div>
<div class="rendering">
<p>It is into those bloody circumstances that we must put ourselves back to judge the principles that then governed the State. No, Sire, no: it is not by the philosophical declamations of our century, but by the seven thousand German horsemen whom the Calvinists armed, by the blood shed on the plains of Dreux, by the blood of the Duke of Guise, murdered by Poltrot, <span class="cited-passage">that we must judge the system of <i>the tolerant l’Hôpital</i>, and of all those who after him have been, or would be, the echoes of that fatal tolerance.</span></p>
<p class="editorial-note">Translation: this edition’s draft. “Réitres”: German mercenary cavalry.</p>
</div>
</div>
<p>Lecky (vol. II, ch. V, note 132) offers the pamphlet as evidence that the French Church “having repeatedly asserted the evil of toleration … had no right to complain when the Revolutionists treated her according to her principles”. The page shows the argument he means: toleration as the cause of civil war, and “philosophical” humanity as the enemy.</p>
</section>
