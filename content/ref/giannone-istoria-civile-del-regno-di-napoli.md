abstract: |
  <p>Lecky dates the Church’s first step from condemning books to burning them: “as early as 443 we find Pope St. Leo burning books of the Manichæans on his own authority”, and cites Giannone. Giannone’s sentence carries the whole point, including the “own authority”: the clergy “had begun in these times to usurp the power of burning the books of heretics”, for in 443 <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Leo_I">Pope Leo</a> burned many Manichaean books in Rome, “whereas before, the censure alone belonged to the Church, but the prohibition, or the burning, to the prince”.</p>
  <p>For Giannone, a jurist fighting the Church’s jurisdiction in Naples, this was usurpation; Lecky keeps his date and his point without the polemic. Giannone’s footnote to the sentence cites a French jurist, apparently Fevret’s treatise on appeals “comme d’abus” (“Feuret. l. 8 c. 2 n. 7”, read at small size), not an ancient source.</p>

<h2>Pope Leo burns the Manichaean books (book III, ch. VI, p. 478)</h2>

<div class="original" lang="it">
<p>Avevan però gli Ecclesiastici in questi tempi cominciato ad usurparsi la potestà di bruciare i libri degli Eretici, perchè <span class="cited-passage">nell’anno 443 il Pontefice Lione il Santo bruciò in Roma molti libri de’ Manichei, quando prima la censura solamente apparteneva alla Chiesa, ma la proibizione, o bruciamento al Principe</span> (<i>a</i>), di che altrove ci tornerà occasione di più lungamente ragionare.</p>
</div>
<div class="rendering">
<p>The clergy, however, had begun in these times to usurp the power of burning the books of heretics, for <span class="cited-passage">in the year 443 Pope Leo the Saint burned many books of the Manichaeans in Rome, whereas before, the censure alone belonged to the Church, but the prohibition, or the burning, to the prince</span> (<i>a</i>); a matter we shall have occasion to discuss at greater length elsewhere.</p>
<p class="editorial-note">Translation: this edition’s draft.</p>
</div>
</div>
<p>Lecky (vol. II, ch. V, text at note 18): “At first, though the condemnation belonged to the Church, the execution of the sentence was regarded as the prerogative of the civil ruler; but as early as 443 we find Pope St. Leo burning books of the Manichæans on his own authority.” This is Giannone’s sentence in English, down to the distinction between condemning and burning.</p>
