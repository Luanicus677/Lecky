abstract: |
  <p>The <em>Malleus</em> is the best-known manual of the witch-hunters, and Lecky uses it as his chief witness for what inquisitors believed. In notes 21 and 22 he cites it for two details: that St. Paul’s instruction that women cover their heads “because of the angels” was read as a reference to incubi, and that exorcism before baptism was a later addition to the rite.</p><p>Two of Lecky’s three statements are close to the print. The third attributes to the <em>Malleus</em> a claim it does not make in so many words, and the first cites the wrong question number for this edition. See the comparisons below.</p>

<section id="angels-32" class="passage">
<h2>Pars I, Quaestio III, p. 32: “Because of the Angels”</h2>

<p class="passage-source">Latin: Lyon, 1620, vol. I, <em>Pars</em> I, Quaestio III (“An per Daemones detur procreatio” in the running title), p. 32 (<a href="https://archive.org/details/bub_gb_Dm4ywfjNpVUC/page/n77/mode/2up">scan</a>). Spelling regularised: <em>u</em>/<em>v</em> and <em>æ</em> normalised and abbreviations expanded. The right-hand margin carries the note “Mulieres velantur propter Incubos” (“Women are veiled because of incubi”). <span class="cited-passage">Highlighted</span>: the sentence Lecky reports.</p>

<div class="parallel">
<div class="original" lang="la">
<p>Insuper illud Apostoli, 1. Cor. 11, <span class="cited-passage">“Mulier debet habere velamen super caput suum propter Angelos”, multi Catholici exponunt quod sequitur “propter Angelos”, id est Incubos.</span> Ad idem est Beda in Historiis Angelorum. Item Guliel. in lib. de universo, par. ultima, tractat. 6, multipliciter. Praeterea hoc determinat S. Doctor prima par. q. 25, et in secundo scripto di. 8, et Quodlibet. 6, quo atque super Esaiam c. 13 et 14. Unde talia negare, dicit S. Tho., imprudentiae est. Id enim quod multis videtur, non potest omnino falsum esse, secundum Philosophum, de somno et vigilia, in fine, et in 2. Ethicorum. Sileo de historiis multis et authenticis tam Catholicorum quam Ethnicorum, qui Incubos esse palam asserunt.</p>
</div>
<div class="rendering" lang="en">
<p>Moreover, as to the Apostle’s saying, 1 Cor. 11, <span class="cited-passage">“A woman ought to have a veil upon her head because of the angels”, many Catholics explain the words that follow, “because of the angels”, as meaning incubi.</span> Bede, in his <em>Histories of the Angels</em>, is to the same effect. So too William, in the book <em>On the Universe</em>, last part, treatise 6, at some length. The Holy Doctor also decides this in the first part, q. 25, in the second <em>Scriptum</em>, dist. 8, and in <em>Quodlibet</em> 6, and in his commentary on Isaiah 13 and 14. Hence to deny such things, says St. Thomas, is a mark of imprudence; for what seems so to many cannot be wholly false, according to the Philosopher, in the end of <em>On Sleep and Waking</em> and in book 2 of the <em>Ethics</em>. I say nothing of the many authentic histories, of Catholics as of pagans, which openly assert that there are incubi.</p>
</div>
</div>

<details class="collapse">
<summary>Doubtful readings and untraced references</summary>
<p>The phrase “quo atque super Esaiam” is read from a dark page and may be wrong. “Beda in Historiis Angelorum” is as printed; no work of that title by Bede has been identified here. The Thomist and Aristotelian citations (“S. Doctor”, “the Philosopher”) are given as printed and have not been traced to their texts. The question number is taken from the running heads of the two preceding pages, which read “Quaestio III”; the page itself carries only the shorter title “An per Daemones detur proc. &amp;c.”.</p>
</details>
</section>

<section id="hair-273" class="passage">
<h2>Pars II, Quaestio II, Cap. I, p. 273: Incubi and Women’s Hair</h2>

<p class="passage-source">Latin: Lyon, 1620, vol. I, <em>Pars</em> II, Quaestio II, cap. I, p. 273 (<a href="https://archive.org/details/bub_gb_Dm4ywfjNpVUC/page/n312/mode/2up">scan</a>), conventions as above. The writer is not Sprenger: the sentence reports “Guilhelmus” (William of Paris, as the same text is credited in the <em>Formicarius</em> printed later in the volume). The margin note reads “Puellae pulchros crines habentes magis sentiunt incubos” (“Girls with beautiful hair feel incubi the more”). <span class="cited-passage">Highlighted</span>: the clause that bears on Lecky’s sentence.</p>

<div class="parallel">
<div class="original" lang="la">
<p>Notatur etiam a Guilhelmo quod <span class="cited-passage">Incubi plus vexare videntur mulieres et puellas pulchros crines habentes</span>, ideo quia aut curae aut ornatui crinium incumbunt huiusmodi, aut quia per crines viros inflammare cupiunt vel consueverunt, aut quia in eis inaniter gloriantur, aut quia divina bonitas hoc permittit, ut terreantur mulieres viros inflammare per ea quae etiam Daemones inflammari viros volunt.</p>
</div>
<div class="rendering" lang="en">
<p>It is also noted by William that <span class="cited-passage">incubi are seen to vex more those women and girls who have beautiful hair</span>, either because such women devote themselves to the care or adornment of their hair, or because they wish, or are accustomed, to inflame men by their hair, or because they take empty pride in it, or because divine goodness permits it, so that women may be frightened off inflaming men by the very things by which the Demons also wish men to be inflamed.</p>
</div>
</div>

<p>Lecky’s sentence (<a href="../rationalism/vol1-ch1.html#fn21">note 21</a>) is: “It was observed that they had a peculiar attachment to women with beautiful hair; and it was an old Catholic belief that St. Paul alluded to this in that somewhat curious passage, in which he exhorts women to cover their heads because of the ‘angels’ (Sprenger, <em>Mall. Mal.</em> Pars i. Quaest. 4; and Pars ii. Quaest. 2).” Set against the two passages:</p>

<tr><th></th><th>Lecky, 1865</th><th>Lyon, 1620</th></tr>
</thead>

<section id="exorcism-305" class="passage">
<h2>Pars II, Quaestio II, Cap. VI, pp. 300 and 305: Exorcism Before Baptism</h2>

<p class="passage-source">Latin: Lyon, 1620, vol. I, <em>Pars</em> II, Quaestio II, cap. VI, p. 300 (<a href="https://archive.org/details/bub_gb_Dm4ywfjNpVUC/page/n352/mode/2up">scan</a>) and p. 305 (<a href="https://archive.org/details/bub_gb_Dm4ywfjNpVUC/page/n355/mode/2up">scan</a>), conventions as above. “[…]” marks omitted text. The margin note on p. 305 reads “In primitiva Ecclesia sine exorcismo baptismus peragebatur” (“In the primitive Church baptism was carried out without exorcism”). <span class="cited-passage">Highlighted</span>: the sentences that bear on Lecky’s note.</p>

<div class="parallel">
<div class="original" lang="la">
<p><em>P. 300.</em> […] Unde cum prima satisfactio qua homo Deo consecratur sit in baptismo, oportet quod etiam homo prius exorcizetur quam baptizetur, multo fortiori ratione quam aliae res. Quia in ipso homine est causa quare Diabolus potestatem accepit in alia quae sunt propter hominem, scilicet peccatum originale vel actuale. […]</p>
<p><em>P. 305.</em> <span class="cited-passage">Nec obiectio valet quod sine exorcismis in primitiva Ecclesia baptizabantur, aut etiam quod nunc characterem baptismalem sine exorcismo valeat baptizatus recipere, quia sic Gregorius in vanum exorcismos instituisset, et Ecclesia in suis caeremoniis potius erraret.</span> Quare ne ausus sum omnino reprehendere qui maleficiatos sub conditione vellent rebaptizare, et neglecta fortassis recuperare.</p>
</div>
<div class="rendering" lang="en">
<p><em>P. 300.</em> […] Hence, since the first dedication by which a man is consecrated to God is in baptism, a man must also be exorcised before he is baptised, by a much stronger reason than other things. For the reason why the Devil received power over other things that exist for man’s sake lies in man himself: namely original or actual sin. […]</p>
<p><em>P. 305.</em> <span class="cited-passage">Nor is it a valid objection that in the primitive Church people were baptised without exorcisms, or that even now a baptised person can receive the baptismal character without exorcism; for in that case Gregory would have instituted exorcisms in vain, and the Church would be erring rather than right in her ceremonies.</span> Therefore I have not dared altogether to censure those who would rebaptise the bewitched on condition, and so perhaps recover what had been neglected.</p>
</div>
</div>

<p>Lecky’s note (<a href="../rationalism/vol1-ch1.html#fn22">note 22</a>) says that he did not identify the ancient exorcists’ inscriptions with those of the baptismal exorcists, “because Sprenger asserts that the employment of exorcising at baptisms was not introduced till a later period (<em>Mall. Mal.</em> Pars ii. Quaest. 2). Sprenger does not give his authority, but as he is usually well informed on matters of tradition, and as he treats the omission as a difficulty, I have adopted his view.” Set against the print:</p>

<div class="table-wrap">
<table class="collation">
<thead>
<tr><th></th><th>Lecky, 1865</th><th>Lyon, 1620</th></tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr><th>The claim</th><td>Sprenger “asserts” that exorcism at baptisms “was not introduced till a later period”</td><td lang="la">p. 305 does not say this in so many words. <p>Lecky’s summary is a fair reading of the substance, since the passage does concede that the early Church had no baptismal exorcism. “Asserts” is stronger than the text. Only these two pages were read; a plainer statement on another page of the same chapter has not been ruled out.</p>

<section id="cologne-461" class="passage">
<h2>Pp. 461–462: The Cologne Approbation, and the Preachers</h2>

<p class="passage-source">Latin: Lyon, 1620, vol. I, pp. 461–462 (<a href="https://archive.org/details/bub_gb_Dm4ywfjNpVUC/page/n535/mode/2up">scan, p. 461</a>; <a href="https://archive.org/details/bub_gb_Dm4ywfjNpVUC/page/n536/mode/2up">p. 462</a>), conventions as above. These pages are the preface to the approbation of the <em>Malleus</em> by the University of Cologne: the inquisitors’ own account of why they compiled the tract, before the doctors’ signatures (from p. 463). The sentence runs across the page break. <span class="cited-passage">Highlighted</span>: the words Lecky’s “rebellion” rests on.</p>

<div class="parallel">
<div class="original" lang="la">
<p><em>P. 461.</em> Et quia <span class="cited-passage">nonnulli animarum rectores, et verbi Dei praedicatores, publice in eorum sermonibus ad populum asserere et affirmare non verebantur</span></p>
<p><em>P. 462.</em> <span class="cited-passage">maleficas non esse, aut qui nihil in nocumentum creaturarum quacunque operatione efficere possent.</span> Ex quibus incautis sermonibus nonnunquam saeculari brachio ad puniendum huiusmodi maleficas amputabatur facultas: et hoc in maximum augmentum maleficarum et confortationem illius haereseos. Ideo praefati inquisitores, totis eorum viribus, cunctis periculis et insultibus obviare volentes, tractatum quendam non tam studiose quam laboriose collegerunt. […] At quoniam consonum rationi est, ut ea quae pro communi utilitate fiunt, etiam communi approbatione Doctorum roborentur, […] eundem almae universitati Coloniensi seu nonnullis ibidem sacrae paginae professoribus ad discutiendum et collationandum obtulerunt, ut si qua reprehensibilia, a Catholica veritate dissona reperirentur, eorum iudice sic refutarentur: quod tamen consona Catholicae veritati approbarentur.</p>
</div>
<div class="rendering" lang="en">
<p><em>P. 461.</em> And because <span class="cited-passage">some rectors of souls and preachers of the word of God did not fear to assert and affirm publicly in their sermons to the people</span></p>
<p><em>P. 462.</em> <span class="cited-passage">that witches do not exist, or that they could do no harm to creatures by any operation.</span> From these incautious sermons it sometimes happened that the secular arm was deprived of the power to punish such witches, and this was a very great increase of witches and a strengthening of that heresy. Therefore the aforesaid inquisitors, wishing to meet all dangers and insults with all their might, compiled a certain treatise, no less laboriously than carefully. […] But since it is agreeable to reason that things done for the common good should also be strengthened by the common approval of the Doctors, […] they offered it to the University of Cologne, or to some professors of sacred theology there, to be discussed and collated, so that if anything blameworthy, out of tune with Catholic truth, were found, it might be refuted by their judgement, and what was in harmony with Catholic truth might be approved.</p>
</div>
</div>

<p>Lecky’s sentence (<a href="../rationalism/vol1-ch1.html#fn83">note 83</a>, p. 92) is: “An assembly of doctors of the University of Cologne, which was held in 1487, lamented, and severely and authoritatively condemned, a still more startling instance of rebellion, arising from a quarter in which it was least to be expected. When the panic was raging most fiercely in the diocese of Cologne, some priests had attempted to allay the alarm by questioning the reality of the crime.” Set against the print:</p>

<div class="table-wrap">
<table class="collation">
<thead>
<tr><th></th><th>Lecky, 1865</th><th>Lyon, 1620</th></tr>
</thead>
<tbody>

<tr><th>What they said</th><td>“questioning the reality of the crime”</td><td lang="la">“maleficas non esse, aut … nihil in nocumentum … efficere possent”: witches do not exist, or can do no harm. Fits, and slightly stronger than “questioning”.</td></tr>

<p>Lecky has turned the inquisitors’ complaint about incautious preachers into a condemnation by the Cologne doctors of a “rebellion”. The facts of the preachers’ statement are right; the framing and the doctors’ part are his own.</p>
