abstract: |
  <p>Lecky’s quotation is accurate (“The water and the spirit in this place signify the same thing; and by water is meant …”). Taylor’s sentence runs across the page break: “For, first, the water and the Spirit in this place signify the same thing; and by water is meant the effect of the Spirit, cleansing and purifying the soul, as appears in its parallel place of Christ baptizing with the Spirit and with fire.” Taylor’s purpose is a defence of the Anabaptists’ objection to infant baptism: he goes on to say that if the “exclusive negative” is read by analogy with Peter’s “not the washing of the flesh”, it cannot prove the necessity of infant baptism.</p>

<section id="water-and-spirit" class="passage">
<h2>“The water and the Spirit in this place signify the same thing”</h2>
<p class="passage-source">English: pp. 328–329, <a href="https://archive.org/details/discourseofliber1836tayl/page/n369/mode/2up">scan</a> and <a href="https://archive.org/details/discourseofliber1836tayl/page/n370/mode/2up">scan</a> (chapter “Case of the Anabaptists”).</p>
<blockquote>
<p>… However, there is no danger that infants should perish for want of this external ministry, much less for prevaricating Christ’s precept of ‘Except a man be born again,’ &amp;c. For, first, <span class="cited-passage">the water and the Spirit in this place signify the same thing; and by water is meant the effect of the Spirit, cleansing and purifying the soul, as appears in its parallel place of Christ baptizing with the Spirit and with fire.</span> For although this was literally fulfilled in Pentecost, yet morally there is more in it, for it is the sign of the effect of the Holy Ghost, and his productions upon the soul … and you may as well conclude that infants must also pass through the fire as through the water. …</p>
</blockquote>
<p>Collation with Lecky: Lecky’s “baptising” for Taylor’s “baptizing” and lower-case “spirit”; otherwise identical.</p>
</section>
