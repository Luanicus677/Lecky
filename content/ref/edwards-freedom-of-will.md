abstract: |
  <p>Lecky argues that Calvinist predestination makes God responsible for sin, and calls Edwards as a hostile witness: if “author of sin” means the one who permits sin while arranging events so that it must follow, then “I do not deny that God is the author of sin.” The words are Edwards’s. But Lecky stops in mid-sentence. Edwards goes straight on: “though I dislike and reject the Phrase”, and “it is no Reproach for the most High to be thus the Author of Sin. This is not to be the Actor of Sin, but on the contrary, of Holiness.” He also insists that in the ordinary sense, God as the doer of sin, “I utterly deny” it, and that the Arminians’ own doctrine leads to the same conclusion.</p>

<section id="author-of-sin" class="passage">
<h2>“I don’t deny that God is the Author of Sin” (part IV)</h2>
<p class="passage-source">English: Boston, 1754, p. 254, <a href="https://archive.org/details/carefulstrictenq00edwa_2/page/n269/mode/2up">scan</a>. Long s modernised; italics as printed. <span class="cited-passage">Highlighted</span>: Lecky’s quotation.</p>
<figure class="facsimile"><a href="../assets/img/ref/edwards-1754-p254.jpg"><img src="../assets/img/ref/edwards-1754-p254.jpg" alt="A page of eighteenth-century print with long s: “II. They who object, that this Doctrine makes God the Author of Sin …”" loading="lazy"></a><figcaption>Edwards, <i>Freedom of Will</i>, first edition (Boston, 1754), p. 254. <span class="credit">Scan: Internet Archive · public domain</span></figcaption></figure>
<blockquote>
<p>II. They who object, that this Doctrine makes God the Author of Sin, ought distinctly to explain what they mean by that Phrase, <i>The Author of Sin</i>. I know, the Phrase, as it is commonly used, signifies something very Ill. If by <i>the Author of Sin</i>, be meant <i>the Sinner</i>, <i>the Agent</i>, or <i>Actor of Sin</i>, or <i>the Doer of a wicked Thing</i>; so it would be a Reproach and Blasphemy, to suppose God to be the Author of Sin. In this Sense, I utterly deny God to be the Author of Sin; rejecting such an Imputation on the most High, as what is infinitely to be abhor’d; and deny any such Thing to be the Consequence of what I have laid down. But <span class="cited-passage">if by <i>the Author of Sin</i>, is meant the Permitter, or not a Hinderer of Sin; and at the same Time, a Disposer of the State of Events, in such a Manner, for wise, holy and most excellent Ends and Purposes, that Sin, if it be permitted or not hindered, will most certainly and infallibly follow: I say, if this be all that is meant, by being the Author of Sin, I don’t deny that God is the Author of Sin,</span> (tho’ I dislike and reject the Phrase, as that which by Use and Custom is apt to carry another Sense) it is no Reproach for the most High to be thus the Author of Sin. This is not to be the <i>Actor of Sin</i>, but on the contrary, <i>of Holiness</i>. What God doth herein, is holy; and a glorious Exercise of the infinite Excellency of his Nature. And I don’t deny, that God’s being thus the Author of Sin, follows from what I have laid down; and I assert, that it equally follows from the Doctrine which is maintained by most of the <i>Arminian</i> Divines.</p>
</blockquote>
