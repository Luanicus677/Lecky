abstract: |
  <p>Lecky opens his history of persecution with this remark of Fox’s: a man who truly believes he can save souls from hell will think himself bound to save them by any means, even by “cutting off one generation”; only some degree of scepticism makes toleration possible. That is close to Lecky’s own thesis. The quotation is exact. On the page it comes as one of Rogers’s jottings, between Fox on the nightingales (“Had heard no nightingales here this spring—but many thrushes”) and Fox on Burke (“Never heard Burke say he was no Christian; but had no reason to think he was one—certainly no papist”).</p>

<h2>“A degree of scepticism” (pp. 49–50)</h2>
<p class="passage-source">English: London 1859, p. 49 (<a href="https://archive.org/details/recollections02rogegoog/page/n79/mode/2up">scan</a>) to p. 50 (<a href="https://archive.org/details/recollections02rogegoog/page/n80/mode/2up">scan</a>), in the chapter “Charles James Fox”. <span class="cited-passage">Highlighted</span>: Lecky’s quotation.</p>
<figure class="facsimile"><a href="../assets/img/ref/rogers-fox-pp49-50.jpg"><img src="../assets/img/ref/rogers-fox-pp49-50.jpg" alt="Two strips of a printed page headed “Charles James Fox”, a list of Fox’s remarks ending with the one on toleration" loading="lazy"></a><figcaption>Rogers, <i>Recollections</i> (London, 1859), pp. 49–50: Fox’s table-talk. <span class="credit">Scan: Internet Archive (Google Books) · public domain</span></figcaption></figure>
<blockquote>
<p>Had read Euripides with great delight.</p>
<p>The Electra of Sophocles his best.</p>
<p>Shakespeare after all affected him most. I like the three Henrys (in answer to some objection of mine); the character of Henry always excellently kept up.</p>
<p>Liked Pope, but thought him much inferior to Dryden.—Fitzpatrick was a great Popist, and would not hear of the Rape of the Lock as his best. Perhaps his Homer should be mentioned as his great work after all.</p>
<p>Bolingbroke—did not like him.</p>
<p>It was the fashion to say surgeons were always right.</p>
<p>Had heard no nightingales here this spring—but many thrushes.</p>
<p><span class="cited-passage">The only foundation for toleration is a degree of scepticism; and without it there can be none. For if a man believes in the saving of souls, he must soon think about the means; and if, by cutting off one generation, he can save many future ones from Hell-fire, it is his duty to do it.</span></p>
<p>Never heard Burke say he was no Christian; but had no reason to think he was one—certainly no papist.</p>
</blockquote>
