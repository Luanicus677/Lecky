abstract: |
  <p>When Lecky writes that “the Fathers employed all their eloquence in favour of labour”, his authority is a section of Franz de Champagny’s prize-essay on Christian charity headed “Défense du travail”. Its sharpest witness is Augustine, scolding the monks of Africa who had come to the cloister from poor trades only to be fed and clothed for doing nothing: “In the cells there will be senators working with their hands and artisans at rest! Where the landowner comes to lead a humble and poor life, the peasant will come to lead a soft and easy one!”</p>

<section id="defense-du-travail" class="passage">
<h2>“Défense du travail” (pp. 275–289)</h2>
<p class="passage-source">French: Paris 1854, ch. “Lutte contre la misère”, § II, p. 275, <a href="https://archive.org/details/lacharitechreti00chamgoog/page/n294/mode/2up">scan n294</a>.</p>
<figure class="facsimile"><a href="../assets/img/ref/champagny-p275.jpg"><img src="../assets/img/ref/champagny-p275.jpg" alt="Printed French: “II. Défense du travail. Voilà ce qui se faisait pour la propriété. Mais à côté de la propriété souffrante et menacée il y avait le travail découragé, abandonné. L’une a besoin de l’autre …”" loading="lazy"></a><figcaption>Champagny, 1854, p. 275. <span class="credit">Scan: Internet Archive (Google Books) · public domain</span></figcaption></figure>
<figure class="title-page"><a href="../assets/img/ref/champagny-1854-title.jpg"><img src="../assets/img/ref/champagny-1854-title.jpg" alt="Title page: “La Charité chrétienne dans les premiers siècles de l’Église, par le comte Franz de Champagny … Paris, Ch. Douniol, L. Maison, 1854”" loading="lazy"></a><figcaption>Title page, Paris 1854. <span class="credit"><a href="https://archive.org/details/lacharitechreti00chamgoog/page/n12/mode/2up">Scan: Internet Archive</a> · public domain</span></figcaption></figure>
<div class="parallel">
<div class="original" lang="fr">
<p>II. <span class="cited-passage">Défense du travail.</span></p>
<p>Voilà ce qui se faisait pour la propriété. Mais à côté de la propriété souffrante et menacée il y avait le travail découragé, abandonné. L’une a besoin de l’autre; la propriété est infructueuse sans le travail; le travail est sans but quand la propriété est incertaine. Ils s’utilisent, s’aident, se rémunèrent mutuellement. Autant il y avait de possesseurs expulsés ou émigrés, autant il y avait d’artisans désœuvrés …</p>
</div>
<div class="rendering" lang="en">
<p>II. <span class="cited-passage">In defence of labour.</span></p>
<p>That is what was done for property. But beside suffering and threatened property there was labour, discouraged and abandoned. Each needs the other: property is fruitless without labour; labour has no aim when property is insecure. They use, help and reward each other. For every owner driven out or emigrated, there was an artisan with nothing to do …</p>
</div>
</div>
<p>Lecky’s ch. VI note 9 (“Champagny, <i>La Charité Chrétienne</i>, pp. 275–289”) supports his sentence “The Fathers employed all their eloquence in favour of labour”. The range is the whole of this section of Champagny’s book.</p>
</section>

<section id="augustine-monks" class="passage">
<h2>Augustine on idle monks: “senators labouring, artisans at rest” (pp. 278–279)</h2>
<p class="passage-source">French (Champagny’s translation of Augustine, <i>De opere monachorum</i>, cc. 21–22, with the Latin in his notes): Paris 1854, pp. 278–279, <a href="https://archive.org/details/lacharitechreti00chamgoog/page/n298/mode/2up">scan n298</a>.</p>
<figure class="facsimile"><a href="../assets/img/ref/champagny-p279.jpg"><img src="../assets/img/ref/champagny-p279.jpg" alt="Printed French: “Mais vous, qui avez reçu une éducation plus dure, c’est-à-dire plus heureuse, vous ne voulez pas imiter ces riches du siècle qui deviennent souvent des ouvriers laborieux! Il y aura dans les cellules, des sénateurs travaillant de leurs mains, des artisans livrés au repos!”" loading="lazy"></a><figcaption>Champagny, 1854, p. 279. <span class="credit">Scan: Internet Archive (Google Books) · public domain</span></figcaption></figure>
<div class="parallel">
<div class="original" lang="fr">
<p>[p. 278] … Mais, dites-le-moi, êtes-vous venus dans le seul but d’embrasser le service de Dieu? ou bien, las d’une vie pauvre et laborieuse, n’avez-vous seulement pas cherché à être nourris et vêtus sans rien faire, et de plus à être honorés par ceux qui vous méprisaient autrefois? …</p>
<p>[p. 279] « Mais vous, qui avez reçu une éducation plus dure, c’est-à-dire plus heureuse, vous ne voulez pas imiter ces riches du siècle qui deviennent souvent des ouvriers laborieux! <span class="cited-passage">Il y aura dans les cellules, des sénateurs travaillant de leurs mains, des artisans livrés au repos! Là où le possesseur de la terre vient pour mener une vie humble et pauvre, le paysan viendra pour mener une vie douce et commode!</span> »</p>
<p>[note] <i lang="la">Nullo modo decet, in hâc vitâ ubi sunt senatores laboriosi, sint artifices otiosi; quò veniunt relictis divitiis qui fuerunt prædiorum domini, ibi sint rustici delicati.</i> Ibid., 22, 25.</p>
</div>
<div class="rendering" lang="en">
<p>[p. 278] … But tell me, did you come only to take up the service of God? Or, tired of a poor and laborious life, did you not simply seek to be fed and clothed for doing nothing, and honoured besides by those who used to despise you? …</p>
<p>[p. 279] “But you, who had a harder upbringing, that is, a happier one, will not imitate those rich men of the world who often become hard-working labourers! <span class="cited-passage">In the cells there will be senators working with their hands and artisans at rest! Where the landowner comes to lead a humble and poor life, the peasant will come to lead a soft and easy one!</span>”</p>
<p>[note] “It is in no way fitting that in this life, where there are senators who labour, there should be craftsmen at leisure; that where those who were lords of estates come, leaving their riches, peasants should be pampered.”</p>
</div>
</div>
<p>Champagny is paraphrasing and translating Augustine’s treatise <i>On the work of monks</i>; the Latin of the footnote is given as Champagny prints it, read on the page image. Lecky does not quote this passage, but it is the most vivid evidence in the pages he cites.</p>
</section>
