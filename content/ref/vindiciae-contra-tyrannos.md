abstract: |
  <p>Lecky says the Huguenot authors of the <i>Vindiciae</i> did not “shrink from the consequence” of their theory that a people may resist a king who breaks God’s law: they cited “the conduct of the people who at the instigation of Elijah massacred the whole priesthood of Baal”. They did. But the book’s point is the opposite of a massacre. Elijah, it says, “gathers the people and, as it were, summons a council”; the people put the priests to death “not in tumult or rashly, but by public authority, a council having been held and the cause tried in due order.” When the king abandons his duty, Israel must do its own, lawfully. Lecky’s page numbers fit the 1610 printing he names.</p>

<h2>Elijah and the priests of Baal (1610, pp. 38–39)</h2>

<div class="parallel">
<div class="original" lang="la">
<p>Hinc vero est, quod regibus fœdus violantibus, Prophetæ Iudam, domum Iacob, Samariam perpetuo alloquuntur, ut eos officii sui admoneant. Nec id tantum, ne Baali sacrificent, sed ut Baalem etiam, eiusque Sacerdotes, Rege invito &amp; reclamante, deturbent &amp; exterminent. [<i>margin:</i> Iere. c. 17. v. 20. &amp; passim.] Sic cum Achab occidisset Prophetas Dei, Elias Propheta congregat populum, &amp; quasi Concilium indicit, corripit, coarguit, convincit. [<i>margin:</i> 1. Reg. c. 18. v. 19.] <span class="cited-passage">Populus vero eius hortatu, Sacerdotes Baal morte mulctat.</span> Nempe officium suum deserente Rege, est quod suo fungatur Israel: nec tumultuarie aut temere, sed publica auctoritate, Concilio habito, causa ordine cognita.</p>
</div>
<div class="rendering" lang="en">
<p>Hence it is that when kings break the covenant, the prophets continually address Judah, the house of Jacob, Samaria, to remind them of their duty: and not only that they should not sacrifice to Baal, but that they should even cast down and destroy Baal and his priests, against the king’s will and protest. [Jeremiah 17:20 and passim.] So when Ahab had killed the prophets of God, Elijah the prophet gathers the people and, as it were, summons a council, rebukes them, convicts them, convinces them. [1 Kings 18:19.] <span class="cited-passage">And the people, at his urging, punish the priests of Baal with death.</span> For when the king abandons his duty, it is for Israel to perform its own: not in tumult or rashly, but by public authority, a council having been held and the cause tried in due order.</p>
</div>
</div>
<p>Lecky (vol. II, ch. V, text at note 113): the author “quotes, as an applicable precedent, the conduct of the people who at the instigation of Elijah massacred the whole priesthood of Baal”. That is the precedent; but “massacred” hides the author’s insistence that the killing was done by public authority after a council, not by a mob. The second half of Lecky’s sentence (the toleration of an “impious sacred rite” as a cause of rebellion, his p. 60) is not checked here.</p>
