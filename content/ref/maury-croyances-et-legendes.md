abstract: |
  <p>Maury catches Eusebius, the first historian of the Church, improving his evidence: quoting Josephus on the death of King Agrippa, Eusebius replaced the owl, “a bird of ill omen”, with the avenging angel of the Christian legend. Such “little frauds”, Maury says, “were regarded by the Fathers of that time as fair play against heretics”; Athanasius, Basil and Chrysostom used them more than Eusebius did, and Origen distinguished deceit used with a bad intention from deceit used with a good one. Lecky cites the page in his note on the early Church’s tolerance of pious lying.</p>
  <p>The same book gave Lecky Simon Magus’s Helena: in Simon’s system she was the incarnation of Ennoia, “the First Thought of God, who created the angels, and the world through their ministry”.</p>

<section id="pious-frauds-267" class="passage">
<h2>“Fair play against heretics”: Eusebius and the Fathers (pp. 267–268)</h2>
<p class="passage-source">French: Paris 1863, essay “La première histoire du christianisme” (on Eusebius), pp. 267–268, <a href="https://archive.org/details/croyancesetlge00maur/page/n274/mode/2up">scans n274</a>–<a href="https://archive.org/details/croyancesetlge00maur/page/n275/mode/2up">n275</a>.</p>
<figure class="facsimile"><a href="../assets/img/ref/maury-croyances-1863-p268.jpg"><img src="../assets/img/ref/maury-croyances-1863-p268.jpg" alt="Printed French: “les hérétiques; mais il faut reconnaître qu’Eusèbe, dans ses écrits, les a moins pratiquées que les Athanase, les Basile et les Chrysostôme. L’hérésie inspirait alors une telle horreur, que tout moyen paraissait légitime pour en préserver les fidèles, et Origène distingue formellement la fraude κενή de la fraude οὐ κενή …”" loading="lazy"></a><figcaption>Maury, <em>Croyances et légendes</em>, p. 268. <span class="credit">Scan: Internet Archive · public domain</span></figcaption></figure>
<div class="parallel">
<div class="original" lang="fr">
<p>[p. 267] … Dans un passage, Eusèbe altère le texte même de Josèphe qu’il cite, en substituant l’ange exterminateur au hibou, oiseau de sinistre présage dont parle l’historien juif. … Eusèbe a évidemment mis ici dans la bouche de Josèphe un détail tiré de la légende chrétienne de la mort du tétrarque israélite, détails qu’il a voulu appuyer par là de l’autorité de cet imposant écrivain. <span class="cited-passage">Ces petites fraudes ont été regardées, par les Pères de cette époque, comme de bonne guerre contre [p. 268] les hérétiques; mais il faut reconnaître qu’Eusèbe, dans ses écrits, les a moins pratiquées que les Athanase, les Basile et les Chrysostôme. L’hérésie inspirait alors une telle horreur, que tout moyen paraissait légitime pour en préserver les fidèles</span>, et Origène distingue formellement la fraude <span lang="grc">κενή</span> de la fraude <span lang="grc">οὐ κενή</span>, c’est-à-dire celle qui est employée à bonne intention, de celle qui est employée à mauvaise intention.</p>
</div>
<div class="rendering" lang="en">
<p>[p. 267] … In one passage Eusebius alters the very text of Josephus that he quotes, putting the exterminating angel in place of the owl, the bird of ill omen of which the Jewish historian speaks. … Eusebius has plainly put into Josephus’s mouth a detail taken from the Christian legend of the death of the Jewish tetrarch, a detail he wished to support with the authority of that imposing writer. <span class="cited-passage">These little frauds were regarded by the Fathers of that time as fair play against [p. 268] heretics; but it must be admitted that Eusebius practised them less in his writings than the Athanasiuses, the Basils and the Chrysostoms. Heresy then inspired such horror that any means seemed legitimate to keep the faithful from it</span>, and Origen formally distinguishes “empty” fraud (<span lang="grc">κενή</span>) from fraud that is “not empty” (<span lang="grc">οὐ κενή</span>), that is, the one used with a good intention from the one used with a bad intention.</p>
</div>
</div>
<p>Lecky’s ch. IV note 37, on the early Church’s readiness to lie for the faith, cites “the remarks … of Maury (<i>Croyances et Légendes</i>, p. 268)” beside Chrysostom’s praise of deceit in <i>On the Priesthood</i> (see the <a href="chrysostom-de-sacerdotio.html#deceit">Chrysostom page</a>). The page fits. Maury’s footnotes give Eusebius, <i>Hist. eccl.</i> II.10 for the Josephus passage and “Origen. Paulli coll., II, 8” for the two kinds of fraud; neither has been checked here.</p>
</section>

<section id="prounice-338" class="passage">
<h2>Simon Magus and Helena, the “First Thought” of God (p. 338)</h2>
<p class="passage-source">French: Paris 1863, essay “La Véronique”, p. 338, <a href="https://archive.org/details/croyancesetlge00maur/page/n345/mode/2up">scan n345</a>.</p>
<figure class="facsimile"><a href="../assets/img/ref/maury-croyances-1863-p338.jpg"><img src="../assets/img/ref/maury-croyances-1863-p338.jpg" alt="Printed French: “Dans le système religieux de Simon le Magicien, Prounice, Προύνικος, est un des noms d’Ennoia, Ἔννοια, la Première pensée de Dieu qui a créé les anges, et le monde par leur ministère. Cette Ennoia-Prounice s’était, disait-on, incarnée dans Hélène, la concubine de Simon.”" loading="lazy"></a><figcaption>Maury, <em>Croyances et légendes</em>, p. 338. <span class="credit">Scan: Internet Archive · public domain</span></figcaption></figure>
<div class="parallel">
<div class="original" lang="fr">
<p><span class="cited-passage">Dans le système religieux de Simon le Magicien, Prounice, <span lang="grc">Προύνικος</span>, est un des noms d’<i>Ennoia</i>, <span lang="grc">Ἔννοια</span>, la Première pensée de Dieu qui a créé les anges, et le monde par leur ministère. Cette Ennoia-Prounice s’était, disait-on, incarnée dans Hélène, la concubine de Simon.</span></p>
<p>Les Barbelonites donnaient également le nom de Prounice, <span lang="grc">Προύνικος</span>, à la Sophia, qu’ils représentaient comme la première émanation du premier ange. …</p>
</div>
<div class="rendering" lang="en">
<p><span class="cited-passage">In the religious system of Simon the Magician, Prounikos is one of the names of <i>Ennoia</i>, the First Thought of God, who created the angels, and the world through their ministry. This Ennoia-Prounikos had, it was said, become incarnate in Helena, Simon’s concubine.</span></p>
<p>The Barbelonites likewise gave the name Prounikos to Sophia, whom they represented as the first emanation of the first angel. …</p>
</div>
</div>
<p>Lecky’s ch. III note 23 is attached to his sentence that “Simon Magus had led about with him a woman named Helena, who, according to the Catholics, was simply his mistress, but whom he proclaimed to be the incarnation of the Divine Thought”. Maury’s page says the same, citing Epiphanius, <i>Adversus haereses</i> (“I, t. II, c. II, p. 56, éd. Petau”, his note 1). The note’s further details (the angels warring for her beauty, the Evil One holding her prisoner) are not on this page.</p>
<p>Two pages on, in the same essay (p. 340, <a href="https://archive.org/details/croyancesetlge00maur/page/n347/mode/2up">scan n347</a>), Maury quotes Epiphanius on the word and adds: “Les lexicographes s’accordent pour attribuer au mot <span lang="grc">Προύνικος</span>, le sens de <i>lascif</i>, de <i>lubrique</i>” (lexicographers agree in giving the word the sense of lascivious, lewd). This is the source of Lecky’s ch. III note 25, “Prounice properly signifies lasciviousness”, which cites “Maury, <i>Croyances et Légendes</i>, art. <i>Veronica</i>”: “La Véronique” is the running title of these pages.</p>
</section>
