heading: Sources
abstract: <p>The books, pamphlets, and documents Lecky cites, each on its own page with the passage he used, a translation where it isn’t in English, and a link to a scan of the edition the text comes from. Every source page lists the places in Lecky that cite it, so you can move in either direction.</p>

<ul class="work-list">
<li>
<div class="work-title"><a class="popup-ref" href="grillandi-de-sortilegiis.html">Grillandus, <em>De Sortilegiis</em></a><span class="work-status">2 passages</span></div>
<div class="work-desc">Paolo Grillandi’s handbook on sorcery, 1530s. Quaestio III, §12, compared against the 1545 and 1592 editions, and the passage on sorcerers and physicians from the Lyon 1620 <em>Malleus</em> collection, which also shows which edition of the <em>Malleus</em> Lecky used. Cited in <em>Rationalism</em>, ch. I, notes 1 and 71.</div>
</li>
<li>
<div class="work-title"><a class="popup-ref" href="garinet-histoire-de-la-magie.html">Garinet, <em>Histoire de la Magie en France</em></a><span class="work-status">3 passages</span></div>
<div class="work-desc">Jules Garinet’s history of sorcery in France, 1818. The preface’s survey of ancient laws against sorcerers (pp. xiii–xiv), including the sorceress “Lemia”, and the famine of 795 and the eclipse under Louis the Debonair (pp. 38, 42), in French and English, each compared with Lecky. Cited in <em>Rationalism</em>, ch. I, notes 3, 4, 9, 14, 39, 40, and others.</div>
</li>
<li>
<div class="work-title"><a class="popup-ref" href="bodin-demonomanie-des-sorciers.html">Bodin, <em>Démonomanie des Sorciers</em></a><span class="work-status">3 passages</span></div>
<div class="work-desc">Jean Bodin’s treatise on sorcerers, 1580. Baal-zebub and the flies (p. 15), the pardon of Trois-Échelles (pp. 151–152), and the appeal to the laws of all peoples against Wier (near p. 252), in French and English, each compared with Lecky. Cited in <em>Rationalism</em>, ch. I, notes 68, 74, 88, and 89.</div>
</li>
<li>
<div class="work-title"><a class="popup-ref" href="codex-theodosianus-ix-16.html"><em>Codex Theodosianus</em>, IX.16</a><span class="work-status">2 passages</span></div>
<div class="work-desc">Constantius II’s laws of 357 and 358 against divination and magic (laws 4 and 6), in Latin and English from the 1738 Leipzig edition, compared with Lecky’s quotation. Cited in <em>Rationalism</em>, ch. I, note 28.</div>
</li>
<li>
<div class="work-title"><a class="popup-ref" href="sprenger-malleus-maleficarum.html">Sprenger, <em>Malleus Maleficarum</em></a><span class="work-status">4 passages</span></div>
<div class="work-desc">The inquisitors’ handbook, Lyon 1620 edition. “Because of the angels” and incubi (Pars I), incubi and women’s hair, exorcism before baptism (Pars II), and the Cologne preface on sceptical preachers, in Latin and English, each compared with Lecky. Cited in <em>Rationalism</em>, ch. I, notes 21, 22 and 83.</div>
</li>
<li>
<div class="work-title"><a class="popup-ref" href="gerson-de-probatione-spirituum.html">Gerson, <em>De probatione spirituum</em></a><span class="work-status">2 passages</span></div>
<div class="work-desc">Jean Gerson’s tracts on testing visions and on errors about magic, from the Lyon 1620 <em>Malleus</em> collection. The holy man who shut his eyes against a devil disguised as Christ (p. 77), and those who ridicule theologians who speak of demons (p. 253, part 2), in Latin and English. Cited in <em>Rationalism</em>, ch. I, notes 37 and 82.</div>
</li>
<li>
<div class="work-title"><a class="popup-ref" href="spina-quaestio-de-strigibus.html">Spina, <em>Quaestio de Strigibus</em> and <em>Apologia</em></a><span class="work-status">4 passages</span></div>
<div class="work-desc">Bartolomeo Spina on the numbers burned at Como (p. 193), the inquisitors’ opponents and Peter Martyr (p. 278), and the doubters of the witch trials (pp. 191, 299–300), Lyon 1620 reprint, in Latin and English, compared with Lecky. Cited in <em>Rationalism</em>, ch. I, notes 5, 67 and 84.</div>
</li>
<li>
<div class="work-title"><a class="popup-ref" href="madden-phantasmata.html">Madden, <em>Phantasmata</em></a><span class="work-status">1 passage</span></div>
<div class="work-desc">R. R. Madden’s 1857 survey of religious and witch manias, vol. I p. 305: the Venetian rising of 1518 and the Como figures that Lecky’s note 5 draws on. Cited in <em>Rationalism</em>, ch. I, note 5.</div>
</li>
<li>
<div class="work-title"><a class="popup-ref" href="thiers-traite-des-superstitions.html">Thiers, <em>Traité des Superstitions</em></a><span class="work-status">1 passage</span></div>
<div class="work-desc">Jean-Baptiste Thiers on papal bulls against sorcerers in Germany and Lombardy (pp. 139–140 of the 1697 printing; Lecky’s 1741 edition was not found). Cited in <em>Rationalism</em>, ch. I, note 5.</div>
</li>
<li>
<div class="work-title"><a class="popup-ref" href="middleton-free-inquiry.html">Middleton, <em>Free Inquiry into the Miraculous Powers</em></a><span class="work-status">3 passages</span></div>
<div class="work-desc">Conyers Middleton’s 1749 attack on the Patristic miracles. The exorcists (pp. 85–87), the “continual succession” of miracles in the Church historians (Preface, p. xv), and the Fathers’ “surprizing confidence” (Introductory Discourse, p. lxxxiv), in English from the 1749 Dublin printing, compared with Lecky. Cited in <em>Rationalism</em>, ch. II, notes 3, 10 and 11.</div>
</li>
<li>
<div class="work-title"><a class="popup-ref" href="simancas-de-catholicis-institutionibus.html">Simancas, <em>De Catholicis Institutionibus</em></a><span class="work-status">4 passages</span></div>
<div class="work-desc">Diego de Simancas’s manual for judges of heresy, Rome 1575 edition. Faith with heretics (pp. 365–366), the inquisitors’ use of torture (p. 494), his attack on Vives (p. 495), and the Spanish rule of private torture (p. 506), in Latin and English, compared with Lecky. Lecky’s page numbers for torture do not match this edition. Cited in <em>Rationalism</em>, ch. III, notes 155 and 158 and ch. IV, note 37.</div>
</li>
</ul>
