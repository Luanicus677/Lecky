abstract: |
  <p>Lecky gives the number of saints’ lives in the great Bollandist collection as “about 25,000”, on Guizot’s authority, to show how thick the miraculous lay on the Middle Ages. Guizot did not count them all. He took one month, April, and counted the lives day by day: 40 on the first, 141 on the twelfth, 126 on the thirtieth, 1,472 in all. From that one month, filling three of the collection’s fifty-three folio volumes, he judged that there were “more than 25,000”, without counting the lives lost or still unpublished. Lecky’s “about” is a little lower than Guizot’s “more than”; the method, a sample of one month, is Guizot’s, and he says so.</p>

<h2>The Bollandists and the saints of April (Leçon XVII, pp. 31–32)</h2>
<p class="passage-source">French: tome II, pp. 31–32, <a href="https://archive.org/details/histoiredelacivi0002guiz/page/n44/mode/2up">scans n44</a>–<a href="https://archive.org/details/histoiredelacivi0002guiz/page/n45/mode/2up">n45</a>. Guizot’s day-by-day table is summarised, not transcribed. <span class="cited-passage">Highlighted</span>: what Lecky uses.</p>

<div class="parallel">
<div class="original" lang="fr">
<p>Ainsi se sont amassés les matériaux de la <span class="cited-passage">collection commencée en 1643 par Bolland, jésuite belge</span>, continuée depuis par beaucoup d’autres savants, et connue sous le nom de <i>Recueil des Bollandistes</i>. Tous les monuments relatifs à la vie des saints y sont recueillis et classés par mois et par jour. <span class="cited-passage">L’entreprise fut interrompue en 1794 par la révolution de la Belgique</span>; aussi le travail n’est-il terminé que pour les neuf premiers mois de l’année et les quatorze premiers jours du mois d’octobre. La fin d’octobre et les mois de novembre et de décembre manquent; mais les matériaux en étaient préparés; on les a retrouvés, et l’on ne tardera pas, dit-on, à les publier.</p>
<p>Dans son état actuel, ce recueil contient 53 volumes in-folio … Voulez-vous avoir une idée du nombre de vies de saints, longues ou succinctes, contemporaines ou non, qui remplissent ces 53 volumes? Voici le tableau, jour par jour, de celles du mois d’avril: [1<sup>er</sup> avril, 40 saints … 30 avril, 126; total, 1472.]</p>
<p>Je n’ai pas fait ce dépouillement sur les 53 volumes; mais d’après ce compte d’un mois, et à en juger par approximation, <span class="cited-passage">ils contiennent plus de 25,000 vies de saints.</span> J’ajoute que beaucoup, sans doute, ont été perdues, et que beaucoup d’autres restent encore inédites dans les bibliothèques.</p>
</div>
<div class="rendering" lang="en">
<p>So the materials accumulated for the <span class="cited-passage">collection begun in 1643 by Bolland, a Belgian Jesuit</span>, continued since by many other scholars, and known as the <i>Collection of the Bollandists</i>. All the documents on the lives of the saints are gathered in it and arranged by month and by day. <span class="cited-passage">The undertaking was interrupted in 1794 by the revolution in Belgium</span>, so the work is finished only for the first nine months of the year and the first fourteen days of October. The end of October and the months of November and December are missing; but the materials for them had been prepared; they have been found, and it is said they will soon be published.</p>
<p>In its present state the collection has 53 folio volumes … Would you like an idea of the number of lives of saints, long or short, contemporary or not, that fill these 53 volumes? Here is the table, day by day, of those of the month of April: [1 April, 40 saints … 30 April, 126; total, 1,472.]</p>
<p>I have not made this count for all 53 volumes; but from this count of one month, and judging by approximation, <span class="cited-passage">they contain more than 25,000 lives of saints.</span> I add that many, no doubt, have been lost, and that many others remain unpublished in libraries.</p>
</div>
</div>
<p>Lecky’s note 5 adds that the collection “was stopped for a time by the French Revolution, but renewed under the patronage of the Belgian Chambers”. Guizot, lecturing about 1829, has the interruption of 1794 (“the revolution in Belgium”) and only the rumour that the work would go on; the renewal is Lecky’s own information.</p>
