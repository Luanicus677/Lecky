abstract: |
  <p>Lecky describes the hypocrisy of the Inquisition’s “form of supplication”: the inquisitors handed a condemned heretic to the secular arm with a plea that his life be spared, “though Innocent VIII. had excommunicated any magistrate who either altered their sentence, or delayed more than six days in carrying it into execution.” Limborch gives the papal text. Innocent IV’s bull <em>Ad extirpanda</em> had already required the magistrate to receive the condemned “immediately, or within five days at least”; Innocent VIII’s rescript <em>Dilectus filius</em> told the inquisitors to order secular officials, “under pain of excommunication and other ecclesiastical censures”, to carry out their sentences “within six days … without any sight of the said proceedings”, all appeal being excluded.</p>
  <p>The rescript forbids the magistrate even to look at the case. Lecky’s “altered their sentence” is a fair gloss: a judge who may not see the proceedings can only execute.</p>

<h2>“Ut infra sex dies” (Book IV, ch. 41, p. 367)</h2>
<p class="passage-source">Latin: Amsterdam 1692, Book IV, ch. 41, p. 367, <a href="https://archive.org/details/per_witchcraft-in-europe-and-america_historia-inquisitionis_limborch-philippus_1692_1_578/page/n384/mode/2up">scan n384</a>. The italic marks the papal texts Limborch quotes; long s modernised. <span class="cited-passage">Highlighted</span>: what Lecky’s text reports.</p>

<div class="original" lang="la">
<p>… quod videtur fieri autoritate Innocentii IV. in Bullâ incipiente, <i>Ad exstirpanda</i>. ubi ita legitur. <i>Damnatos verò de hæresi per Diœcesanum vel ejus Vicarium, seu per Inquisitores prædictos, Potestas, vel Rector, vel ejus nuntius specialis, eos sibi relictos recipiat, statim vel infra quinque dies ad minus, circa eos constitutiones contra tales editas servaturus.</i> Eoque compelli possunt pœnâ excommunicationis, aliisque censuris Ecclesiasticis, per rescriptum Innocentii VIII. incipiens, <i>Dilectus Filius</i>; cujus hæc sunt verba. <span class="cited-passage"><i>Eisdem officialibus sæcularibus sub excommunicationis pœnâ &amp; aliis censuris Ecclesiasticis præcipiatis atque mandetis, ut infra sex dies, postquam legitimè fuerint requisiti, sine aliquâ dictorum processuum per vos agitatorum visione, sententias per vos latas contra hujusmodi hæreticos promptè exequantur appellatione remotâ.</i></span></p>
</div>
<div class="rendering">
<p>… which seems to be done by the authority of Innocent IV, in the bull beginning <i>Ad exstirpanda</i>, where we read: <i>Those condemned for heresy by the diocesan or his vicar, or by the aforesaid inquisitors, the podestà or rector, or his special messenger, shall receive when they are left to him, immediately or within five days at the least, and shall observe towards them the constitutions published against such persons.</i> And they can be compelled to this by the penalty of excommunication and other ecclesiastical censures, by the rescript of Innocent VIII beginning <i>Dilectus filius</i>, whose words are these: <span class="cited-passage"><i>You shall order and command the said secular officials, under pain of excommunication and other ecclesiastical censures, that within six days after they have been lawfully required, without any sight of the said proceedings conducted by you, they promptly carry out the sentences passed by you against such heretics, all appeal being excluded.</i></span></p>
<p class="editorial-note">Translation: this edition’s draft.</p>
</div>
</div>
<p>Limborch goes on to discuss what an inquisitor may do if the judge still delays after the five or six days: some hold that he may then name the penalty of burning, others that he should command the execution only in general terms, to avoid “irregularity”, the canonical bar incurred by a cleric who causes a death (p. 367, from the OCR).</p>
