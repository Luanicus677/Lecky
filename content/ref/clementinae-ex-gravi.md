abstract: |
  <p>Lecky’s Latin is the second paragraph of the single chapter of the Clementine title <em>De usuris</em>. He quotes it nearly word for word and stops before the clause that tells bishops and inquisitors to proceed against anyone defamed or suspected of the error. His “istum” is the edition’s “illum”; the edition’s “cum velut haereticum” (read on the image) looks like a misprint for “eum”, which Lecky has.</p>
  <p>The first paragraph of the chapter, which Lecky does not quote, is the decree against the statutes of Italian communes that allow or enforce the payment of usury: officials who make or enforce them are excommunicated.</p>

<section id="ex-gravi" class="passage">
<h2>Whoever affirms that usury is no sin is a heretic (§ 2)</h2>
<p class="passage-source">Latin: Friedberg vol. 2, col. 1184, <a href="https://archive.org/details/BD1141952/page/n627/mode/2up">scan</a>, right column, last lines. <span class="cited-passage">Highlighted</span>: Lecky’s words.</p>
<div class="parallel">
<div class="original" lang="la">
<p>§. 2. <span class="cited-passage">Sane, si quis in illum errorem inciderit, ut pertinaciter affirmare praesumat, exercere usuras non esse peccatum: decernimus, cum velut haereticum puniendum</span>, locorum nihilominus ordinariis et haereticae pravitatis inquisitoribus districtius iniungentes, ut contra eos, quos de errore huiusmodi diffamatos invenerint aut suspectos, tanquam contra diffamatos vel suspectos de haeresi procedere non omittant.</p>
</div>
<div class="rendering" lang="en">
<p>§ 2. <span class="cited-passage">Indeed, if anyone shall have fallen into that error, so as to presume to affirm obstinately that to practise usury is not a sin, we decree that he is to be punished as a heretic</span>, enjoining, moreover, more strictly on the ordinaries of places and the inquisitors of heretical pravity that against those whom they find defamed of, or suspected of, an error of this kind they shall not fail to proceed as against persons defamed of, or suspected of, heresy.</p>
</div>

<p>Lecky’s Latin has the ligature “hæreticum” and “præsumat” where this edition prints “haereticum” and “praesumat”. The differences in the table are the only ones in the words he quotes. Lecky ends at “puniendum” and so leaves out the instruction to the inquisitors.</p>
