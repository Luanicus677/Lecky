abstract: |
  <p>Lecky quotes Descartes to show what the theologians of his day believed about the fire of hell: that the damned are “really tormented by fire”, not deceived by a false idea of fire. The point arose because the objectors (p. 322) had used the opposite opinion against him: if, as “several theologians” hold, God keeps the damned in the belief that they are burning when there is no fire at all, why could he not deceive us in the same way, so that there would be “no sky, no stars, no earth”, and we would have “no arms, no feet, no eyes”? Descartes replies (p. 343) that the common opinion is that the fire is real, and cites Peter Lombard for it.</p>
  <p>Lecky’s quotation drops “naturellement” (“naturally held in the body”) and has “la mort” for Descartes’s “sa mort”. The quoted words inside Descartes’s reply are themselves a quotation, from the <em>Sentences</em> of <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Lombard">Peter Lombard</a> (book IV, distinction 44), as Descartes says.</p>

<section id="objection" class="passage">
<h2>The objection: the damned deceived by an idea of fire (p. 322)</h2>
<p class="passage-source">French: vol. II, p. 322, <a href="https://archive.org/details/oeuvresdedescar04cousgoog/page/n335/mode/2up">scan</a>. The objectors’ fifth “scruple”.</p>
<div class="parallel">
<div class="original" lang="fr">
<p>De ce scrupule en naît un cinquième, qui prend sa force de cette déception que vous voulez dénier entièrement à Dieu&#8239;; car si plusieurs théologiens sont dans ce sentiment, que les damnés, tant les anges que les hommes, sont continuellement déçus par l’idée que Dieu leur a imprimée d’un feu dévorant, en sorte qu’ils croient fermement et s’imaginent voir et ressentir effectivement qu’ils sont tourmentés par un feu qui les consume, quoiqu’en effet il n’y en ait point, Dieu ne peut-il pas nous décevoir par de semblables espèces, et nous imposer continuellement, imprimant sans cesse dans nos âmes de ces fausses et trompeuses idées&#8239;; en sorte que nous pensions voir très clairement et toucher de chacun de nos sens des choses qui toutefois ne sont rien hors de nous, étant véritable qu’il n’y a point de ciel, point d’astres, point de terre, et que nous n’avons point de bras, point de pieds, point d’yeux, etc.&#8239;?</p>
</div>
<div class="rendering" lang="en">
<p>From this scruple a fifth arises, which takes its force from the deception you wish to deny entirely to God. For if several theologians hold that the damned, angels as well as men, are continually deceived by the idea of a devouring fire that God has imprinted in them, so that they firmly believe, and imagine they actually see and feel, that they are tormented by a fire that consumes them, although in fact there is none: can God not deceive us by similar appearances, and continually impose on us by ceaselessly imprinting such false and deceitful ideas in our souls, so that we think we see very clearly, and touch with each of our senses, things that are nothing outside us, when in truth there is no sky, no stars, no earth, and we have no arms, no feet, no eyes, and so on?</p>
</div>
</div>
</section>

<section id="feu" class="passage">
<h2>The reply: “véritablement tourmentés par le feu” (p. 343)</h2>
<p class="passage-source">French: vol. II, p. 343, <a href="https://archive.org/details/oeuvresdedescar04cousgoog/page/n356/mode/2up">scan</a>; the sentence begins at the foot of p. 342 (“Et bien que …”). <span class="cited-passage">Highlighted</span>: the words Lecky quotes.</p>
<figure class="facsimile"><a href="../assets/img/ref/descartes-p343.jpg"><img src="../assets/img/ref/descartes-p343.jpg" alt="A printed French page headed “Objections et réponses 343”, with the words “qu’ils sont déçus par une fausse idée que Dieu leur a imprimée d’un feu qui les consume” in italics" loading="lazy"></a><figcaption>Descartes’s reply, Cousin’s edition, vol. II, p. 343. <span class="credit">Scan: Internet Archive (Google Books) · public domain</span></figcaption></figure>
<div class="parallel">
<div class="original" lang="fr">
<p>Et <span class="cited-passage">bien que la commune opinion des théologiens soit que les damnés sont tourmentés par le feu des enfers, néanmoins leur sentiment n’est pas pour cela <i>qu’ils sont déçus par une fausse idée que Dieu leur a imprimée d’un feu qui les consume</i>, mais plutôt qu’ils sont véritablement tourmentés par le feu&#8239;; parceque comme «&#8239;l’esprit d’un homme vivant, bien qu’il ne soit pas corporel, est néanmoins</span> naturellement <span class="cited-passage">détenu dans le corps, ainsi Dieu, par sa toute-puissance, peut aisément faire qu’il souffre les atteintes du feu corporel après</span> sa <span class="cited-passage">mort</span>, etc.&#8239;» Voyez le Maître des sentences, liv. IV, dist. XLIV.</p>
</div>
<div class="rendering" lang="en">
<p>And <span class="cited-passage">although the common opinion of the theologians is that the damned are tormented by the fire of hell, their view is not, for all that, <i>that they are deceived by a false idea of a consuming fire that God has imprinted in them</i>, but rather that they are truly tormented by fire; because “just as the spirit of a living man, though it is not corporeal, is nevertheless</span> naturally <span class="cited-passage">held in the body, so God by his omnipotence can easily make it suffer the touch of corporeal fire after</span> its <span class="cited-passage">death</span>, etc.” See the Master of the Sentences, book IV, distinction 44.</p>
</div>
