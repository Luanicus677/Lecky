abstract: |
  <p>Lecky says La Boétie’s <i>Servitude volontaire</i> first appeared, with Hotman’s <i>Franco-Gallia</i>, in “a seditious book called <i>Mémoires de l’Estat de France sous Charles IX</i>”, and refers to Tallemant. Tallemant’s page is one of his best stories. Richelieu, he says, had never read those <i>Mémoires</i>: someone mentioned the <i>Servitude volontaire</i> to him (“a schoolboy’s amplification”, Tallemant thought, “with far more reputation than it deserves”), and the cardinal sent one of his gentlemen all along the rue Saint-Jacques, the booksellers’ street, to ask for it. Nobody had heard of it, until the son of the bookseller Blaise remembered it was in the <i>Mémoires</i>. When the gentleman came back, the father told him a collector had a copy, unbound, at five pistoles. “No matter,” said the gentleman. The rogue went out by the back door and came back with the gatherings he had just unstitched from his own copy, and got his five pistoles.</p>
  <p>A footnote in this edition supplies Lecky’s fact: the treatise was first printed in 1578, in the third volume of that collection.</p>

<div class="original" lang="fr">
<p>Une chose m’a encore surpris de cet homme, c’est qu’il n’avoit jamais lu les <span class="cited-passage">Mémoires de Charles <span class="sc">ix</span></span> (2). En voici une preuve convaincante. Quelqu’un lui ayant parlé de <i>la Servitude volontaire</i> d’Etienne de La Boëtie, <span class="cited-passage">c’est un des Traités de ces Mémoires</span>, et un Traité, pour dire ce que j’en pense, qui n’est qu’une amplification de collége, et qui a eu bien plus de réputation qu’il n’en mérite; il eut envie de voir cette pièce: il envoie un de ses gentilshommes par toute la rue Saint-Jacques demander <i>la Servitude volontaire</i>. Les libraires disoient tous: « Nous ne savons ce que « c’est. » Ils ne se ressouvenoient point que cela étoit [p. 396] dans les Mémoires de Charles <span class="sc">ix</span>. Enfin le fils de Blaise, un libraire assez célèbre, s’en ressouvint et le dit à son père; et quand le gentilhomme repassa: « Monsieur, « lui dit-il, il y a un curieux qui a ce que vous cher« chez, mais sans être relié, et il en veut avoir cinq « pistoles. — N’importe, » dit le gentilhomme. Le galant sort par la porte de derrière et revient avec les cahiers qu’il avoit décousus, et eut les cinq pistoles.</p>
<p>(2) <span class="cited-passage">Mémoires de l’état de la France sous Charles <span class="sc">ix</span>. Le Traité de la servitude volontaire a été imprimé pour la première fois, en 1578, dans le tome 3 de ce Recueil</span>, folio 116.</p>
</div>
<div class="rendering">
<p>Another thing about this man surprised me: he had never read the <span class="cited-passage">Memoirs of Charles IX</span>. Here is convincing proof. Someone having spoken to him of Étienne de La Boétie’s <i>Voluntary Servitude</i>, <span class="cited-passage">which is one of the treatises in those Memoirs</span> (and a treatise which, to say what I think of it, is nothing but a schoolboy’s amplification, and has had far more reputation than it deserves), he wanted to see the piece: he sends one of his gentlemen along the whole rue Saint-Jacques to ask for the <i>Voluntary Servitude</i>. The booksellers all said: “We don’t know what that is.” They did not remember that it was in the Memoirs of Charles IX. At last the son of Blaise, a fairly well-known bookseller, remembered it and told his father; and when the gentleman came by again: “Sir,” he said to him, “there is a collector who has what you are looking for, but unbound, and he wants five pistoles for it.” “No matter,” said the gentleman. The rascal goes out by the back door and comes back with the gatherings he had unstitched, and got the five pistoles.</p>
<p>(2) [Footnote in this edition:] <span class="cited-passage">Memoirs of the State of France under Charles IX. The treatise of voluntary servitude was printed for the first time in 1578, in volume 3 of this collection</span>, folio 116.</p>

<p>Lecky (vol. II, ch. V, note 125): “It appeared for the first time, together with the <i>Franco-Gallia</i>, in a seditious book called <i>Mémoires de l’Estat de France sous Charles IX</i>.” The first printing and the collection come from this page and its note; that the <i>Franco-Gallia</i> was in the same collection is not on these pages.
