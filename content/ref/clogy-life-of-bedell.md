abstract: |
  <p>Lecky’s “amusing instance of the uncompromising Protestantism of Bedell”: when the insurgents of 1641 allowed him to hold Anglican services freely, “he availed himself of that permission to celebrate the thanksgiving for the 5th of November”, the yearly service of thanks for the deliverance of 1605 from the Catholic Gunpowder Plot. Clogy, who was with him, records both halves. While the rebels held the bishop’s house, “until the 18th day of December”, “we had liberty without interruption in all the public and private duties of religion”. And, in his list of the bishop’s sermons: “On the Gun-powder-treason day he preached on the whole one-hundred-and-twenty-fourth Psalm”, the psalm of deliverance (“If it had not been the Lord who was on our side … then they had swallowed us up quick”). The 1862 editor’s footnote dates it 5 November 1641, a fortnight after the rising began.</p>
  <p>Clogy says nothing of a formal permission from the insurgents; Lecky’s “gave him permission” is his reading of the “liberty” Clogy describes. Clogy adds elsewhere that Bedell read the letter announcing the discovery of “the hellish powder-plot” from the pulpit every 5 November “all the years of his life” (p. 227, from the OCR, not checked on the image).</p>

<section id="liberty" class="passage">
<h2>“Liberty without interruption” (p. 183)</h2>
<p class="passage-source">English: London 1862, p. 183, <a href="https://archive.org/details/memoirlifeandep00cloggoog/page/n200/mode/2up">scan n200</a>. Clogy describes the weeks between the rising of October 1641 and the bishop’s imprisonment on 18 December.</p>
<figure class="facsimile"><a href="../assets/img/ref/clogy-bedell-1862-p183.jpg"><img src="../assets/img/ref/clogy-bedell-1862-p183.jpg" alt="Printed text: “Yet (by the blessing of God) we had liberty without interruption in all the public and private duties of religion to converse with God; and in everything with prayer and supplication, with thanksgiving, to make our case and requests known unto Him … Then began we to prize Gospel-ordinances more than when we enjoyed them in peace and plenty; whereas now we waited upon God in them in great danger, and want of all things.”" loading="lazy"></a><figcaption>Clogy, <em>Memoir of Bedell</em>, 1862, p. 183. <span class="credit">Scan: Internet Archive (Google Books) · public domain</span></figcaption></figure>
<blockquote>
<p>… until the 18th day of December following; where though we got our bread daily, with the perils of our lives, … <span class="cited-passage">Yet (by the blessing of God) we had liberty without interruption in all the public and private duties of religion to converse with God</span>; and in everything with prayer and supplication, with thanksgiving, to make our case and requests known unto Him; that can speak a word in due season to a weary soul, and turn him to the prayer of the destitute at last. Then began we to prize Gospel-ordinances more than when we enjoyed them in peace and plenty; whereas now we waited upon God in them in great danger, and want of all things.</p>
</blockquote>
</section>

<section id="gunpowder-day" class="passage">
<h2>“On the Gun-powder-treason day” (p. 212)</h2>
<p class="passage-source">English: London 1862, p. 212, <a href="https://archive.org/details/memoirlifeandep00cloggoog/page/n229/mode/2up">scan n229</a>, with the editor’s footnote from the foot of the page. <span class="cited-passage">Highlighted</span>: what Lecky’s note reports.</p>
<figure class="facsimile"><a href="../assets/img/ref/clogy-bedell-1862-p212.jpg"><img src="../assets/img/ref/clogy-bedell-1862-p212.jpg" alt="Printed text: “On the Gun-powder-treason day* he preached on the whole one-hundred-and-twenty-fourth Psalm.” Footnote: “* 5 Nov. 1641.”" loading="lazy"></a><figcaption>Clogy, <em>Memoir of Bedell</em>, 1862, p. 212, with its footnote. <span class="credit">Scan: Internet Archive (Google Books) · public domain</span></figcaption></figure>
<blockquote>
<p><span class="cited-passage">On the Gun-powder-treason day* he preached on the whole one-hundred-and-twenty-fourth Psalm.</span> The sabbath day next after his commitment, which was the 19th day of December, he preached upon Phil. iv. 4–7 …</p>
<p>* 5 Nov. 1641.</p>
</blockquote>
<p>Lecky (vol. II, ch. IV, note 3): “when the insurgents who retained him prisoner gave him permission to perform the Anglican service freely with his friends, he availed himself of that permission to celebrate the thanksgiving for the 5th of November.” Strictly, Bedell was not yet a prisoner on 5 November: the rebels held his house and district, and he was committed to Cloughoughter castle on 18 December.</p>
</section>
