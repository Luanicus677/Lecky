<section id="enfant-damne" class="passage">
<h2>The infant damned for a sin of six thousand years ago</h2>
<p class="passage-source">French: Brunschvicg ed., Section VII (French Wikisource). <span class="cited-passage">Highlighted</span>: what Lecky quotes.</p>
<div class="parallel">
<div class="original" lang="fr">
<p>… Cet écoulement ne nous paraît pas seulement impossible, il nous semble même très injuste ; <span class="cited-passage">car qu’y a-t-il de plus contraire aux règles de notre misérable justice que de damner éternellement un enfant incapable de volonté, pour un péché où il paraît avoir si peu de part, qu’il est commis six mille ans avant qu’il fût en être ? Certainement rien ne nous heurte plus rudement que cette doctrine ; et cependant, sans ce mystère, le plus incompréhensible de tous, nous sommes incompréhensibles à nous-mêmes.</span> Le nœud de notre condition prend ses replis et ses tours dans cet abîme ; de sorte que l’homme est plus inconcevable sans ce mystère que ce mystère n’est inconcevable à l’homme. …</p>
</div>
<div class="rendering" lang="en">
<p>… This flowing down does not merely seem impossible to us, it seems to us very unjust; <span class="cited-passage">for what is more contrary to the rules of our wretched justice than to damn eternally a child incapable of will, for a sin in which it appears to have had so little part that it was committed six thousand years before it was in being? Certainly nothing shocks us more roughly than this doctrine; and yet, without this mystery, the most incomprehensible of all, we are incomprehensible to ourselves.</span> The knot of our condition takes its folds and turns in this abyss; so that man is more inconceivable without this mystery than this mystery is inconceivable to man. …</p>
</div>
</div>
<p>Lecky introduces it as Pascal “stating the doctrine in a very repulsive form”, which is how the passage reads; Pascal’s purpose is to show that the doctrine, hard as it is, explains man to himself.</p>
