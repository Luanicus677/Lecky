abstract: |
  <p>Under Roman law an actress could not simply leave the stage; Christian emperors let her go if she asked for baptism. This law of 381 says what happens if she then goes back to her old life: she is to be “dragged back to the stage” and kept there, with no hope of release, “until she is a ridiculous old woman, deformed by age”, and not even then. Lecky quotes the phrase <i>ridicula anus</i> to show how the Christian law regarded the theatre: the stage itself is the punishment. The law’s last clause is the sting: not even then is she to be released, “when she can be nothing but chaste”.</p>

<section id="law-8" class="passage">
<h2>“Donec anus ridicula, senectute deformis” (381)</h2>
<p class="passage-source">Latin: Leipzig 1741, vol. V, p. 420, <a href="https://archive.org/details/BRes1409275/page/n423/mode/2up">scan</a>. Addressed by “the same Augusti” as the law before (in 381, Gratian, Valentinian II and Theodosius I) to Valerianus, prefect of the City. Editors’ note letters omitted. <span class="cited-passage">Highlighted</span>: the words Lecky quotes.</p>
<figure class="facsimile"><a href="../assets/img/ref/codex-theodosianus-xv-7-8.jpg"><img src="../assets/img/ref/codex-theodosianus-xv-7-8.jpg" alt="Printed Latin: “Scaenae mulier, si vacationem Religionis nomine postularit … donec anus ridicula, senectute deformis”" loading="lazy"></a><figcaption>CTh XV.7.8 in Gothofredus’s edition, vol. V, p. 420. <span class="credit">Scan: Internet Archive · public domain</span></figcaption></figure>
<div class="parallel">
<div class="original" lang="la">
<p>VIII. Idem AAA. ad Valerianum P. V.</p>
<p>Scaenae mulier, si vacationem religionis nomine postularit, obtentu quidem petitionis veniae non desit, verum si post turpibus volutata complexibus, et religionem quam expetierit prodidisse, et gerere, quod officio desierat, animo tamen scaenica detegetur, detracta in pulpitum, sine spe absolutionis ullius ibi eousque permaneat, <span class="cited-passage">donec anus ridicula, senectute deformis</span>, nec tunc quidem absolutione potiatur, cum aliud quam casta esse non possit. Dat. VIII Id. Mai., Aquila, Syagrio et Eucherio coss. [381.]</p>
</div>
<div class="rendering" lang="en">
<p>8. The same Augusti to Valerianus, prefect of the City.</p>
<p>If a woman of the stage asks for release in the name of religion, the indulgence is not to be refused her on the strength of her petition. But if afterwards, having wallowed in base embraces, she is found to have betrayed the religion she sought, and to be, in her heart, still an actress, doing what she had ceased to do by profession, she is to be dragged back to the stage and stay there without hope of any release <span class="cited-passage">until she is a ridiculous old woman, deformed by age</span>; and not even then shall she obtain release, when she can be nothing but chaste. Given on the eighth day before the Ides of May [8 May], at Aquileia, in the consulship of Syagrius and Eucherius [381].</p>
</div>
</div>
<p>The place of issue is printed “Aquila”; Gothofredus’s note (h) asks “Aquileia?”, and the rendering above follows him. In his commentary he explains that the law adds a condition to the earlier privilege: release lasted only “if the woman persevered in that religion”. Lecky’s “If the emancipated actress turned out badly, she was to be dragged back to the stage and kept there till she was ‘a ridiculous old woman’” is a fair summary; he leaves out the final clause.</p>
