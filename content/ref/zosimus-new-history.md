abstract: |
  <p>Lecky uses the pagan historian twice. For the end of the emperor’s pagan priesthood: Constantine and his successors down to Valentinian and Valens all accepted the title of <i>Pontifex Maximus</i>, but when the priests brought the sacred robe to <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gratian">Gratian</a>, “he, considering it a garment unlawful for a Christian to use, rejected their offer.” Their chief, Zosimus says, remarked: “If the emperor refuses to become Pontifex, we shall soon make one.” The remark is usually read as a pun (the translation does not show it): the usurper who soon overthrew and killed Gratian was named Maximus, and Zosimus tells the story just after Gratian’s death.</p>
  <p>And for the pagans’ freedom early in the reign of Theodosius: the townspeople, ruined by corrupt magistrates, had one resource left, “to intreat the gods to deliver them from such afflictions; for hitherto they were permitted to enter the temples, and to worship the gods in the manner of their country.”</p>

<section id="pontifex" class="passage">
<h2>Gratian refuses the robe of Pontifex Maximus (pp. 115–116)</h2>
<p class="passage-source">English: London 1814, Book IV, pp. 115–116, <a href="https://archive.org/details/TheHistoryOfCountZosimus/page/n114/mode/2up">scan n114</a>–n115. <span class="cited-passage">Highlighted</span>: what Lecky’s note 16 reports.</p>
<figure class="facsimile"><a href="../assets/img/ref/zosimus-1814-pp115-116.jpg"><img src="../assets/img/ref/zosimus-1814-pp115-116.jpg" alt="Printed text: “Upon the elevation of any one to the imperial dignity, the pontifices brought him the priestly habit, and he was immediately styled, Pontifex Maximus … But when the Pontifices, in the accustomed manner, brought the sacred robe to Gratian, he, considering it a garment unlawful for a Christian to use, rejected their offer. When the robe was restored to the priests who brought it, their chief is said to have made this observation, If the emperor refuses to become Pontifex, we shall soon make one.”" loading="lazy"></a><figcaption>Zosimus, <em>History</em>, English translation, London 1814, pp. 115–116. <span class="credit">Scan: Internet Archive (Google Books) · public domain</span></figcaption></figure>
<blockquote>
<p>… Upon the elevation of any one to the imperial dignity, the pontifices brought him the priestly habit, and he was immediately styled, <i>Pontifex Maximus</i>, or chief priest. All former emperors, indeed, appeared gratified with the distinction, and willingly adopted the title. Even Constantine himself, when he was emperor, accepted it, although he was seduced from the path of rectitude in regard to sacred affairs, and had embraced the Christian faith. In like manner did all who succeeded him to Valentinian and Valens. <span class="cited-passage">But when the Pontifices, in the accustomed manner, brought the sacred robe to Gratian, he, considering it a garment unlawful for a Christian to use, rejected their offer.</span> When the robe was restored to the [p. 116] priests who brought it, their chief is said to have made this observation, If the emperor refuses to become Pontifex, we shall soon make one.</p>
</blockquote>
<p>Lecky (vol. II, ch. IV, text at note 16): Constantine “still retained the old imperial title of Pontifex Maximus”; note 16: “The first emperor who refused it was Gratian (Zosimus, book iv.).”</p>
</section>

<p>… The inhabitants of the towns lived in misery through their own poverty and the iniquity of the magistrates; their only resource being to intreat the gods to deliver them from such afflictions; <span class="cited-passage">for hitherto they were permitted to enter the temples, and to worship the gods in the manner of their country.</span></p>
</blockquote>
<p>Lecky (vol. II, ch. IV, note 22): “the pagan Zosimus tells us expressly that in the beginning of the reign of Theodosius his coreligionists were still at liberty to worship in the temples.”</p>
