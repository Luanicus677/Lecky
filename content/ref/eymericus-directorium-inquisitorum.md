abstract: |
  <p>The three quotations are Eymericus’s own words (with the commentary by Peña around them), and Lecky’s Latin matches closely. One omission is important: the Barcelona anecdote in note 40, which Lecky ends with “verum si bene vel male, nescio”, goes on to say that the priest who was pulled from the fire and abjured was accused fourteen years later, found to have stayed in heresy and to have infected many, and was burned after all as a relapsed heretic. Lecky’s “edifying anecdote” is therefore less pointless than it seems: Eymericus is arguing that a sudden repentance under the flames can be insincere.</p>

<section id="barchinon" class="passage">
<h2>The priest in the flames (Part III, p. 516)</h2>
<p class="passage-source">Latin: p. 516, <a href="https://archive.org/details/10488917bsb/page/n551/mode/2up">scan</a>, left column. Peña’s margin note: “Hoc narrat Eymericus supra par. 2. q. xj.” <span class="cited-passage">Highlighted</span>: Lecky’s words.</p>
<div class="parallel">
<div class="original" lang="la">
<p>Et facto sic contingit in Cathalonia in civitate Barchinon, ubi fuerunt <span class="cited-passage">tres hæretici ut impœnitentes, sed non relapsi, traditi brachio sæculari; et cum unus eorum, qui erat sacerdos, fuisset igni expositus, et ex uno latere jam aliqualiter adustus, clamavit quod educeretur, quia volebat abjurare, et pœnitebat, et sic factum est: verum si bene vel male, nescio</span>: unum scio quod post quatuordecim annos accusatus, inventus est in hæresi permansisse continue, et multos infecisse, nec converti voluit, sed ut impœnitens et relapsus iterum est relictus brachio sæculari, et ignibus consumatus.</p>
</div>
<div class="rendering" lang="en">
<p>And in fact it so happened in Catalonia, in the city of Barcelona, where there were three heretics, as impenitent but not relapsed, handed over to the secular arm; and when one of them, who was a priest, had been exposed to the fire, and was already somewhat scorched on one side, <span class="cited-passage">he cried out that he should be taken out, because he wished to abjure, and he repented, and so it was done: whether well or ill, I do not know</span>: one thing I know, that after fourteen years he was accused, and found to have remained continually in heresy, and to have infected many, and he would not be converted, but as impenitent and relapsed he was again left to the secular arm and consumed by fire.</p>
</div>
</div>
<p>Lecky’s quotation opens “In Cathalonia, in civitate Barchinon, fuerunt tres hæretici”, dropping the print’s “Et facto sic contingit” and “ubi”; from “ut impœnitentes” to “nescio” it follows the print. He then stops, dropping the fourteen-year sequel. His remark that burning at the stake gave the victim more time for repentance is his own; Eymericus’s point is different, as above.</p>
</section>

<section id="tormenta" class="passage">
<h2>Continuing, not repeating, torture (Part III, p. 481)</h2>
<p class="passage-source">Latin: p. 481, right column, <a href="https://archive.org/details/10488917bsb/page/n516/mode/2up">scan</a>.</p>
<div class="parallel">
<div class="original" lang="la">
<p>… ponantur alia genera tormentorum coram eo, dicendo quod oportet eum transire per omnia, nisi prodat veritatem: quod si nec sic, <span class="cited-passage">poterit ad terrorem vel etiam ad veritatem, secunda dies, vel tertia assignari, ad continuandum tormenta non ad iterandum: quia iterari non debent, nisi novis supervenientibus indiciis contra eum; quia tunc possunt; sed continuari non prohibentur.</span></p>
</div>
<div class="rendering" lang="en">
<p>… other kinds of torments are to be set before him, saying that he must pass through them all unless he reveals the truth: and if not even so, <span class="cited-passage">a second or a third day may be assigned, for terror or even for the truth, to continue the torments, not to repeat them: for they ought not to be repeated unless new evidence emerges against him; for then they may be; but to be continued they are not forbidden.</span></p>
</div>
</div>
<p>Lecky’s Latin begins: “Si quaestionatus decenter noluerit fateri veritatem… poterit ad terrorem…”. The words “Si quaestionatus decenter noluerit fateri veritatem” are not at this place in the print (the preceding sentence runs “Quod si fateri noluerit, ponatur alia genera tormentorum coram eo”); the ellipsis in Lecky’s quotation covers the difference. Everything he quotes after the ellipsis is exact. I did not search for his opening words elsewhere in the book.</p>
</section>

<section id="reprobum" class="passage">
<h2>Sentence on the relapsed heretic (Part III, about p. 520)</h2>
<p class="passage-source">Latin: image <a href="https://archive.org/details/10488917bsb/page/n555/mode/2up">n555</a>, left column, in the sentence pronounced against a relapsed heretic.</p>
<div class="parallel">
<div class="original" lang="la">
<p>… sed <span class="cited-passage">tu in reprobum sensum datus, maligno spiritu ductus pariter &amp; seductus, præelegisti torqueri diris, &amp; perpetuis cruciatibus in infernum, &amp; hic temporalibus ignibus corporaliter consumari, quam adhærendo consilio saniori, ab erroribus damnabilibus ac pestiferis resilire</span>, &amp; ad gremium ac misericordiam sanctæ matris Ecclesiæ advolare. …</p>
</div>
<div class="rendering" lang="en">
<p>… but <span class="cited-passage">you, given over to a reprobate mind, led and misled together by the evil spirit, have chosen to be tormented by dire and perpetual torments in hell, and to be physically consumed here by temporal fires, rather than, adhering to sounder counsel, to draw back from damnable and pestilent errors</span>, and fly to the bosom and mercy of holy mother Church. …</p>
</div>
</div>
<p>Lecky’s note 42 gives this accurately (“Tu in reprobum sensum datus … resilire”, from “sed tu”), with a reference to p. 337 of his edition. Lecky calls it “part of the sentence pronounced upon the relapsed heretic”; the page shows it is in Eymericus’s form for a relapsed heretic, and I did not check this reading against the whole form.</p>
</section>
