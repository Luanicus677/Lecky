abstract: |
  <p>Lecky says that Locke “defended toleration against the theologians of Oxford”, and in his note: “His opponent was Archdeacon Proast, whose pamphlets were printed in the University.” The title pages bear him out. Proast’s first reply to Locke’s <em>Letter concerning Toleration</em> came out in 1690 “Printed at the Theatre”, the University press in the Sheldonian, whose engraving fills the middle of the page, and opens with the licence of the University: “Imprimatur, Jonathan Edwards, Vice-Can. Oxon. April 9. 1690.” The <em>Third Letter</em> of 1691 carries the same Vice-Chancellor’s imprimatur, “Univ. Oxon.”</p>
  <p>Neither title page names the author. In the copy filmed for the 1690 pamphlet a reader has written under the title: “By Jonas Proast M.A. &amp; Chaplain of All Souls College Oxon.” Lecky’s “Archdeacon” is the title Proast held later; when he received it is not checked here.</p>
