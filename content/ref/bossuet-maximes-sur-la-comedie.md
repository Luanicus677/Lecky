abstract: |
  <p>Bossuet sets out, in one breathless sentence, the Church’s whole treatment of actors in seventeenth-century France: no sacraments in life or at death unless they gave up the stage, no communion (the priest simply passed them by at the altar rail), no ordination, as “infamous persons”, and therefore no Christian burial. Gazier’s notes add that at Saint-Sulpice actors had to sign a written renunciation of their profession. Lecky quotes the passage to show how complete the ecclesiastical ban on the theatre was; his quotation is accurate.</p>

<section id="comediens" class="passage">
<h2>“La sépulture ecclésiastique leur est déniée” (§ XI)</h2>
<p class="passage-source">French: Gazier’s edition, p. 46, <a href="https://archive.org/details/maximesetrflexi00bossgoog/page/n68/mode/2up">scan</a>; § XI, “Si on a raison d’alléguer les lois en faveur de la comédie” (“Whether it is right to appeal to the laws in favour of the theatre”). The last word, “déniée”, is on the next page. <span class="cited-passage">Highlighted</span>: Lecky’s quotation.</p>
<figure class="facsimile"><a href="../assets/img/ref/bossuet-comedie-xi.jpg"><img src="../assets/img/ref/bossuet-comedie-xi.jpg" alt="A printed French page headed XI, with Bossuet’s sentence on the Church’s condemnation of actors" loading="lazy"></a><figcaption>Bossuet, <i>Maximes et réflexions sur la comédie</i>, § XI (Gazier’s edition, 1881, p. 46). <span class="credit">Scan: Internet Archive (Google Books) · public domain</span></figcaption></figure>
<div class="parallel">
<div class="original" lang="fr">
<p>L’auteur, pour ne rien omettre, appelle enfin les lois à son secours&#8239;; et, dit-il, si la comédie était si mauvaise, on ne la tolérerait pas, on ne la fréquenterait pas&#8239;: sans songer que Saint Thomas, dont il abuse, a décidé que les lois humaines ne sont pas tenues à réprimer tous les maux, mais seulement ceux qui attaquent directement la société. L’Église même, dit saint Augustin, <i>n’exerce la sévérité de ses censures que sur les pécheurs dont le nombre n’est pas grand</i>&#8239;: — <i lang="la">severitas exercenda est in peccata paucorum</i>&#8239;; c’est pourquoi <span class="cited-passage">elle condamne les comédiens, et croit par là défendre assez la comédie&#8239;: la décision en est précise dans les Rituels, la pratique en est constante&#8239;: on prive des sacrements, et à la vie et à la mort, ceux qui jouent la comédie, s’ils ne renoncent à leur art&#8239;; on les passe à la sainte table comme des pécheurs publics&#8239;: on les exclut des ordres sacrés comme des personnes infâmes&#8239;: par une suite infaillible, la sépulture ecclésiastique leur est déniée.</span></p>
</div>
<div class="rendering" lang="en">
<p>The author, so as to leave nothing out, at last calls the laws to his aid: and, he says, if the theatre were so bad, it would not be tolerated, it would not be frequented; without considering that St. Thomas, whom he misuses, decided that human laws are not bound to repress every evil, but only those that directly attack society. The Church herself, says St. Augustine, <i>exercises the severity of her censures only on sinners who are not many</i>: “severity is to be used against the sins of the few”; that is why <span class="cited-passage">she condemns the actors, and thinks that in this way she sufficiently forbids the theatre: the ruling is precise in the Rituals, the practice is constant: those who act in plays are deprived of the sacraments, both in life and at death, unless they renounce their art; they are passed over at the holy table as public sinners; they are excluded from holy orders as infamous persons; and, as an infallible consequence, Christian burial is denied them.</span></p>
</div>
</div>
<p>Gazier’s notes on the same page: “Rituel de Paris” (Bossuet’s source, which Lecky gives as “Rit. de Paris, pp. 108–114”); “In certain parishes, at Saint-Sulpice for example, a written renunciation was required of them”; and, on “on les passe à la sainte table”: “that is, they are passed by without being given communion.”</p>
</section>

<section id="eutrapelia" class="passage">
<h2>Bossuet admits St. Thomas went too far (§ XXXI)</h2>
<p class="passage-source">French: Gazier’s edition, p. 84, <a href="https://archive.org/details/maximesetrflexi00bossgoog/page/n106/mode/2up">scan</a>; § XXXI, “Réflexions sur la vertu qu’Aristote et Saint Thomas après lui ont appelée <i>eutrapelia</i>” (on the virtue of wit, or pleasant recreation). <span class="cited-passage">Highlighted</span>: Lecky’s quotation (ch. VI, note 114).</p>
<div class="parallel">
<div class="original" lang="fr">
<p><span class="cited-passage">Après avoir purgé la doctrine de Saint Thomas des excès dont on la chargeait,</span> à la fin <span class="cited-passage">il faut avouer, avec le respect qui est dû à un si grand homme, qu’il semble s’être un peu éloigné, je ne dirai pas des sentiments dans le fond, mais plutôt des expressions des anciens Pères sur le sujet des divertissements.</span> Cette discussion ne nous sera pas inutile, puisqu’elle nous fournira des principes pour juger des pièces comiques, et en général de tous les discours qui font rire.</p>
</div>
<div class="rendering" lang="en">
<p><span class="cited-passage">Having cleared the doctrine of St. Thomas of the excesses with which it was being loaded,</span> in the end <span class="cited-passage">it must be admitted, with the respect due to so great a man, that he seems to have departed a little, I will not say from the substance of the views, but rather from the expressions, of the ancient Fathers on the subject of amusements.</span> This discussion will not be useless to us, since it will give us principles for judging comic plays, and in general all talk that provokes laughter.</p>
</div>
</div>
<p>Aquinas had allowed that the actor’s trade is not sinful in itself, since people need recreation; the defenders of the theatre leaned on him. Bossuet’s concession is that Aquinas was more indulgent in his wording than the Fathers, who condemned laughter and the stage outright. Lecky omits “à la fin” (“in the end”).</p>
</section>
