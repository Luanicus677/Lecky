abstract: |
  <p>Lecky cites Berkeley as an early voice for replacing the death penalty with penal labour. The words are Berkeley’s first-edition query 58, but Lecky has made one silent change: Berkeley asks whether we may “contrive Employment for <em>them</em>”, meaning the criminals of the previous query, whom Ireland was sending “either to America, or to the other World”, presumably transportation or the gallows. In the revised edition of 1750 Berkeley went further and asked “whether all sturdy Beggars should not be seized and made Slaves to the Public, for a certain Term of Years”. The facing page has a query that still bites: “Whether … the fine Gentlemen, whose Employment is only to dress, drink, and play, be not a public Nuisance?”</p>

<section id="felons" class="passage">
<h2>“A more adequate Punishment for Felons, than even Death itself” (queries 55–60)</h2>
<p class="passage-source">English: Dublin 1735, p. 13, <a href="https://archive.org/details/bim_eighteenth-century_the-querist-containing-_berkeley-george_1735/page/n12/mode/2up">scan</a>. Long s modernised. <span class="cited-passage">Highlighted</span>: Lecky’s quotation.</p>
<figure class="facsimile"><a href="../assets/img/ref/berkeley-querist-1735-p13.jpg"><img src="../assets/img/ref/berkeley-querist-1735-p13.jpg" alt="A printed page headed “The Querist. 13”, with queries 56 to 60" loading="lazy"></a><figcaption>Berkeley, <i>The Querist</i>, Part I (Dublin, 1735), p. 13. <span class="credit">Scan: Internet Archive · public domain</span></figcaption></figure>
<blockquote>
<p>56. <i>Qu.</i> Whether small Gains be not the way to great Profit? And if our Tradesmen are Beggars, whether they may not thank themselves for it?</p>
<p>57. <i>Qu.</i> Whether some way might not be found for making Criminals useful in Public Works, instead of sending them either to America, or to the other World?</p>
<p>58. <i>Qu.</i> <span class="cited-passage">Whether we may not, as well as other Nations, contrive Employment for them? And whether Servitude, Chains and hard Labour, for a term of Years, would not be a more discouraging, as well as a more adequate Punishment for Felons, than even Death itself.</span></p>
<p>59. <i>Qu.</i> Whether there are not such Things in Holland as bettering Houses for bringing young Gentlemen to Order? And whether such an Institution might be useless among us?</p>
<p>60. <i>Qu.</i> Whether it be true, that the Poor in Holland have no Resource but their own Labour, and yet there are no Beggars in their Streets?</p>
</blockquote>
<p>And on the facing page (p. 14): “62. <i>Qu.</i> Whether, consequently, the fine Gentlemen, whose Employment is only to dress, drink, and play, be not a public Nuisance?”</p>
