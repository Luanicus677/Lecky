abstract: |
  <p>Cudworth was no credulous man: he wrote the most learned defence of reason and free will of his century. Yet in a chapter on the phenomena that “confute atheism”, he lists apparitions, witches and demoniacs as proofs that spirits exist, and says that those who now “explode” witches “can hardly escape the suspicion of having some hankering towards atheism”. Lecky quotes the sentence accurately except for a dropped phrase (“in a peculiar manner”). Cudworth’s very next sentence raises a question that troubled believers: why were there so many demoniacs in Christ’s time, “and hardly any, or none, in this present age of ours”?</p>

<h2>“Some hankering towards atheism” (Book I, ch. V)</h2>
<p class="passage-source">English: London 1743, vol. II, p. 702, <a href="https://archive.org/details/trueintellectual02cudw_0/page/n75/mode/2up">scan</a>. Long s modernised. <span class="cited-passage">Highlighted</span>: Lecky’s quotation.</p>
<figure class="facsimile"><a href="../assets/img/ref/cudworth-1743-p702.jpg"><img src="../assets/img/ref/cudworth-1743-p702.jpg" alt="A page of Cudworth with long s: “To this phaenomenon of apparitions might be added those two others of magicians or wizards, daemoniacks or Energumeni”" loading="lazy"></a><figcaption>Cudworth, <i>True Intellectual System</i>, 2nd ed. (1743), vol. II, p. 702. <span class="credit">Scan: Internet Archive · public domain</span></figcaption></figure>
<blockquote>
<p>To this phænomenon of apparitions might be added those two others of magicians or wizards, dæmoniacks or <i>Energumeni</i>; both of these proving also the real existence of spirits, and that they are not mere phancies, and imaginary inhabitants of men’s brains only, but real inhabitants of the world. As also, that among those spirits there are some foul, unclean, and wicked ones, (though not made such by God, but by their own apostacy) which is some confirmation of the truth of Christianity, the Scripture insisting so much upon these evil dæmons or devils, and declaring it to be one design of our Saviour Christ’s coming into the world, to oppose these confederate powers of the kingdom of darkness, and to rescue mankind from the thraldom and bondage thereof. <span class="cited-passage">As for wizards and magicians, persons who associate and confederate themselves</span> in a peculiar manner <span class="cited-passage">with these evil spirits, for the gratification of their own revenge, lust, ambition, and other passions; besides the Scriptures, there hath been so full an attestation given to them by persons unconcerned in all ages, that those our so confident exploders of them, in this present age, can hardly escape the suspicion of having some hankering towards atheism.</span> But as for the dæmoniacks and <i>Energumeni</i>, it hath been wondered, that there should be so many of them in our Saviour’s time, and hardly any, or none, in this present age of ours.</p>
</blockquote>
