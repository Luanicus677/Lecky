abstract: |
  <p>Lecky says that a lawyer named Huerne de la Motte ventured “in 1761” to denounce the refusal of burial to actors and to defend the actor’s profession, and that his work was burnt by the executioner and his name erased from the list of advocates. The decree confirms all of this: the book was torn up and burnt at the foot of the great staircase of the Palais, and Huerne was struck from the Tableau des Avocats. It is dated 22 April 1761, as Lecky says, and the burning is recorded the next day.</p>

<section id="arret" class="passage">
<h2>The decree’s operative words (p. 481)</h2>
<p class="passage-source">French: p. 481, <a href="https://archive.org/details/lettressurlesspe01desp/page/n522/mode/2up">scan</a>. Long s modernised. <span class="cited-passage">Highlighted</span>: what Lecky reports.</p>
<div class="parallel">
<div class="original" lang="fr">
<p>… LA COUR ordonne <span class="cited-passage">que le Livre en question sera lacéré &amp; brûlé par l’Exécuteur de la Haute-Justice, au pied du grand escalier du Palais</span> ; fait défenses à tous Imprimeurs, Libraires, Colporteurs ou autres, de l’imprimer, vendre, colporter ou autrement distribuer, à peine de punition exemplaire ; ordonne en outre <span class="cited-passage">que ledit François-Charles Huerne de la Mothe sera &amp; demeurera rayé du Tableau des Avocats</span>, étant au Greffe de la Cour, en date du 9 Mai dernier, comme aussi ordonne que le présent Arrêt sera imprimé, lu, publié &amp; affiché par-tout où besoin sera. … Fait en Parlement, le vingt-deux Avril mil sept cent soixante-un. <em>Signé</em>, Ysabeau.</p>
<p><em>Et le vingt-trois Avril audit an mil sept cent soixante-un, à la levée de la Cour, l’Écrit mentionné en l’Arrêt ci-dessus, a été lacéré &amp; brûlé dans la Cour du Palais</em>, &amp;c.</p>
</div>
<div class="rendering" lang="en">
<p>… THE COURT orders <span class="cited-passage">that the book in question shall be torn up and burned by the Executioner of the High Justice at the foot of the great staircase of the Palace</span>; forbids all printers, booksellers, hawkers or others to print, sell, hawk or otherwise distribute it, on pain of exemplary punishment; orders besides <span class="cited-passage">that the said François-Charles Huerne de la Mothe shall be and remain struck from the Roll of Advocates</span>, being at the Registry of the Court, dated the 9th of May last; and also orders that the present Decree shall be printed, read, published and posted wherever needed. … Done in Parliament, the twenty-second of April, one thousand seven hundred and sixty-one. Signed, Ysabeau.</p>
<p><em>And on the twenty-third of April of the said year 1761, at the rising of the Court, the writing mentioned in the above decree was torn up and burned in the Court of the Palace</em>, &amp;c.</p>
</div>
</div>
<p>Everything Lecky reports is in these lines. I did not read the beginning of the decree (pp. 473–480), which sets out the grounds, so I do not know how it characterised Huerne’s book.</p>
