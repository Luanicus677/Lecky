abstract: |
  <p>Lecky quotes Milton’s most famous image to show how the idea of truth as something still being discovered undermined persecution: Truth came perfect into the world with Christ, was hacked to pieces by deceivers as Typhon hacked Osiris, and her friends, like Isis, are still gathering up the limbs. Lecky modernises the spelling and stops at “her Master’s second coming”. Milton’s next sentence draws the moral the whole pamphlet is about: Christ will one day put the body back together, so “suffer not these licencing prohibitions to stand at every place of opportunity forbidding and disturbing them that continue seeking”.</p>

<section id="osiris" class="passage">
<h2>“Hewd her lovely form into a thousand peeces”</h2>
<p class="passage-source">English: Hales’s edition, p. 43, lines 3–23, <a href="https://archive.org/details/areopagitica00miltuoft/page/n94/mode/2up">scan</a>. Spelling of 1644. <span class="cited-passage">Highlighted</span>: Lecky’s quotation.</p>
<figure class="facsimile"><a href="../assets/img/ref/milton-areopagitica-p43.jpg"><img src="../assets/img/ref/milton-areopagitica-p43.jpg" alt="A printed page with numbered lines: “Truth indeed came once into the world with her divine Master …”" loading="lazy"></a><figcaption>Milton, <i>Areopagitica</i>, in Hales’s Clarendon Press edition, p. 43 (with a former reader’s pencil marks). <span class="credit">Scan: Internet Archive (University of Toronto) · public domain</span></figcaption></figure>
<blockquote>
<p>… that man by this very opinion declares that he is yet farre short of Truth.</p>
<p><span class="cited-passage">Truth indeed came once into the world with her divine Master, and was a perfect shape most glorious to look on; but when he ascended, and his Apostles after him were laid asleep, then strait arose a wicked race of deceivers, who, as that story goes of the <i>Ægyptian Typhon</i> with his conspirators how they dealt with the good <i>Osiris</i>, took the virgin Truth, hewd her lovely form into a thousand peeces, and scatter’d them to the four winds. From that time ever since, the sad friends of Truth, such as durst appear, imitating the carefull search that <i>Isis</i> made for the mangl’d body of <i>Osiris</i>, went up and down gathering up limb by limb still as they could find them. We have not yet found them all, Lords and Commons, nor ever shall doe, till her Masters second comming</span>; he shall bring together every joynt and member, and shall mould them into an immortall feature of loveliness and perfection. Suffer not these licencing prohibitions to stand at every place of opportunity forbidding and disturbing them that continue seeking, that continue to do our obsequies to the torn body of our martyr’d Saint.</p>
</blockquote>
<p>Lecky’s text is modernised (“straight”, “hewed”, “pieces”) and has “as the story goes” for Milton’s “as that story goes”.</p>
</section>
