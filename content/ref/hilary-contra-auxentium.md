abstract: |
  <p>Lecky names Hilary of Poitiers, writing during the Arian persecution, as one of the two most conspicuous early advocates of “absolute and complete toleration”, and cites his tract against Auxentius. The passage is a single savage paragraph. The apostles, Hilary says, converted the nations without any help from the palace, in prison, in chains, against the decrees of the senate and the edicts of kings. “But now, alas, earthly votes commend the divine faith … The Church frightens men with exile and prison, and forces belief in herself, she who was believed in through exile and prison. She hangs on the favour of those who communicate with her, she who was consecrated by the terror of her persecutors. She drives out priests, she who was spread by priests driven out. She boasts that she is loved by the world, she who could not have been Christ’s if the world had not hated her.”</p>
  <p>Hilary’s target is the Arian church under imperial protection, which had driven him and other Catholic bishops into exile.</p>

<div class="original" lang="la">
<p>[§ 3, OCR] … edictisque regis Paulus, cum in theatro spectaculum ipse esset, Christo Ecclesiam congregabat? Nerone se, credo, aut Vespasiano, aut Decio patrocinantibus tuebatur? …</p>
<p>4. <i>Fidei vis illata improbatur.</i> — At nunc, proh dolor! divinam fidem suffragia terrena commendant, inopsque virtutis suæ Christus, dum ambitio nomini suo conciliatur, arguitur. <span class="cited-passage">Terret exsiliis et carceribus Ecclesia: credique sibi cogit, quæ exsiliis et carceribus est credita. Pendet ad dignationem communicantium, quæ persequentium est consecrata terrore. Fugat sacerdotes, quæ fugatis est sacerdotibus propagata. Diligi se gloriatur a mundo, quæ Christi esse non potuit, nisi eam mundus odisset.</span></p>
</div>
<div class="rendering">
<p>[§ 3] … And was it by the king’s edicts that Paul, when he was himself made a spectacle in the theatre, gathered a Church for Christ? Was he protected, I suppose, by the patronage of Nero, or Vespasian, or Decius? …</p>
<p>4. <i>Force applied to faith is condemned.</i> — But now, alas, earthly votes commend the divine faith, and Christ is accused of lacking power of his own, while ambition is enlisted for his name. <span class="cited-passage">The Church frightens men with exile and prison, and forces belief in herself, she who was believed in through exile and prison. She hangs on the favour of those who communicate with her, she who was consecrated by the terror of her persecutors. She drives out priests, she who was spread by priests driven out. She boasts that she is loved by the world, she who could not have been Christ’s if the world had not hated her.</span></p>
<p class="editorial-note">Translation: this edition’s draft. The italic heading is the editor’s summary printed in Migne.</p>
