abstract: |
  <p>Lecky traces the doctrine of tyrannicide from the schoolmen to the French stage: Grévin, “one of the founders of the French Drama”, brought it on stage in a play on the death of Caesar, “reprinted with an anti-monarchical preface at the time of <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fran%C3%A7ois_Ravaillac">Ravaillac</a>”, Henri IV’s assassin. His source, Chasles, says the same in a few lines and quotes the verses. In Grévin’s play Brutus is not only the champion of liberty but, “by an excess that pleased the sixteenth century”, the apologist of the doctrine “that advises striking at monarchy by striking the monarch”. When people say Caesar was master of the empire, cries Brutus, let them add that Brutus killed him. The reprint bore a title that said it all: <i>La liberté vengée ou César poignardé</i>, “Liberty avenged, or Caesar stabbed”.</p>

<section id="cesar" class="passage">
<h2>Grévin’s <i>César</i> (p. 31)</h2>
<p class="passage-source">French: 1862, chapter on Grévin, p. 31, <a href="https://archive.org/details/lacomdieenfranc00chasgoog/page/n45/mode/2up">scan n45</a>. <span class="cited-passage">Highlighted</span>: what Lecky’s text and note draw on.</p>
<figure class="facsimile"><a href="../assets/img/ref/chasles-comedie-1862-p31.jpg"><img src="../assets/img/ref/chasles-comedie-1862-p31.jpg" alt="Printed page 31: “Dans César, Brutus personnifie l’idée républicaine … Et quand on parlera de César et de Rome, / Qu’on se souvienne aussi qu’il a été un homme, / Un Brute, le vengeur de toute cruauté … La tragédie de Grevin fut réimprimée au temps de Ravaillac, avec une préface hardiment hostile à la monarchie, et sous ce titre: La liberté vengée ou César poignardé.”" loading="lazy"></a><figcaption>Chasles, <em>La Comédie en France au seizième siècle</em>, 1862, p. 31. <span class="credit">Scan: Internet Archive (Google Books) · public domain</span></figcaption></figure>
<div class="parallel">
<div class="original" lang="fr">
<p>Dans <i>César</i>, Brutus personnifie l’idée républicaine, et non-seulement il est le champion de la liberté, mais encore, par un excès qui plaisait au <span class="sc">xvi</span><sup>e</sup> siècle, <span class="cited-passage">il est l’apologiste de la doctrine qui conseille de frapper la monarchie en frappant le monarque.</span> Au moment où il s’apprête à immoler César, il s’écrie, avec un accent de contagieuse éloquence:</p>
<p>Et quand on parlera de César et de Rome,<br>Qu’on se souvienne aussi qu’il a été un homme,<br>Un Brute, le vengeur de toute cruauté,<br>Qui aura d’un seul coup gagné la liberté.<br>Quand on dira: César fut maître de l’empire,<br>Qu’on dise quant et quant: Brute le fit occire!<br>Quand on dira: César fut le premier empereur,<br>Qu’on dise quant et quant: Brute en fut le vengeur!</p>
<p>Le même personnage, après avoir accompli le meurtre qu’il méditait, s’écrie:</p>
<p>Respire donc à l’aise, ô liberté romaine!</p>
<p><span class="cited-passage">La tragédie de Grevin fut réimprimée au temps de Ravaillac, avec une préface hardiment hostile à la monarchie, et sous ce titre: <i>La liberté vengée ou César poignardé.</i></span></p>
</div>
<div class="rendering">
<p>In <i>César</i>, Brutus personifies the republican idea, and he is not only the champion of liberty but also, by an excess that pleased the sixteenth century, <span class="cited-passage">the apologist of the doctrine that advises striking at monarchy by striking the monarch.</span> As he prepares to sacrifice Caesar he cries out, with an infectious eloquence:</p>
<p>And when men speak of Caesar and of Rome,<br>Let them remember too that there was a man,<br>A Brutus, the avenger of all cruelty,<br>Who with a single blow will have won liberty.<br>When men say: Caesar was master of the empire,<br>Let them say in the same breath: Brutus had him killed!<br>When men say: Caesar was the first emperor,<br>Let them say in the same breath: Brutus was his avenger!</p>
<p>The same character, once the murder he planned is done, cries:</p>
<p>Breathe freely, then, O Roman liberty!</p>
<p><span class="cited-passage">Grévin’s tragedy was reprinted in Ravaillac’s time, with a boldly anti-monarchical preface, under this title: <i>Liberty Avenged, or Caesar Stabbed.</i></span></p>
<p class="editorial-note">Translation: this edition’s draft. “Quant et quant”: at the same time.</p>
</div>
</div>
<p>Lecky (vol. II, ch. V, text at note 63): Grévin “brought it upon the stage in a play upon ‘The Death of Cæsar,’ which was first acted in 1560, and was reprinted with an anti-monarchical preface at the time of Ravaillac.” Chasles’s page supports the reprint and the preface. Lecky’s date of first performance is not on this page.</p>
</section>
