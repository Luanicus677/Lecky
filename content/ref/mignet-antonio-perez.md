abstract: |
  <p>To get Antonio Pérez out of the hands of the Aragonese courts, Philip II handed him to the Inquisition, and the Inquisition needed heresy. It found some in a remark Pérez had made in anger: that to clear himself he would spare nobody, and “if God the Father tried to stand in the way, I would cut off his nose”. The theologian who “qualified” the words judged them blasphemous and smelling of the heresy of those “who claim that God is corporeal and has human members”, and he headed off the obvious defence: Christ, being made man, has a body and a nose, but this was said of the First Person of the Trinity.</p>
  <p>Lecky’s note says the Inquisitors called the words heresy “of the Anthropomorphites and of the Vaudois”. The Spanish censure, printed in Mignet’s footnote, names one sect only, the “Vadiani”, defined as those who say God is corporeal; Mignet turned the name into “Vaudois” (Waldensians), who held nothing of the kind, and Lecky kept both the definition and Mignet’s name. Who the “Vadiani” were is not explained on the page; the definition fits the ancient anthropomorphite sect of the <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audianism">Audians</a> (our suggestion).</p>

<h2>“Je lui couperais le nez” (p. 234)</h2>
<p class="passage-source">French: 2nd ed. 1846, p. 234, <a href="https://archive.org/details/antonioperezetp00migngoog/page/n263/mode/2up">scan n263</a>, from the articles added to Pérez’s trial and their “qualification” by Fr. Diego de Chaves (so Mignet’s footnote, citing Llorente’s collection, vol. XIII). Mignet italicises Pérez’s words. <span class="cited-passage">Highlighted</span>: what Lecky’s note reports.</p>

<div class="parallel">
<div class="original" lang="fr">
<p>[Quelqu’un disant à Antonio Perez de ne point mal parler du prince don Juan d’Autriche, ledit Perez répondit: <i>Depuis que le roi m’a fait le reproche de travestir le sens des lettres que j’écrivais et de trahir le secret du conseil, je dois me justifier</i> (p. 234)] <i>sans ménagement pour personne: <span class="cited-passage">si Dieu le père voulait y mettre obstacle, je lui couperais le nez</span>, pour avoir permis que le roi se soit montré si peu loyal chevalier envers moi.</i> Q<span class="sc">ualification</span>. Cette proposition, en tant qu’elle dit que, si Dieu le père venait à la traverse, on lui couperait le nez, <span class="cited-passage">est une proposition blasphématoire, scandaleuse, offensant les oreilles pieuses et sentant l’hérésie des Vaudois, qui prétendent que Dieu est corporel et qu’il a des membres humains.</span> On ne peut l’excuser, en disant que le Christ a un corps et un nez puisqu’il s’est fait homme, car il est constant qu’il s’agit ici de la première personne de la très-sainte Trinité, qui est le Père.</p>
</div>
<div class="rendering">
<p>[When someone told Antonio Pérez not to speak ill of Prince Don John of Austria, Pérez answered: <i>Since the king has reproached me with twisting the sense of the letters I wrote and betraying the secrets of the council, I must clear myself</i>] <i>without sparing anyone: <span class="cited-passage">if God the Father tried to stand in the way, I would cut off his nose</span>, for having allowed the king to behave so unlike a loyal knight towards me.</i> Q<span class="sc">ualification</span>. This proposition, in so far as it says that if God the Father got in the way one would cut off his nose, <span class="cited-passage">is a proposition blasphemous, scandalous, offensive to pious ears and smelling of the heresy of the Vaudois, who claim that God is corporeal and has human members.</span> It cannot be excused by saying that Christ has a body and a nose since he was made man, for it is plain that what is meant here is the first person of the most holy Trinity, which is the Father.</p>
<p class="editorial-note">Translation: this edition’s draft. The bracketed opening is from p. 233, read in the OCR only.</p>
</div>

<p>The censure’s Latin core reads: “piarum aurium offensiva, et, ut jacet, est suspecta de heresi <b>Vadianorum</b>, dicentium Deum esse corporeum et habere membra humana” (offensive to pious ears and, as it stands, suspect of the heresy of the Vadiani, who say that God is corporeal and has human members). Lecky (vol. II, ch. V, note 8): the Inquisitors said the words “partook of the heresy of the Anthropomorphites and of the Vaudois, who maintain that the Father has bodily parts.” Lecky’s “Anthropomorphites” is a fair gloss of the Latin definition; his “Vaudois” is Mignet’s rendering of “Vadiani”. See also <a class="popup-ref" href="llorente-histoire-de-linquisition.html">Llorente</a>, whose history Mignet cites for the trial.</p>
