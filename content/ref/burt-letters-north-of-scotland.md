abstract: |
  <p>Lecky says the last Scottish execution for witchcraft is “generally said” to be of 1722, but that Captain Burt, “who visited the country in 1730, speaks of a woman who was burnt as late as 1727”, adding that he suspects Burt has misdated the 1722 case. Burt’s text bears out the first part: he says that in the beginning of 1727 a mother and daughter in Sutherland were condemned, and that the old woman was burnt in a pitch-barrel at Dornoch in the following June.</p><p>Burt gives the year plainly, and gives details (the daughter’s escape, the deputy sheriff’s court). Lecky’s suspicion that Burt confused 1722 with 1727 is Lecky’s own and cannot be checked here. The case is the one now usually called the last Scottish execution for witchcraft, but I did not check that claim against a modern source.</p>

<h2>Letter XII, p. 281: The Burning at Dornoch</h2>

<p class="passage-source">English: Burt, 1754, vol. I, p. 281 (<a href="https://archive.org/details/bim_eighteenth-century_letters-from-a-gentleman_burt-edward-author-of-_1754_1/page/n302/mode/2up">scan</a>). Long s printed as s. <span class="cited-passage">Highlighted</span>: the sentences Lecky relies on.</p>

<div class="parallel">
<div class="original" lang="en">
<p>But I shall now give you an Instance (in this Country) wherein the Judge was not so clear-sighted.</p>
<p><span class="cited-passage">In the Beginning of the Year 1727, two poor Highland Women (Mother and Daughter) in the Shire of <em>Sutherland</em>, were accused of <em>Witchcraft</em>, tried and condemned to be burnt.</span> This Proceeding was in a Court held by the Deputy Sheriff. The young one made her Escape out of Prison, but <span class="cited-passage">the old Woman suffered that cruel Death, in a Pitch-Barrel, in <em>June</em> following, at <em>Dornoch</em>, the Head Borough of that County.</span></p>
<p>In the Introduction to the Chapter under the Title of Witchcraft in <em>Nelson’s Justice</em>, which I have by me, there are these Words: “It seems plain that there are <em>Witches</em>, because Laws have been made to punish such Offenders, though few have been convicted of <em>Witchcraft</em>.” …</p>
