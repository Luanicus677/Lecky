abstract: |
  <p>Lecky summarises Plato’s theory: the soul that seeks its pleasure in the body will after death be imprisoned in a new body, punished, or “visible to the human eye, will appear upon earth in the form of a ghost to scare the survivors amid their pleasures.” In the <em>Phaedo</em> such a soul is “weighed down and dragged back into the visible world” by the bodily element it has absorbed, and “rolls about the monuments and the tombs”, where “shadowy phantoms of souls” have indeed been seen: they are visible because they still share in the visible. They wander there as a penalty for their bad former way of life, until desire for the body binds them into another one; the next lines send the gluttons into asses and the tyrants into wolves and hawks.</p>
  <p>Ghosts in Plato do not scare anyone. They haunt graves, not feasts; “to scare the survivors amid their pleasures” is Lecky’s own colouring.</p>

<h2>Shadowy phantoms about the tombs (81c–e)</h2>

<div class="original" lang="grc">
<p>Πάνυ γε. Ἐμβριθὲς δέ γε, ὦ φίλε, τοῦτο οἴεσθαι χρὴ εἶναι καὶ βαρὺ καὶ γεῶδες καὶ ὁρατόν· ὃ δὴ καὶ ἔχουσα ἡ τοιαύτη ψυχὴ βαρύνεταί τε καὶ ἕλκεται πάλιν εἰς τὸν ὁρατὸν τόπον, φόβῳ τοῦ ἀειδοῦς τε καὶ Ἅιδου, ὥσπερ λέγεται, <span class="cited-passage">περὶ τὰ μνήματά τε καὶ τοὺς τάφους κυλινδουμένη, περὶ ἃ δὴ καὶ ὤφθη ἄττα ψυχῶν σκιοειδῆ φαντάσματα, οἷα παρέχονται αἱ τοιαῦται ψυχαὶ εἴδωλα, αἱ μὴ καθαρῶς ἀπολυθεῖσαι ἀλλὰ τοῦ ὁρατοῦ μετέχουσαι, διὸ καὶ ὁρῶνται.</span> Εἰκός γε, ὦ Σώκρατες. Εἰκὸς μέντοι, ὦ Κέβης· καὶ οὔ τί γε τὰς τῶν ἀγαθῶν ταύτας εἶναι, ἀλλὰ τὰς τῶν φαύλων, <span class="cited-passage">αἳ περὶ τὰ τοιαῦτα ἀναγκάζονται πλανᾶσθαι δίκην τίνουσαι τῆς προτέρας τροφῆς κακῆς οὔσης· καὶ μέχρι γε τούτου πλανῶνται, ἕως ἂν τῇ τοῦ ξυνεπακολουθοῦντος τοῦ σωματοειδοῦς ἐπιθυμίᾳ πάλιν ἐνδεθῶσιν εἰς σῶμα.</span></p>
</div>
<div class="rendering">
<p>“Certainly.” “And this, my friend, we must suppose to be heavy and weighty and earthy and visible; and such a soul, having it, is weighed down and dragged back into the visible region, through fear of the invisible and of Hades, as they say, <span class="cited-passage">rolling about the monuments and the tombs, around which, indeed, certain shadowy phantoms of souls have been seen: such images as souls of this kind present, which have not been released in purity but share in the visible; and that is why they are seen.</span>” “That is likely, Socrates.” “Likely indeed, Cebes; and likely that these are not the souls of the good but of the bad, <span class="cited-passage">which are compelled to wander about such places, paying the penalty for their former way of life, which was evil; and they wander until, through the desire of the bodily element that follows them about, they are bound again into a body.</span>”</p>
<p class="editorial-note">Translation: this edition’s draft.</p>
</div>
</div>
<p>The text goes on at once (81e–82b, read in the OCR only) to say where such souls are bound: gluttons and drunkards into asses, the unjust and tyrannical into wolves, hawks and kites, and the merely respectable into bees, wasps and ants.</p>
