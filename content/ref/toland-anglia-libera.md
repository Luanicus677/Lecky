abstract: |
  <p>Lecky names Toland among the few freethinkers who wrote for political liberty, and quotes <i>Anglia Libera</i>: “the success of the Protestant religion, politically speaking, depends on the liberty of the several States of Europe”. The page matches, and the sentence goes on with Toland’s reason: where the Inquisition is established, or wherever government is arbitrary, it is next to impossible “to Preach any Doctrin but what sutes the Conscience, or Interest of the Prince”, and such princes sooner or later turn against Protestantism, “because the more Knowlege any People have the more they value Liberty”. On the next page he adds that “’tis only Error that needs the help of Force to propagat it”.</p>

<section id="liberty-of-states" class="passage">
<h2>Protestantism and the liberty of states (p. 185)</h2>
<p class="passage-source">English: London 1701, p. 185, <a href="https://archive.org/details/bim_eighteenth-century_anglia-libera_toland-john_1701/page/n190/mode/2up">scan n190</a>. Toland’s spelling (“Doctrin”, “sutes”, “Knowlege”, “propagat”) as printed; long s modernised. <span class="cited-passage">Highlighted</span>: Lecky’s quotation.</p>
<figure class="facsimile"><a href="../assets/img/ref/toland-anglia-libera-1701-p185.jpg"><img src="../assets/img/ref/toland-anglia-libera-1701-p185.jpg" alt="Printed page 185 of Anglia Libera, with show-through from the facing page: “… we may positively conclude that the Success of the Protestant Religion, Politically speaking, depends on the Liberty of the several States of Europe: for in those Contrys where the Inquisition is establisht, ’tis next to impossible for a Man to Preach any Doctrin but what sutes the Conscience, or Interest of the Prince …”" loading="lazy"></a><figcaption>Toland, <em>Anglia Libera</em>, London 1701, p. 185. <span class="credit">Scan: Internet Archive (ECCO) · public domain</span></figcaption></figure>
<blockquote>
<p>… ’Tis a Subject lately toucht in several Pieces. But from all of ’em, and our own Observation, we may positively conclude that <span class="cited-passage">the Success of the <i>Protestant</i> Religion, Politically speaking, depends on the Liberty of the several States of <i>Europe</i></span>: for in those Contrys where the <i>Inquisition</i> is establisht, ’tis next to impossible for a Man to Preach any Doctrin but what sutes the Conscience, or Interest of the Prince; and so it is wherever the Government is arbitrary, which makes all such Princes sooner or later grow out of conceit with the <i>Protestant</i> Religion, because the more Knowlege any People have the more they value Liberty, which makes ’em of Course the less dispos’d to [p. 186] be Slaves. …</p>
</blockquote>
<p>Lecky (vol. II, ch. V, note 104) quotes the highlighted words accurately, apart from modern capitals and spelling. His date “1702” is a year late for this printing, whose title page we have not viewed (1701 is the date in the catalogue).</p>
