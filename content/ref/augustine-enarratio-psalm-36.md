abstract: |
  <p>Lecky gives Augustine’s description of usury as the taking of “something more than you gave”: wheat, wine, oil or anything else. The Latin matches Migne with four small differences: “mutuam pecuniam dederis” for Migne’s “mutuam pecuniam tuam dederis”, “expectas” for “exspectes”, “sive vinum”, and the first words (“Si foeneraveris” for Migne’s “Si feneraveris”). The first two change the grammar, since “a quo … exspectes” is a subjunctive in a relative clause.</p>

<section id="foeneraveris" class="passage">
<h2>“Si feneraveris homini” (on Ps. 36:26)</h2>
<p class="passage-source">Latin: <a href="https://archive.org/details/bim_early-english-books-1641-1700_patrologiae-cursus-completus-_1845_36/page/n192/mode/2up">scan</a>. Augustine has just argued that God commands lending to him: “Fenera Deo”. <span class="cited-passage">Highlighted</span>: Lecky’s words.</p>
<div class="parallel">
<div class="original" lang="la">
<p>… Et dicitur tibi, Fenera Deo. Si feneras homini, habes spem; et si feneras Deo, spem non habebis? <span class="cited-passage">Si feneraveris homini, id est, mutuam pecuniam tuam dederis, a quo aliquid plus quam dedisti exspectes accipere: non pecuniam solam, sed aliquid plus quam dedisti, sive illud triticum sit, sive vinum, sive oleum, sive quodlibet aliud; si plus quam dedisti exspectas accipere, fenerator es, et in hoc improbandus, non laudandus.</span></p>
</div>
<div class="rendering" lang="en">
<p>… And it is said to you, Lend to God. If you lend to a man, you have hope; and if you lend to God, will you have no hope? <span class="cited-passage">If you have lent to a man, that is, have given him your money, from whom you expect to receive something more than you gave: not the money only, but something more than you gave, whether it be wheat, or wine, or oil, or anything else; if you expect to receive more than you gave, you are a usurer, and in this to be blamed, not praised.</span></p>
</div>

<p>The spelling (<em>foen-</em> for <em>fen-</em>) reflects the older editions that used the diphthong. “Expectas” for “exspectes” and the missing “tuam” may be the edition Lecky used (probably an older Benedictine text); I have not seen it. The meaning is the same. Lecky’s English “description given by St. Augustine of the sin” is a fair label.</p>
