abstract: |
  <p>Baxter was a moderate, and a sufferer for his conscience after 1660, yet in 1659 he could put the case against religious liberty as a neat syllogism: false religions lead to damnation; liberty to preach them makes converts to them; therefore liberty is the way to damnation. Lecky quotes it as the purest statement of the logic that made persecution seem a duty. It is the fifth of a numbered list of arguments in Baxter’s “Addition to the Preface” (Lecky’s “2nd Preface”), aimed at those who wanted the magistrate to leave religion alone; the others say that such a view would make magistrates mere guardians of “vile corporeal things”, and that the Ten Commandments were enforced by the penal laws of the Jews.</p>

<section id="liberty" class="passage">
<h2>“Therefore this Liberty is the way to mens damnation”</h2>
<p class="passage-source">English: London 1659, “An Addition to the Preface”, <a href="https://archive.org/details/holywealth00baxt/page/n50/mode/2up">scan</a>. Long s modernised. <span class="cited-passage">Highlighted</span>: Lecky’s quotation.</p>
<figure class="title-page"><a href="../assets/img/ref/baxter-holy-commonwealth-addition.jpg"><img src="../assets/img/ref/baxter-holy-commonwealth-addition.jpg" alt="A page headed “An Addition to the Preface”, with numbered paragraphs 3 to 5" loading="lazy"></a><figcaption>Baxter, <i>A Holy Commonwealth</i> (1659), “An Addition to the Preface”. <span class="credit">Scan: Internet Archive · public domain</span></figcaption></figure>
<blockquote>
<p>3. That is no good Cause that vilifieth the Magistrate, and teacheth the People so to do, and sets up the Ministers of the Gospel above him, more than a Prince in worldly splendor is above a Slave. But such is the Cause that I am now opposing. The matters of Gods Worship, of Faith and Religion, are more above the matters of this world, than that comes to. If Magistrates be once taken to be such terrestriall Creatures, as that their business is only about these vile corporeall things, their office will be esteemed of no better than the Object of it, or the work in which they are imployed.</p>
<p>4. The Decalogue was the Vitall part of the <i>Jews</i> Political Laws, and every Commandement of the first Table was seconded with a Penall Sanction: therefore these things belong to the Magistrate.</p>
<p>5. That is not the Good Cause that tendeth directly to the destruction of Faith and Piety, and the Everlasting damnation of mens souls: But such is this of Libertinism which we oppose: <span class="cited-passage">For, Popery, Mahometanism, Infidelity, and Heathenism, are the way to Damnation: But Liberty to preach up and to practice them, is the means to make men Papists, Mahometans, Infidels, and Heathens; therefore this Liberty is the way to mens damnation.</span></p>
<p>It’s well known by experience, how ready the multitude of ignorant, unsetled and proud people are to be led into any damning course, if they be dealt with by men of voluble tongues …</p>
</blockquote>
