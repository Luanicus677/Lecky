abstract: |
  <p>Lecky quotes “an Anglican clergyman” for the boast that no other religion had a hell described with such particularity as Christianity’s. He does not say what made the clergyman notable. Swinden’s book argues, at length and with astronomical calculations, that the sun is the material hell: its chapter VII opens, “Having conjectured that the Body of the Sun is the Tartarus or Local Hell, it will be expected that some Reasons be laid down to justify” it. The sun, he reasons, is large enough and hot enough to hold the damned of all ages. Lecky’s quotation is accurate apart from one word (“particularity” for “Particularities”).</p>

<h2>“Not with the same Particularities … in any other Religion” (p. 129)</h2>

<p class="passage-source">English: London 1727, p. 129, <a href="https://archive.org/details/natureofhell00swin/page/n156/mode/2up">scan</a>. <span class="cited-passage">Highlighted</span>: Lecky’s quotation.</p>
<figure class="facsimile"><a href="../assets/img/ref/swinden-1727-p129.jpg"><img src="../assets/img/ref/swinden-1727-p129.jpg" alt="The top of a page headed “Nature and Place of Hell. 129”" loading="lazy"></a><figcaption>Swinden, p. 129. <span class="credit">Scan: Internet Archive · public domain</span></figcaption></figure>
<blockquote>
<p>The Christians Heaven is clearly another thing, and of a quite different Nature from the Elyzian Fields of the ancient Heathen, and from the sensual <i>Paradise</i> of the present <i>Mahumetan</i>; <span class="cited-passage">and the <i>Hell</i> described in the Gospel is not with the same Particularities to be met with in any other Religion, that is or hath been in the whole World.</span> <i>Knowing therefore, on the one hand, these Terrors of the Lord, we persuade Men</i>; and having, on the other, these <i>Promises</i>, <i>we exhort them to cleanse themselves from all Filthiness both of Flesh and Spirit, perfecting Holiness in the Fear of the Lord.</i></p>
</blockquote>
