abstract: |
  <p>Lecky quotes Cyprian at length to show how early the Church read the Old Testament penal laws as applying to itself. The bishop cites Deuteronomy 17: whoever will not hear the priest or the judge “shall die”. Then, says Cyprian, the disobedient were killed with the sword, while circumcision was of the flesh; “now, since spiritual circumcision has begun among the faithful servants of God, the proud and contumacious are slain with the spiritual sword when they are cast out of the Church.”</p>
  <p>Lecky’s translation is close, but it drops three words: “spiritali gladio”, with the spiritual sword. Without them Cyprian seems to say simply that the contumacious “are killed”; with them, the killing is plainly excommunication. Lecky’s own argument in the note (that later writers such as Bellarmine reasoned from excommunication, as the greater penalty, to the right of inflicting death) does not depend on the omission, but a reader of the English alone would take Cyprian to be harsher than he is.</p>

<h2>“Spiritali gladio … necantur” (cols. 370–371)</h2>
<p class="passage-source">Latin: Migne, PL 4, Epistola LXII, cols. 370–371, <a href="https://archive.org/details/bim_early-english-books-1641-1700_patrologiae-cursus-completus-_1844_4/page/n186/mode/2up">scan n186</a>–<a href="https://archive.org/details/bim_early-english-books-1641-1700_patrologiae-cursus-completus-_1844_4/page/n187/mode/2up">n187</a>. <div class="parallel">
<div class="original" lang="la">
<p><span class="cited-passage">Nec putent sibi vitæ aut salutis constare rationem si episcopis et sacerdotibus obtemperare noluerint, cum in Deuteronomio Dominus Deus dicat: <i>Et homo quicumque fecerit in superbia, ut non exaudiat sacerdotem aut judicem quicumque fuerit in diebus illis, morietur homo ille, et omnis populus cum audierit timebit, et non agent impie etiam nunc</i> (Deut. XVII, 12, 13). Interfici Deus jussit sacerdotibus suis non obtemperantes et judicibus a se ad tempus constitutis non obaudientes. Et tunc quidem gladio occidebantur, quando adhuc et circumcisio carnalis manebat. Nunc autem, quia circumcisio spiritalis esse apud fideles servos Dei cœpit, spiritali gladio superbi et contumaces necantur dum de Ecclesia ejiciuntur. Neque enim vivere foris possunt, cum domus Dei una sit, et nemini salus esse nisi in Ecclesia possit.</span></p>
</div>
<div class="rendering">
<p><span class="cited-passage">Let them not think that the way of life or salvation stands open to them if they refuse to obey the bishops and priests, since the Lord God says in Deuteronomy: <i>And the man that will do presumptuously, and will not hearken unto the priest or the judge, whoever he be in those days, that man shall die; and all the people shall hear and fear, and do no more presumptuously</i> (Deut. 17:12–13). God commanded that those who did not obey his priests, and did not hearken to the judges he appointed for the time, should be put to death. And then, indeed, they were killed with the sword, while the circumcision of the flesh still remained. But now, since spiritual circumcision has begun among the faithful servants of God, the proud and contumacious are slain with the spiritual sword when they are cast out of the Church. For they cannot live outside it, since the house of God is one, and there can be salvation for no one except in the Church.</span></p>
<p class="editorial-note">Translation: this edition’s draft.</p>
