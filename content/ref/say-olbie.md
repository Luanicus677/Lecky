abstract: |
  <p>Lecky ends his chapter on the industrial spirit by contrasting the ascetic ideal with the utilitarian one, and gives Say’s sentence as the utilitarian position in its plainest form. It is note (A) to p. 2 of <em>Olbie</em>, a note on the sentence “the duties it prescribes to us can only be of two kinds”, that is, duties useful to others or to ourselves. A Talapoin was a Buddhist monk of Siam or Burma, a stock figure of eighteenth-century French writing; Say’s example of a useless virtue is the holy man who stands on one foot for years.</p>

<h2>“Pareille à cette perfection de Talapoin” (note A)</h2>
<p class="passage-source">French: Notes, note (A), <a href="https://archive.org/details/olbieouessaisurl00sayj/page/n98/mode/2up">scan</a>. <span class="cited-passage">Highlighted</span>: the words Lecky quotes.</p>
<figure class="facsimile"><a href="../assets/img/ref/say-olbie-note-a.jpg"><img src="../assets/img/ref/say-olbie-note-a.jpg" alt="A printed page headed “Notes. Note (A).” in spaced capitals" loading="lazy"></a><figcaption>Say, <i>Olbie</i> (Paris 1800), note (A). <span class="credit">Scan: Internet Archive · public domain</span></figcaption></figure>
<div class="parallel">
<div class="original" lang="fr">
<p><i>Les devoirs qu’elle nous prescrit ne peuvent être que de deux espèces.</i> Page 2.</p>
<p>Je ne conçois pas qu’il puisse exister des devoirs parfaitement inutiles pour les autres créatures ou pour nous-mêmes. <span class="cited-passage">Toute vertu qui n’a pas l’utilité pour objet immédiat, me paraît futile, ridicule, pareille à cette perfection de Talapoin, qui consiste à se tenir sur un seul pied plusieurs années de suite, ou dans quelque autre mortification nuisible à lui-même, inutile aux autres, et que son Dieu même doit regarder en pitié.</span></p>
</div>
<div class="rendering" lang="en">
<p><i>The duties it prescribes to us can only be of two kinds.</i> Page 2.</p>
<p>I cannot conceive that there can be duties perfectly useless to other creatures or to ourselves. <span class="cited-passage">Every virtue that does not have utility for its immediate object seems to me futile and ridiculous, like that perfection of the Talapoin which consists in standing on one foot for several years on end, or in some other mortification harmful to himself, useless to others, and which his God himself must regard with pity.</span></p>
