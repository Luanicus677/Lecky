abstract: |
  <p>Lecky’s note on Descartes ends with a story he calls even more preposterous: “Some good people in Sweden desired, it is said, to have his system of botany suppressed”, because Linnaeus classified plants by their sexes, which “was therefore calculated to inflame the minds of youth”. His source, the Italian statistician Gioja, names the good people’s audience. It was “represented to Queen Ulrika Eleonora, then on the throne and already advanced in years, that the system of Linnaeus, which is based on the sex of plants, was indecent and apt to corrupt the morals of the young”. The queen, “a stranger to the sciences and inclined to rigorism”, was about to obtain a decree against him from the king her husband, “had not, by a rare chance, powerful men defended the botanist at court”. Gioja tells it as an example of how ignorant governments, misled by charlatans, oppress the learned.</p>

<h2>Linnaeus and the queen (tome II, p. 389)</h2>

<div class="original" lang="it">
<p>8.º I governi ignoranti, principalmente se superstiziosi, incapaci di distinguere il ciarlatano dal dotto, opprimono il secondo per suggestione del primo che ha maggiore abilità nell’ingannare. Poco mancò che questa sventura non accadesse al celebre Linneo in Isvezia. <span class="cited-passage">Fu rappresentato alla regina Urlica Eleonora, allora assisa sul trono e già avanzata in età, che il sistema di Linneo, il quale ha per base il sesso delle piante, era indecente e proprio a guastare i costumi della gioventù.</span> La regina, straniera alle scienze e inclinata al rigorismo, <span class="cited-passage">andava ad ottenere dal re suo sposo un decreto contro Linneo</span>, se per raro accidente uomini potenti non avessero difeso alla corte il botanico.</p>
</div>
<div class="rendering" lang="en">
<p>8. Ignorant governments, especially superstitious ones, unable to tell the charlatan from the scholar, oppress the second at the prompting of the first, who is the cleverer at deceiving. This misfortune very nearly befell the celebrated Linnaeus in Sweden. <span class="cited-passage">It was represented to Queen Ulrika Eleonora, then on the throne and already advanced in years, that the system of Linnaeus, which is based on the sex of plants, was indecent and apt to corrupt the morals of the young.</span> The queen, a stranger to the sciences and inclined to rigorism, <span class="cited-passage">was about to obtain from the king her husband a decree against Linnaeus</span>, had not, by a rare chance, powerful men defended the botanist at court.</p>
</div>
</div>
<p>Lecky (vol. II, ch. IV, note 56): “Some good people in Sweden desired, it is said, to have his system of botany suppressed, because it was based upon the discovery of the sexes of the plants, and was therefore calculated to inflame the minds of youth. (Gioja, <i>Philosofia della Statistica</i>, tom. ii. p. 389.)” A fair summary, though Lecky leaves out the queen. Gioja’s own footnote gives his source: “Catteau, <i>Voyage en Allemagne et en Suède</i>, t. III, pag. 8” (not checked here), so Lecky’s “it is said” is a reasonable caution at two removes. “Philosofia” is Lecky’s spelling of the title, as printed.</p>
