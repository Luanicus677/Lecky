abstract: |
  <p>In note 83 Lecky says Buchanan “tersely puts it”: “Rex, lex loquens; lex, rex mutus.” The words are Buchanan’s, but the sentence is not his own invention and Lecky’s punctuation is not the print’s. Buchanan has his interlocutor say that, as “the greatest master of governing a commonwealth” put it, the king should be a speaking law and the law a silent king.</p>

<section id="lex-loquens" class="passage">
<h2>The king as speaking law (p. 16)</h2>
<p class="passage-source">Latin: p. 16, <a href="https://archive.org/details/bim_early-english-books-1475-1640_de-jure-regni-apud-scoto_buchanan-geo_1581/page/n15/mode/2up">scan</a>. The speaker is “M.” (Maitland), who is arguing with Buchanan’s other character. <span class="cited-passage">Highlighted</span>: Lecky’s words.</p>
<div class="parallel">
<div class="original" lang="la">
<p>… Regibus igitur cætera liberis hic unus imperii modus est præfinitus, ut suas actiones &amp; orationem ad legum præscripta conformarent: &amp; præmia pœnasque, maxima societatis continenda vincula, ex earum sanctionibus dividerent. Deniqe quemadmodum ait summus administrandæ reipub. Magister, ut <span class="cited-passage">rex esset lex loquens, lex Rex mutus.</span> …</p>
</div>
<div class="rendering" lang="en">
<p>… For kings, then, free in other respects, this one rule of government is laid down: that they should shape their actions and speech to the prescriptions of the laws, and should distribute rewards and punishments, the greatest bonds that hold a society together, from their sanctions. Finally, as the greatest master of administering a commonwealth says, that <span class="cited-passage">the king should be a speaking law, and the law a silent king.</span> …</p>
</div>
</div>

<p>Lecky drops the introductory “ut … esset” and the author’s attribution to “the greatest master of administering a commonwealth”; the 1581 print has no punctuation between “lex loquens” and “lex Rex mutus” that I could read. Lecky’s “tersely puts it” therefore slightly overstates Buchanan’s authorship: he is quoting a maxim, which he does not name in the words I read. I have not identified its source.</p>
