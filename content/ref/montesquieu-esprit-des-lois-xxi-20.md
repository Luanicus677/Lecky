abstract: |
  <p>Lecky says Montesquieu, speaking of the scholastic writings on usury, “says, with a little exaggeration”: “Ainsi nous devons aux spéculations des scholastiques tous les malheurs qui ont accompagné la destruction du commerce”. The sentence is the end of a chapter on how commerce emerged in the Middle Ages from the violence against the Jews; Montesquieu continues: “& à l’avarice des princes l’établissement d’une chose qui le met en quelque façon hors de leur pouvoir.” Lecky’s “t Ainsi” is a misread opening quotation mark; “speculations” and “accompagne” are the OCR of his note, which lacks the accents.</p>

<section id="xxi-20" class="passage">
<h2>“Nous devons aux spéculations des scholastiques tous les malheurs …”</h2>
<p class="passage-source">French: book XXI, chapter XX (1777 ed., French Wikisource). <span class="cited-passage">Highlighted</span>: Lecky’s quotation.</p>
<div class="parallel">
<div class="original" lang="fr">
<p>… Les Juifs, proscrits tour-à-tour de chaque pays, trouverent le moyen de sauver leurs effets. … Ils inventerent les lettres de change ; & par ce moyen, le commerce put éluder la violence & se maintenir par-tout … Les théologiens furent obligés de restreindre leurs principes ; & le commerce qu’on avoit violemment lié avec la mauvaise foi, rentra pour ainsi dire dans le sein de la probité. <span class="cited-passage">Ainsi nous devons aux spéculations des scholastiques tous les malheurs qui ont accompagné la destruction du commerce</span> ; & à l’avarice des princes l’établissement d’une chose qui le met en quelque façon hors de leur pouvoir.</p>
</div>
<div class="rendering" lang="en">
<p>… The Jews, proscribed in turn from every country, found the means of saving their effects. … They invented bills of exchange; and by this means commerce was able to elude violence and maintain itself everywhere … The theologians were obliged to restrict their principles; and commerce, which had been violently tied to bad faith, returned, so to speak, into the bosom of probity. <span class="cited-passage">Thus we owe to the speculations of the scholastics all the misfortunes that accompanied the destruction of commerce</span>; and to the avarice of princes the establishment of a thing which puts it in some manner beyond their power.</p>
</div>
</div>
<p>Lecky’s “with a little exaggeration” is his own comment; Montesquieu’s sentence is part of a passage about the Jews and the bill of exchange, which also bears on Lecky’s <a href="../rationalism/vol2-ch6.html#fn81">note 81</a> (the invention of the letter of exchange).</p>
</section>
