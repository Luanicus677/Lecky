abstract: |
  <p>Bodin’s book, a huge compilation of authorities and trial reports in French, was the standard reply to <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Weyer">Wier</a>’s attack on the witch trials. Lecky devotes pp. 95–99 of Chapter I to it as the sceptics’ chief opponent, and cites it for a handful of details and quotations.</p><p>The three passages here are the ones Lecky gives page numbers for and quotes in French or paraphrases closely. Lecky modernises Bodin’s spelling and condenses and rearranges his lists; where he adds to or departs from Bodin, the comparisons below say so.</p>

<section id="p15" class="passage">
<h2>Book I, p. 15: Baal-zebub and the Flies</h2>

<p class="passage-source">French: Paris, 1582, p. 15 (<a href="https://archive.org/details/per_witchcraft-in-europe-and-america_bodin-jean_1582_125/page/n52/mode/2up">scan</a>). Spelling as printed, except that long <em>s</em> is normalised and abbreviation marks are expanded. <span class="cited-passage">Highlighted</span>: the words Lecky quotes.</p>

<div class="parallel">
<div class="original" lang="fr">
<p>Or il est tout notoire, que les Amorrheans, &amp; autres peuples que Dieu extermina, s’exerçoient en telles sortes de Sorceleries, sacrifians aussi les hommes aux Diables, ausquels ils parloient, &amp; qu’ils adoroient, &amp; principalement le Soleil, l’appellant par excellence Bahal c’est à dire en Hebrieu, Seigneur, d’où est venu Bahalzebuf, qui veut dire Maistre mouche, <span class="cited-passage">par ce qu’il n’y auoit pas vne mouche en son temple, comme on dict qu’au Palais de Venise il n’y a pas vne seule mouche &amp; au palais de Tolede qu’il n’y en a qu’vne, qui n’est pas chose estrange, ou nouuelle: car nous lisons que les Cyrenaiques apres auoir sacrifié au Dieu Acaron Dieu des mouches, &amp; les Grecs à Iuppiter surnommé Myiodes, c’est à dire Mouchard, toutes les mouches s’enuoloient en vne nuee, comme nous lisons en Pausanias <em>in Arcadicis</em>, &amp; en Pline au liure xxix. chapitre vi.</span></p>
</div>
<div class="rendering" lang="en">
<p>Now it is well known that the Amorites and the other peoples whom God exterminated practised such kinds of sorcery, sacrificing men also to the Devils, with whom they spoke and whom they worshipped, chiefly the Sun, which they called by excellence Baal, that is to say in Hebrew, Lord; whence came Baalzebub, which means Master Fly, <span class="cited-passage">because there was not a fly in his temple, as it is said that in the Palace of Venice there is not a single fly, and in the palace of Toledo only one, which is no strange or new thing; for we read that the Cyrenaeans, after sacrificing to the god Acaron, god of flies, and the Greeks to Jupiter surnamed Myiodes, that is to say “Mouchard,” all the flies flew away in a cloud, as we read in Pausanias, in his Arcadica, and in Pliny, book xxix, chapter vi.</span></p>
</div>
</div>

<details class="collapse">
<summary>What is not traced</summary>
<p>Neither the Pausanias nor the Pliny reference has been checked against its source here; this edition gives Bodin’s references as he printed them. “Mouchard” in modern French means a spy or informer; what Bodin means by it here has not been checked, and it is left untranslated.</p>
</details>
</section>

<section id="p152" class="passage">
<h2>Book III, pp. 151–152: The Pardon of Trois-Échelles</h2>

<p class="passage-source">French: Paris, 1582, pp. 151–152 (<a href="https://archive.org/details/per_witchcraft-in-europe-and-america_bodin-jean_1582_125/page/n332/mode/2up">scan, pp. 151–152</a>), conventions as above. “[…]” marks the omission of a paragraph about <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaspard_de_Coligny">Coligny</a> and a sorcery case reported by Leovicer, which fills the rest of p. 151; the name “Trois-Echelles” is split across the page break. <span class="cited-passage">Highlighted</span>: the sentences Lecky quotes.</p>

<div class="parallel">
<div class="original" lang="fr">
<p>I’en metteray vn exemple aduenu en Poitou l’an 1571. Le Roy <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_IX_of_France">Charles 9.</a> apres disner commanda qu’on luy amenast Trois-Echelles, auquel il auoit donné sa grace pour accuser ses complices. Et confessa deuant le Roy, en presence de plusieurs grands Seigneurs, la façon du transport des Sorciers, des dances, des sacrifices faits à Satan, des paillardises auec les Diables en figure d’hommes &amp; de femmes: &amp; que chacun prenoit des poudres pour faire mourir hommes, bestes, &amp; fruicts. […]</p>
<p>[p. 152] Trois-Eschelles alors en raconta beaucoup de semblables: Et faut croire que si le Roy, qui estoit d’vne forte complexion &amp; robuste, eust faict brusler ce maistre Sorcier &amp; ses complices, il est à presumer que Dieu luy eust donné pour telles executions heureuse &amp; longue vie. <span class="cited-passage">Car la parole de Dieu est tres-certaine, que celuy qui fait eschapper l’homme digne de mort, verse sur luy mesme la peine d’autruy, comme le Prophete dist au Roy Achab, qu’il mourroit pour auoir donné grace à l’homme digne de mort. Or iamais n’auoit esté ouy qu’on donnast grace pour les Sorciers.</span> Vray est qu’on peut dire que c’estoit pour accuser les complices, qu’on luy donnoit grace, mais tous eschapperent.</p>
</div>
<div class="rendering" lang="en">
<p>I will give an example that happened in Poitou in the year 1571. King Charles IX, after dinner, ordered that Trois-Échelles be brought to him, to whom he had given his pardon so that he would accuse his accomplices. And he confessed before the King, in the presence of several great lords, the manner of the sorcerers’ transport, the dances, the sacrifices made to Satan, the lewdness with the Devils in the shape of men and women; and that each of them took powders to kill men, beasts, and crops. […]</p>
<p>[p. 152] Trois-Échelles then related many similar things. And it must be believed that if the King, who was of a strong and robust constitution, had burnt this master sorcerer and his accomplices, it is to be presumed that God would have given him a happy and long life for such executions. <span class="cited-passage">For the word of God is most certain, that he who lets a man worthy of death escape pours upon himself the penalty of another, as the Prophet said to King Ahab, that he would die for having given pardon to the man worthy of death. Now it had never been heard that pardon was given for sorcerers.</span> It is true one may say that it was in order to accuse his accomplices that he was pardoned; but they all escaped.</p>
</div>
</div>

<p>Lecky’s account of this story is in <a href="../rationalism/vol1-ch1.html#fn89">note 89</a> of Chapter I (the note reads simply “P. 152.”). Set against Bodin’s text:</p>

<section id="refutation-252" class="passage">
<h2>Réfutation des opinions de Jean Wier, near p. 252: The Laws and Experience of All Peoples</h2>

<p class="passage-source">French: Paris, 1582, the “Réfutation des opinions de Jean Wier” at the end of the book, on the page just before the one headed “DE IEAN VVIER. 252” (<a href="https://archive.org/details/per_witchcraft-in-europe-and-america_bodin-jean_1582_125/page/n545/mode/2up">scan</a>), conventions as above. The page number of this leaf is not legible in the scan; it is the page immediately before p. 252, and Lecky cites “p. 252”. The passage begins in the middle of a sentence that starts on the previous page. Bodin’s three marginal references are given after the text. <span class="cited-passage">Highlighted</span>: the clauses Lecky quotes.</p>

<div class="parallel">
<div class="original" lang="fr">
<p>[…] <span class="cited-passage">aux loix de Platon, des douze tables, des Iurisconsultes, des Empereurs, &amp; de tous les peuples &amp; legislateurs, Perses, Hebrieux, Grecs, Latins, Allemans, François, Italiens, Espagnols, Anglois, qui ont decreté peines capitales contre les Sorciers</span>, &amp; contre ceux qui les recelent, ou qui les font euader: S’il faut plustost s’arrester à VVier que à l’experience de tous les peuples, Roys, Princes, Legislateurs, Magistrats, Iurisconsultes, qui ont cogneu au doigt, &amp; à l’œil les impietez &amp; meschancetez execrables, dont les Sorciers sont chargez: s’il faut plustost s’arrester au disciple du plus grand Sorcier, qui fut onques de son aage, que <span class="cited-passage">aux Prophetes, Theologiens, Docteurs, Iuges &amp; Magistrats, qui ont descouuert la verité par mille &amp; mille presomptions violentes, accusations, tesmoignages, recolemens, confrontations, conuictions, recognoissances, repentances, &amp; confessions volontaires iusques à la mort.</span> Nous auons le iugement de Dieu<sup>6</sup>, qui a declaré qu’il auoit arraché de la terre les peuples de la Palestine, pour les horribles Sorcelleries dont ils vsoient, &amp; non pour autre chose, &amp; a menassé d’exterminer non seulement les Sorciers, ains aussi tous ceux qui les souffriront viure,<sup>7</sup> &amp; qui a dit à Hieremie qu’il preschast haut &amp; clair qu’il raseroit<sup>8</sup> à feu &amp; à sang la ville de Hierusalem, &amp; tous les habitans pour les execrables Sorcelleries du Roy Manassés. Voila ce qu’il m’a semblé, qu’on peut respondre aux liures de VVier […]</p>
<p class="source-notes">(6) Deut. c. 18.<br>(7) Leuit. c. 20.<br>(8) Hier. cap. 15.</p>
</div>
<div class="rendering" lang="en">
<p>[…] <span class="cited-passage">to the laws of Plato, the Twelve Tables, the jurisconsults, the Emperors, and all peoples and lawgivers, Persians, Hebrews, Greeks, Latins, Germans, French, Italians, Spaniards, English, who have decreed capital penalties against sorcerers</span>, and against those who harbour them or let them escape: whether one must rather stand by Wier than by the experience of all peoples, Kings, Princes, Lawgivers, Magistrates, Jurisconsults, who have known with the finger and with the eye the execrable impieties and wickednesses with which sorcerers are charged; whether one must rather stand by the disciple of the greatest sorcerer of his age than by <span class="cited-passage">the Prophets, Theologians, Doctors, Judges, and Magistrates, who have discovered the truth by thousands upon thousands of violent presumptions, accusations, testimonies, <em>récolements</em>, confrontations, convictions, acknowledgements, repentances, and voluntary confessions up to death.</span> We have the judgement of God<sup>6</sup>, who declared that he had torn the peoples of Palestine from the earth for the horrible sorceries they practised, and for nothing else, and threatened to exterminate not only the sorcerers but all those who suffer them to live;<sup>7</sup> and who told Jeremiah to preach loud and clear that he would raze with fire and blood the city of Jerusalem and all its inhabitants for the execrable sorceries of King Manasseh.<sup>8</sup> That is what seemed to me could be answered to the books of Wier […]</p>
<p class="source-notes">(6) Deuteronomy 18.<br>(7) Leviticus 20.<br>(8) Jeremiah 15.</p>
</div>
</div>

<details class="collapse">
<summary>Notes on the translation</summary>
<p><em>Récolement</em> is a term of French criminal procedure: the reading back of a witness’s deposition to the witness for confirmation, before the confrontation. The gloss is this edition’s own. The “disciple of the greatest sorcerer of his age” is <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinrich_Cornelius_Agrippa">Agrippa</a>, whom Wier served; Lecky identifies him in <a href="../rationalism/vol1-ch1.html#fn87">note 87</a>.</p>
</details>

<p>Lecky quotes this passage in English on his p. 101 (<a href="../rationalism/vol1-ch1.html#fn91">note 91</a>: “Bodin, p. 252”), as something Montaigne “was, no doubt, perfectly aware” of. Set against Bodin’s text:</p>

<h2>Lecky’s Quotation of Book I, p. 15, Compared</h2>

<p>Lecky quotes the first passage in French in <a href="../rationalism/vol1-ch1.html#fn74">note 74</a> of Chapter I. His text differs from Bodin’s only in spelling and in one reference:</p>

<p>Lecky modernises Bodin’s spelling (u/v, accents) and gives the Pliny chapter in Arabic numerals. The French words are otherwise the same, and the quotation runs to the same place. He introduces it with his own words, “Beelzebub, as is well known, was god of flies”, where Bodin says “Bahalzebuf, qui veut dire Maistre mouche”.</p>
